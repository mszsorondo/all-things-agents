IDEAL Process Model

1. Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: provide a standardized, efficient patient clinical pathway from registration to discharge that ensures consistent assessment, reduces diagnostic and treatment rework, shortens case duration, and improves outcome traceability.
- High-level improvement goals:
  - Standardize assessment routing after registration.
  - Ensure every treatment outcome is explicitly recorded (Treatment successful or Treatment unsuccessful).
  - Eliminate repeated unmanaged rework loops by enforcing escalation to expert assessment before a re-treatment attempt.
  - Improve predictability of resource use and reduce time-to-discharge.

2. Key stakeholders and responsibilities (inferred from activities)
- Registration points: Register at ER, Register at FD — responsible for case intake and initial triage triggers.
- Clinical assessors: Examination, Expert Examination, Thorough Examination — responsible for clinical assessment and diagnostic input.
- Diagnostic decision-maker: Diagnosis — responsible for establishing the working diagnosis and ordering treatment plan.
- Treatment team: Treatment, Treatment successful, Treatment unsuccessful — responsible for administering therapy and recording outcomes.
- Discharge processor: Discharge — responsible for formal case closure.
(Note: these are mappings of activities to the natural accountable groups implied by the AS-IS activities; specific person-role titles/organizational units should be assigned locally.)

3. Start and end conditions (formalized)
- Start: Case begins when patient is recorded at either:
  - Register at ER OR Register at FD.
  - Immediately at registration an initial triage decision must be recorded (see Section 4).
- End: Case is completed only when Discharge is recorded. Discharge must be preceded by an explicit Treatment successful event except in formally approved exception cases (see Section 6).

4. Front-door triage and standardized assessment routing (new governance)
- At registration, a mandatory triage decision is performed and recorded as a single routing choice (this reuses no new activities — it simply enforces a documented decision immediately after Register at ER/FD). Based on triage, exactly one of the following assessment paths is selected:
  A. Standard assessment path (most cases, low-to-moderate complexity)
     - Examination → (if indicated by examination) Thorough Examination → Diagnosis
  B. Specialist-expert path (complex or uncertain cases)
     - Expert Examination → (if indicated by expert) Thorough Examination → Diagnosis
  C. Direct Thorough Examination path (selected when triage signals need for detailed diagnostic study immediately)
     - Thorough Examination → Diagnosis
  D. Restricted direct-to-Diagnosis path (only for simple, protocol-driven cases)
     - Diagnosis (bypassing examination steps), permitted under clearly defined triage criteria (e.g., protocol-validated minor conditions).
- Governance rules:
  - Only one assessment path is chosen at triage. Examination and Expert Examination are not done in sequence in the same case unless specifically escalated later.
  - Thorough Examination is performed only when either Examination or Expert Examination indicates need, or triage selected direct Thorough Examination.

5. Optimized sequence from Diagnosis to Treatment (strict sequencing and recording)
- After Diagnosis, the Treatment activity is executed.
- Outcomes from Treatment must be explicitly recorded as one of:
  - Treatment successful (preferred normal closure)
  - Treatment unsuccessful
- Discharge must follow Treatment successful. Direct Treatment → Discharge without a recorded Treatment successful is allowed only under tightly controlled, documented exceptions (e.g., administrative closure) and must be logged as an exception event to avoid ambiguity.

6. Streamlined exception handling and controlled rework
- Rework policy (enforced):
  - If Treatment unsuccessful occurs, the case must be escalated to the Expert Examination path before another Treatment attempt is scheduled. The required sequence on first unsuccessful outcome:
    Treatment unsuccessful → Expert Examination → Thorough Examination (if indicated) → Diagnosis → Treatment (single retry).
  - The system permits at most one controlled re-treatment cycle under normal governance (i.e., one Treatment unsuccessful → re-assessment → re-treatment). This reduces repeated uncontrolled cycles seen in AS-IS.
  - If the re-treatment after Expert re-evaluation is again unsuccessful (i.e., second Treatment unsuccessful), the case is flagged for mandated case-review (outside the modeled activities) and an approved alternative care plan must be decided before further treatment attempts. Operationally, this means no further automatic loops back to Diagnosis/Treatment without documented review and explicit authorization.
- Rationale: Using Expert Examination as the required escalation step consolidates specialist input early, reducing repeated re-diagnosis attempts by generalists and lowering the probability of multiple treatment failures.

7. Decision points with explicit criteria and outcomes
- Triage decision at registration:
  - Criteria examples (to be formally defined by clinical governance): severity, stability, presenting complaint complexity, prior history, protocol match.
  - Outcomes:
    - Route to Examination (standard)
    - Route to Expert Examination (complex/unstable)
    - Route to Thorough Examination (direct advanced assessment)
    - Route to Diagnosis (protocol-based direct)
- Post-assessment decision (after Examination / Expert / Thorough):
  - If assessment yields sufficient information → proceed to Diagnosis.
  - If more information is required → conduct Thorough Examination (if not already done) before Diagnosis.
- Post-treatment decision:
  - If Treatment successful → proceed to Discharge.
  - If Treatment unsuccessful → escalate to Expert Examination and follow rework policy (one controlled retry only).

8. Parallel activities and synchronization
- Maintain sequential, single-threaded clinical flow for assessments and treatments to ensure clear accountability and avoid duplicate assessments.
- Optional: where laboratory or imaging results (not explicitly part of the AS-IS activities) are retrieved asynchronously, the model enforces synchronization at the Diagnosis step — diagnosis is only finalized once required assessment results are available. (This uses existing activities: Thorough Examination can represent the state where all required test results and detailed assessments are consolidated prior to Diagnosis.)

9. Explicit logging and data quality enforcement
- Mandatory logging points:
  - Triage decision (at registration)
  - Assessment chosen and whether Thorough Examination was performed
  - Diagnosis timestamp and summary code
  - Treatment start and finish timestamps
  - Treatment outcome: Treatment successful or Treatment unsuccessful (mandatory)
  - Any exception allowing direct Treatment → Discharge must be explicitly logged with justification
- Expected effect: better traceability and elimination of ambiguous traces (e.g., Treatment → Discharge without success marker).

10. End conditions and allowable outcomes
- Normal end: Treatment successful → Discharge.
- Controlled exception end: Treatment → Discharge only with documented exception justification.
- Cases with one re-treatment that becomes successful: follow normal end.
- Cases with second Treatment unsuccessful: require case-review and an approved plan before further activities (this prevents unlimited uncontrolled loops).

11. Expected process metrics and performance improvements (targets)
- Reduction in rework loops:
  - Target: reduce frequency of Treatment → Treatment unsuccessful transitions by 60–80% relative to AS-IS (from the observed 1,746 transitions down to a fraction), by enforcing expert escalation prior to re-treatment.
- Case duration:
  - Target: reduce average case duration for typical (no-rework) paths by 15–30% through standardized routing and fewer redundant assessments.
  - Cases with one controlled re-treatment expected to have moderate increase in duration but significantly less than AS-IS repeated cycles.
- Treatment success traceability:
  - Target: 95–100% of discharges associated with an explicit Treatment successful event (versus significant inconsistency in AS-IS).
- Predictability and variability:
  - Target: narrower variance in pathways — majority of cases should follow the standardized assessment → diagnosis → treatment → successful discharge pattern.
- Resource utilization:
  - Target: reduce diagnostic/treatment repeat resource load (physician/reassessment time) by enforcing expert review on first failure, improving overall throughput.

12. Key improvements made compared to the AS-IS model
- Standardized assessment routing:
  - Eliminates ad-hoc mixes of Examination/Expert/Thorough steps by enforcing triage-based routing.
- Forced outcome recording:
  - Removes ambiguity by requiring explicit Treatment successful or Treatment unsuccessful logging before Discharge.
- Controlled and limited rework:
  - Rework is not eliminated but is controlled — one mandated expert-backed retry replaces unlimited uncontrolled loops, substantially reducing repeat cycles.
- Escalation protocol:
  - Unsuccessful treatments must be escalated to Expert Examination (an existing activity) prior to re-diagnosis, improving diagnostic quality before re-treatment.
- Clear exception governance:
  - Direct Treatment → Discharge remains possible only with explicit documented justification, improving data quality.
- Improved predictability and performance:
  - Fewer repeated diagnosis/treatment cycles will shorten average case length and free clinician capacity.

13. Example canonical IDEAL execution path (allowed traces)
- Typical path (targeted majority):
  1. Register at ER | Register at FD
  2. Triage decision → Examination
  3. Examination → (if indicated) Thorough Examination
  4. Diagnosis
  5. Treatment
  6. Treatment successful → Discharge
- Specialist-escalted path (complex cases):
  1. Register at ER | Register at FD
  2. Triage decision → Expert Examination
  3. Expert Examination → (if indicated) Thorough Examination
  4. Diagnosis
  5. Treatment
  6. Treatment successful → Discharge
- Controlled rework path (single retry):
  1. Register at ER | Register at FD
  2. Triage decision → Examination or Expert Examination
  3. Diagnosis
  4. Treatment
  5. Treatment unsuccessful → Expert Examination → Thorough Examination (if indicated) → Diagnosis → Treatment
  6. If Treatment successful → Discharge; if Treatment unsuccessful again → case-review and authorized next steps (no automatic loop).

14. Implementation considerations
- Update registration systems to capture triage routing and to enforce required fields.
- Instrument electronic records / workflow engine to block a second automatic Treatment retry unless expert review is recorded.
- Train staff on the triage rules, escalation to Expert Examination, and mandatory recording of treatment outcomes.
- Monitor compliance and performance against the metrics targets; iterate triage thresholds and escalation triggers based on measured outcomes.

End of IDEAL Process Model.
