AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- This process captures the observed lifecycle from a candidate applying to view a rental apartment through screening, contracting, move-in and subsequent rent-payment behavior — including rejection of applicants, extensive screening, tenant cancellations, missed payments, warnings and eviction.
- Purpose (observed): manage applicant intake, evaluate eligibility, convert eligible applicants to tenants (sign contract and move in), and manage ongoing rent collection and enforcement (payments, late payments, warnings, eviction, and voluntary tenant cancellation).

Key stakeholders and roles
- No explicit roles / departments are specified in the source models. The description therefore uses activity names as observed. Where the source implies handoffs (e.g., screening, issuing warnings) these are described as distinct activities but no actor names are invented.

Start and end events (inferred)
- Start event: an applicant submits an "Apply for Viewing Appointment" (this is the observed process entry point in the mined models).
- Observed end states (traces terminate in one of these observed outcomes):
  - Reject Prospective Tenant (applicant rejected / not admitted)
  - Tenant Cancels Apartment (tenant withdraws / cancels their tenancy; observed as a terminal outcome in many traces)
  - Evict Tenant (formal eviction; observed but rare)
  - Ongoing tenancy / continuing payments — traces may also effectively stop in the logs while the tenant is still active (i.e., many traces end after payment events without an explicit terminal cancellation event). In other words, a trace may end with one or more Pay Rent events without reaching a terminal Reject, Cancel or Evict event.

Detailed step-by-step process flow, decision points, loops and all observed variants

1) Initial intake (start)
- Observed start: Apply for Viewing Appointment.
  - In the most common observed path, Apply for Viewing Appointment is directly followed by Set Appointment (Apply -> Set Appointment frequency: 9,151).
  - Alternative/short-cut observed: Apply for Viewing Appointment can lead directly to Reject Prospective Tenant without intermediate steps (Apply -> Reject observed with frequency: 849). This means some applicants are rejected immediately (no appointment or viewing recorded).

2) Appointment and viewing (optional depending on variant)
- If Set Appointment occurs, the next observed activity is View The Property (Set Appointment -> View The Property frequency: 9,151).
- After viewing, two observed outcomes exist:
  - The candidate proceeds to Hand In The Paperwork (View The Property -> Hand In The Paperwork frequency: 7,441).
  - Or the candidate is rejected immediately after viewing (View The Property -> Reject Prospective Tenant frequency: 1,710). This is an observed alternative branch: screening may not always proceed; viewing can directly result in rejection.

3) Paperwork and screening (when paperwork is handed in)
- When Hand In The Paperwork occurs, it is followed by Check Paperwork (Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork frequency: 7,441).
- Check Paperwork is followed by Screen Prospective Tenant (Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant frequency: 7,441).
- Screen Prospective Tenant branches into three observed alternatives:
  A) Reject Prospective Tenant (direct rejection after screening). Frequency observed: 2,856.
  B) Extensive Screening (additional checks). Frequency observed: 2,793.
  C) Sign Contract (screening passed). Frequency observed: 1,792.

- If Extensive Screening occurs, it has two observed outcomes:
  - It can lead to Reject Prospective Tenant (Extensive Screening -> Reject Prospective Tenant frequency: 1,815).
  - Or it can later lead to Sign Contract (Extensive Screening -> Sign Contract frequency: 978).

Decision point summary (applicant evaluation)
- Decision after screening:
  - Outcome = Reject Prospective Tenant (applicant exits with rejection).
  - Outcome = Require Extensive Screening (may convert later to rejection or to sign contract).
  - Outcome = Sign Contract (move to contracting).

4) Contracting and move-in
- Sign Contract -> Move In (frequency observed: 2,770).
- Move In has observed immediate next steps:
  - Primarily: Move In -> Pay Rent (Move In -> Pay Rent frequency: 2,656). That is, after moving in the core observed behavior is to start paying rent.
  - Less commonly: Move In -> Miss Rent Payment (Move In -> Miss Rent Payment frequency: 114) — some tenants miss an initial rent payment following move in.

5) Ongoing rent/payment behavior (core loop and variations)
- Pay Rent is a repeatable (looping) activity. Observed behavior:
  - Pay Rent -> Pay Rent self-follow (a tenant paying multiple rent cycles) is highly frequent (Pay Rent -> Pay Rent frequency: 14,819). The event logs show many variants with multiple sequential Pay Rent events.
  - After each Pay Rent there are several possible observed transitions:
    - Tenant Cancels Apartment may occur after one or more Pay Rent events (Pay Rent -> Tenant Cancels Apartment frequency: 2,680). Many variants end with a Tenant Cancels Apartment after a sequence of Pay Rent(s).
    - A Miss Rent Payment can occur following Pay Rent (Pay Rent -> Miss Rent Payment frequency: 770). That reflects tenants who pay some cycles but later miss one.
- The logs exhibit many variants where Pay Rent repeats N times (N varies across traces) before Tenant Cancels Apartment. The mining results explicitly list dozens of variants with the same prefix through Move In followed by varying numbers of Pay Rent events and then Tenant Cancels Apartment. Observed N spans from 1 up to many repeated pays (examples in the variant list show repeats in double-, triple-, and many-pay sequences). The model must therefore allow an arbitrary number of Pay Rent occurrences (0..N, where N can be large).

6) Missed payment → enforcement / remediation paths
- Observed enforcement/remediation sequence when a rent is missed:
  - Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning (frequency observed: 945).
  - Issue Warning then has two observed outcomes:
    - Issue Warning -> Accept Late Payment (frequency observed: 855). That is, tenant responds and pays late; Accept Late Payment is a path that resolves the warning.
    - Issue Warning -> Evict Tenant (frequency observed: 90). Eviction is rare but observed following an issued warning.
  - Accept Late Payment then has two observed outcomes (both observed):
    - Accept Late Payment -> Pay Rent (frequency observed: 794). After accepting a late payment the tenant returns to the Pay Rent loop (normal payments resume).
    - Accept Late Payment -> Miss Rent Payment (frequency observed: 61). After accepting a late payment the tenant may still later miss another payment (re-entry to the Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning -> ... cycle). This establishes a possible repeated rework loop of missed payments, warnings, accept late, back to pay, and again miss.
- Move In -> Miss Rent Payment was observed directly in a few traces (114): a tenant can miss payment immediately after move-in, entering the warning/enforcement loop without prior Pay Rent events.

7) Termination behaviors (end conditions)
- Reject Prospective Tenant: many traces end here (three observed patterns: after Apply, after View, after Screening/Extensive Screening).
- Tenant Cancels Apartment: many tenant traces end with an explicit cancellation event after one or more Pay Rent(s). This is an observed common terminal outcome.
- Evict Tenant: rarely observed terminal outcome after missed payments and warnings.
- Ongoing tenancy: traces that end with Pay Rent (or repeated Pay Rent events) without a terminal cancellation or eviction are observed — interpreted as the tenant still active at log end.

Explicit list of all observed valid sequences (representative / canonical variants, all observed alternatives included)
- Short rejection variants (observed):
  1) Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Reject Prospective Tenant (freq: 849)
  2) Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Set Appointment -> View The Property -> Reject Prospective Tenant (freq: 1,710)

- Screening rejection variants (observed):
  3) Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Set Appointment -> View The Property -> Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant -> Reject Prospective Tenant (freq: 2,856)
  4) Apply -> Set -> View -> Hand In -> Check -> Screen -> Extensive Screening -> Reject Prospective Tenant (freq: 1,815)

- Successful applicant -> move-in variants (observed):
  5) Apply -> Set -> View -> Hand In -> Check -> Screen -> Sign Contract -> Move In -> Pay Rent -> (Pay Rent repeated zero or more times) -> Tenant Cancels Apartment (many variant frequencies; aggregated Sign->MoveIn freq: 2,770; MoveIn->PayRent freq: 2,656; PayRent->TenantCancels frequency: 2,680)
  6) Same as 5 but with multiple Pay Rent repeats before Tenant Cancels Apartment (many observed variants with 2, 3, 4, ... repeats of Pay Rent). The process permits any number of Pay Rent events between Move In and Tenant Cancels Apartment (observed variants provide many concrete counts).

- Paths including missed payments and warnings (enforcement loops):
  7) (... Move In ->) Pay Rent -> Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning -> Accept Late Payment -> Pay Rent (loop back) — common remediation path (observed frequencies: Miss->Issue 945; Issue->AcceptLate 855; AcceptLate->PayRent 794).
  8) (... Move In ->) Pay Rent (or Miss) -> Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning -> Evict Tenant (rare: Issue->Evict 90). This is the enforcement termination path.
  9) Accept Late Payment -> Miss Rent Payment (observed: 61) — shows a rework cycle where a late payment occurrence is followed by a subsequent missed payment.

- Variants where Move In is immediately followed by Miss Rent Payment:
  10) Sign Contract -> Move In -> Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning -> (Accept Late Payment -> Pay Rent ... OR Evict Tenant) (MoveIn->Miss observed: 114)

- Note on optional/skips from the Petri net (observed skip behavior in nets):
  - The mined Petri net contains invisible "skip" branches that correspond to optional paths in which some activities (e.g., Set Appointment or View or Hand In) are omitted in observed traces. These are reflected in the variants above (e.g., Apply -> Reject without appointment/view; also screening branching variations). The AS-IS description allows these optional skips: viewing, paperwork and the full screening chain are optional if a rejection occurs earlier.

Parallelism and concurrency
- No explicit parallel branches were observed in the mined models. All recorded behavior is effectively sequential (a well-defined directly-follows order). The Pay Rent self-loop produces repeated events but not parallel concurrent instances of the same process instance. Therefore the process is modeled as primarily sequential with loops (no parallel split/join nodes required by the observed behavior).

All variability and ordering rules (behavioral fidelity requirements)
- Permitted orderings (must allow all observed permutations):
  - Apply must precede any other applicant-related activities in observed traces.
  - Apply -> Set Appointment -> View The Property -> Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant is a highly frequent chain; after Screen the process may:
    - immediately Reject; or
    - require Extensive Screening then either Reject or Sign Contract; or
    - directly Sign Contract.
  - Sign Contract -> Move In is required before any Pay Rent / Miss Rent Payment events occur.
  - Move In -> Pay Rent typically, but Move In -> Miss Rent Payment is allowed (rare).
  - After Move In, Pay Rent may repeat arbitrarily many times (Pay Rent loop); between payments a Miss Rent Payment may occur, triggering the Miss->IssueWarning->(AcceptLate OR Evict) subflow.
  - Accept Late Payment returns the tenant to the Pay Rent flow (Accept Late Payment -> Pay Rent) in most observed cases; it can also be followed by Miss Rent Payment (rework).
  - Tenant Cancels Apartment may occur after one or more Pay Rent events as a terminating event.
  - Rejection (at several possible earlier checkpoints) is a terminating outcome for that trace.

- Important directly-follows frequencies (sample):
  - Pay Rent -> Pay Rent: 14,819 (highly frequent; multiple rent cycles are common)
  - Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Set Appointment: 9,151
  - Set Appointment -> View The Property: 9,151
  - View The Property -> Hand In The Paperwork: 7,441
  - Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork: 7,441
  - Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant: 7,441
  - Screen Prospective Tenant -> Reject Prospective Tenant: 2,856
  - Screen Prospective Tenant -> Extensive Screening: 2,793
  - Screen Prospective Tenant -> Sign Contract: 1,792
  - Sign Contract -> Move In: 2,770
  - Move In -> Pay Rent: 2,656
  - Pay Rent -> Tenant Cancels Apartment: 2,680
  - Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning: 945
  - Issue Warning -> Accept Late Payment: 855
  - Accept Late Payment -> Pay Rent: 794
  - Issue Warning -> Evict Tenant: 90 (rare)
  - Accept Late Payment -> Miss Rent Payment: 61 (rare)
- Observed variants distribution:
  - The most frequent full variant observed: Apply -> Set -> View -> Hand In -> Check -> Screen -> Reject (freq: 2,856).
  - There are many variants that extend the “successful” path with a repeated Pay Rent loop before eventual Tenant Cancels Apartment, with decreasing frequency as the number of repeated payments increases. Variant performance metrics provided with the data show that longer sequences (many Pay Rent repeats before cancellation) correspond to much larger total durations (performance numbers increase with the number of payments).

Notes on timing / performance values
- The source includes performance metrics for directly-follows arcs and for variants (large numeric values). These numbers are the miner’s computed average durations for those flows (units and interpretation depend on the logging system). The data show substantial delays overall (e.g., Apply->Set performance ~82,731; View->HandIn ~81,965; Screen->Reject and Screen->Sign have large values). Variants that include many repeated Pay Rent events have much larger total performance (overall case durations) — consistent with multi-month or multi-period tenancies.

Exception handling, rework loops and escalation procedures (observed)
- Missed payment flow is the principal exception/rework path:
  - Miss Rent Payment triggers Issue Warning.
  - Issue Warning either leads to remediation (Accept Late Payment) or escalates to Evict Tenant (rare).
  - Accept Late Payment typically returns the tenant to the normal Pay Rent loop; sometimes the tenant misses again, re-entering the missed-payment cycle.
- Multiple accept/late/miss cycles are observed — the process must allow repeated enforcement cycles.
- Eviction is observed as a terminal escalation after warnings (observed but uncommon).
- Applicant rejection can occur at several stages (immediately on apply, after viewing, after screening or after extensive screening). Rejections are terminal.

Observed pain points, inefficiencies and risks
- High rejection early and mid-process: substantial share of applicants are rejected at different stages — this can indicate either tight selection criteria, poor applicant filtering up-front, or bottlenecks that cause applicants to be turned away at viewing or screening.
- Heavy variability and long case durations: the performance measures indicate substantial delays between many activities; many cases have very long durations (especially those with many Pay Rent cycles before cancellation). Long delays may reflect slow manual checks (paperwork, screening) or slow response to missed payments.
- Repeated payment and missed-payment cycles: large frequency of repeated Pay Rent events and recurring Miss Rent -> Warning -> Accept Late Payment cycles indicate an operational burden for rent collection and enforcement; Accept Late Payment -> Miss Rent Payment (observed) suggests that remediation does not necessarily resolve the underlying payment issue.
- Eviction is rare but present: although infrequent, eviction is an expensive and risky outcome.
- Optional/skipped steps: the mined Petri net includes observed “skips” (some applicants are rejected before viewing/handing-in paperwork). If these are unintended (e.g., incomplete logging or failed handoffs), they indicate inconsistent process execution.
- Lack of concurrency is fine for correctness but may indicate underutilized parallel processing opportunities (e.g., parallel checks during screening) if speed is a concern.
- Significantly different case durations across variants (especially very long-living tenants with many payment events) suggest that aggregated KPIs (e.g., median or mean case duration) may be skewed; need segmentation.

Process metrics and performance characteristics (summary)
- Most frequent early path: Apply -> Set Appointment -> View The Property (9,151 occurrences).
- Most frequent full rejection path observed: Apply -> ... -> Screen -> Reject (2,856 occurrences).
- Frequent conversion path to tenancy: Sign Contract -> Move In -> Pay Rent (thousands of occurrences: Sign->MoveIn 2,770; MoveIn->PayRent 2,656).
- Payment loop intensity: Pay Rent self-loop is the single most frequent directly-follows relation (14,819), showing strong repeat behavior (multiple rent periods).
- Missed payment and enforcement: Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning (945), Issue Warning -> Accept Late Payment (855), Accept Late Payment -> Pay Rent (794); Issue Warning -> Evict Tenant (90) — indicates the enforcement chain is active and causes rework.
- Variant-level durations: variants with many Pay Rent events before cancellation have substantially larger total performance (total duration) than short variants (examples in the variants list show case durations growing with the number of payments).

Key challenges and pain points observed (actionable observations)
- Large variability in candidate outcomes (many rejections but also many long-living tenant cases) complicates capacity planning for screening, enforcement and customer service.
- The payment enforcement loop is a frequent source of rework and repeated case activity. Because Accept Late Payment sometimes fails to prevent subsequent missed payments, current remediation may be insufficient for certain tenants.
- Processing times between activities (paperwork, screening, appointment scheduling) appear long in the provided performance measures; this suggests possible manual activities or delays that could be targeted for automation or parallelization.
- The presence of many variants with repeated Pay Rent events before Tenant Cancels Apartment indicates churn (tenants leave after some number of payments) — further business analysis is needed to understand reasons for cancellation and whether it represents normal turnover or a problem (e.g., tenant dissatisfaction).
- Eviction is rare but costly; the process shows few successful pathways to prevent eviction besides Accept Late Payment — there may be an opportunity to design better alternatives (payment plans, earlier intervention) to reduce evictions.

Final summary (concise behavioral model)
- Start: Apply for Viewing Appointment.
- Primary happy path (observed): Apply -> Set Appointment -> View The Property -> Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant -> (Sign Contract OR Extensive Screening -> Sign Contract) -> Move In -> Pay Rent (repeat) -> optionally Tenant Cancels Apartment (terminal) OR continue paying.
- Rejection alternatives: Apply -> Reject OR Apply -> Set -> View -> Reject OR Apply -> ... -> Screen -> Reject OR ... -> Screen -> Extensive Screening -> Reject.
- Payment exceptions: at any time after Move In, Miss Rent Payment can occur -> Issue Warning -> Accept Late Payment (back to Pay Rent) OR Issue Warning -> Evict Tenant (terminal). Accept Late Payment can also be followed by Miss Rent Payment (rework).
- The model allows repeated Pay Rent events (0..many), repeated Miss/Warning/Accept cycles, and multiple early-stage skip/reject variants. All observed variants described above are accepted by this AS-IS description.

This AS-IS description captures all observed behaviors in the provided Petri net, DFG and abstract variants: every documented branch, optional skip, loop and terminal outcome is included and permitted by the process description above.
