IDEAL Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Scope: Standardized, governed pathway from registration to discharge using the same activity set observed in the log, with improved sequencing, documentation, and escalation logic.
- Purpose: Reduce rework, ensure one clear outcome per treatment cycle, and harmonize entry and examination practices while preserving necessary clinical flexibility.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Physicians (Doc. Low, Doc. Pro): Lead Diagnosis and Treatment; perform Expert Examination; participate in Thorough Examination as required.
- Nursing staff (Nurse 1–5): Execute registration activities and support Examination and Thorough Examination per protocol.
- Role assignment is standardized via skill-based routing:
  - Expert Examination: primarily Doc. Pro or senior physicians.
  - Thorough Examination: physician-led with nurse support.
  - Examination (basic): nurse-initiated with physician oversight where needed.

Detailed step-by-step process flow
Start conditions and triggers
- Trigger: Patient arrival.
- Harmonized registration (exclusive channel, standardized data set):
  - Register at ER or Register at FD.
  - Both channels capture an identical, complete data set to avoid downstream variability (same mandatory fields, same identifiers).
  - Both feed into the same standardized clinical pathway.

Standardized clinical pathway
1) Initial Examination baseline
   - Perform Examination (basic) once as the default intake assessment.
   - Decision: If complexity/severity warrants, escalate; otherwise proceed.

2) Escalation criteria and options (exclusive-or-escalatory)
   - If findings are unclear or higher acuity is detected, perform Thorough Examination (once per clinical episode).
   - If specialized assessment is required or repeated unsuccessful outcomes occur, perform Expert Examination (once per escalation event).
   - Avoid unnecessary repetition: each examination type should occur at most once per diagnostic cycle unless new clinical information emerges.

3) Diagnosis (must follow examination)
   - Exactly one Diagnosis per treatment cycle.
   - Rule: No Treatment without a preceding Diagnosis in the same cycle.

4) Treatment (must follow diagnosis)
   - Execute Treatment aligned to the current Diagnosis.

5) Mandatory treatment outcome recording (exactly one outcome per treatment)
   - Immediately after each Treatment, record exactly one:
     - Treatment successful, or
     - Treatment unsuccessful.
   - This closes the treatment cycle and enables clear loop control.

6) Post-outcome decision and loop control
   - If Treatment successful: Proceed to Discharge.
   - If Treatment unsuccessful:
     - Initiate controlled rework loop:
       a) Optionally perform additional Examination or escalate to Expert Examination if not yet performed in this episode.
       b) Perform a new Diagnosis reflecting updated findings.
       c) Perform a new Treatment.
       d) Record the outcome (exactly one).
     - After a bounded number of cycles (as per clinical policy), require Expert Examination before any further treatment.

7) Discharge
   - Discharge after the final successful treatment OR, if clinically required, after an unsuccessful treatment when further intervention is not indicated (e.g., referral/transfer context). The Discharge activity remains the single terminal event; the context is captured in case data.

Decision points with criteria and possible outcomes
- Registration (exclusive): ER vs FD based on arrival channel; same data standards apply.
- Examination escalation (exclusive-or):
  - Criteria: Vital signs, severity scales, abnormal findings.
  - Outcomes: Remain at Examination, escalate to Thorough Examination, or escalate to Expert Examination.
- Post-treatment decision (exclusive):
  - Treatment successful → Discharge.
  - Treatment unsuccessful → Controlled rework cycle (with bounded repetitions and enforced Expert Examination before third cycle, when applicable).

Parallel activities and synchronization points
- Examinations are sequenced to enforce clarity of escalation and avoid redundant work. Parallelism is not required to achieve the target improvements and is therefore not introduced.
- Synchronization point: Complete necessary examinations before Diagnosis; record outcome before moving to the next step.

Exception handling and error paths
- Documentation assurance: System enforces one outcome event per treatment; cannot proceed without it.
- Early discharge on clinical grounds after unsuccessful outcome is permitted but must be justified and tagged at Discharge (case attribute), ensuring auditability.
- Protected or special-status patients (per case attributes) are flagged, but the activity set remains the same; access and privacy safeguards apply without altering the control flow.

Rework loops and escalation procedures
- Rework loop is allowed only after Treatment unsuccessful and must include:
  - Optional Examination (typically escalate to Expert Examination if repetition occurs),
  - Mandatory new Diagnosis,
  - Treatment,
  - Single outcome recording.
- Loop is bounded by policy (e.g., escalation to Expert Examination before repeat treatments; hard limits set by governance to avoid uncontrolled cycling).

End conditions and outcomes
- End state: Discharge.
- Two governed contexts:
  1) Discharge after Treatment successful (default).
  2) Discharge after Treatment unsuccessful with documented clinical rationale (e.g., transfer or no further indicated intervention).

Process metrics and performance characteristics (targets)
- Compliance:
  - 100% of Treatments have exactly one recorded outcome event.
  - 100% of Diagnoses are preceded by at least one Examination in the same cycle.
- Rework control:
  - Median of 1 Diagnosis and 1 Treatment per case; rework rate reduced vs. AS-IS.
  - Unsuccessful-to-successful conversion within one rework cycle in the majority of cases.
- Examination efficiency:
  - At most one occurrence of each examination type per cycle unless new data justify repetition.
  - Expert Examination performed before any third treatment attempt.
- Registration harmonization:
  - Zero data-field discrepancies between ER and FD registration; complete and consistent identifiers.

Improvements made (vs. AS-IS)
- Removed ambiguity in treatment outcomes by enforcing exactly-one outcome event per treatment.
- Structured and bounded rework loop with mandatory re-diagnosis and escalation rules, reducing unnecessary repetitions of exams and treatments.
- Standardized examination progression (basic → thorough → expert as needed) to reduce variability and cycle time.
- Harmonized ER and FD registration to eliminate data inconsistencies while preserving both channels.
- Clarified role routing: senior physicians (Doc. Pro) lead Expert Examination and complex cases; nurses support standardized examination steps, improving throughput and reducing bottlenecks.
