AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Overview: This AS-IS description captures the current observed hiring workflow reconstructed from the supplied Petri net, directly-follows frequencies (DFG), and abstract variants. It documents the real sequence of candidate handling from initial application submission through screening and multiple interview tracks, background checks, possible rework loops, and terminal outcomes (Application Rejected or Make Job Offer). The observed workflow is complex, contains optional and parallel branches, frequent rework and contradictory transitions (e.g., Application Rejected leading back into interview activities or to Make Job Offer), and multiple opportunities for skip-logic and invisible synchronizations.
- Purpose: Provide a faithful, detailed description of how hiring cases actually proceed in the observed data so stakeholders can understand current behavior, identify noncompliant or wasteful patterns, and prioritize improvements.

Key stakeholders and roles involved (inferred from activities)
- Applicant: the person submitting the job application and participating in interviews and checks.
- Recruiter / Sourcing / Talent Acquisition: initiates Telephonic Screening, coordinates next steps, and typically manages early rejections and scheduling.
- Technical Interviewers: conduct Coding Interview, ML Interview, Frontend Interview, Backend Interview.
- Behavioral Interviewers / Hiring Manager: conduct Behavioral Interview(s) and make subjective hire/offer judgments.
- Background-Check Team / Vendor / HR Operations: performs Background Check and Extensive Background Check.
- HR / Offer Administration: issues Make Job Offer and records final status (offer accepted/declined).
Note: roles are inferred from activity names; the provided models do not explicitly assign swimlanes.

Detailed step-by-step process flow (AS-IS)
Start conditions and trigger
1. Start: A candidate begins the process by 'Hand In Job Application' (source->Hand In Job Application). This is the unique observed start event.

Primary observed path elements (sequence with frequencies / notes)
2. Telephonic Screening: The most common immediate step after application is 'Telephonic Screening'. DFG frequency: 8,799 direct follows from application to telephonic screening. This step is a major gate but not exclusive: some cases skip it and proceed directly to other steps or are rejected immediately (see variants).
3. From Telephonic Screening the observed outcomes are:
   - To Screening Interview (freq ~3,848) — common progression.
   - To Background Check (freq ~3,961) — indicating some cases move from telephonic screening directly into background verification.
   - Direct Application Rejected (freq ~990) — recruiter rejects after phone screen.
   - Rarely other irregular transitions exist via skip/tau places in the Petri net.
4. Screening Interview: After screening interview, candidates may be:
   - Routed to Coding Interview (freq ~1,517).
   - Routed to Background Check (freq ~1,391) — observed cases where background check occurs after screening.
   - Rejected (Application Rejected) directly (freq ~1,389).
   - Sometimes screening interview directly leads to Extensive Background Check (freq ~1,055) as a separate branch.
5. Technical Interview Track(s): The coding interview is a major pivot. Observed behavior shows:
   - Coding Interview -> Frontend Interview (freq ~1,513) and/or -> Backend Interview (freq ~701).
   - Coding Interview -> ML Interview (freq ~1,304) in many cases.
   - Coding Interview sometimes leads directly to Application Rejected (freq ~386).
6. Cross-links and rework between technical interviews:
   - There is a significant bidirectional flow between Frontend Interview and ML Interview (Frontend->ML freq 878; ML->Frontend freq 913), and also between Backend and Frontend (Backend->Frontend freq 487; Frontend->Backend freq 529). Backend <-> ML also shows some bidirectional activity (Backend->ML 152; ML->Backend 121). This indicates multiple re-evaluations, retesting, or iterative interview loops.
7. Behavioral Interview(s): Behavioral Interview sometimes occurs later in the sequence: frequently it is followed by a Make Job Offer (Behavioral -> Make Job Offer freq ~1,850). However, Behavioral Interview also repeats: Behavioral -> Behavioral observed (freq ~1,032), indicating duplicated/higher-than-expected behavioral rounds.
8. Background Checks and Extensive Background Checks:
   - Background Check appears both early and mid-process, and sometimes after the screening or even after technical interviews.
   - There is an 'Extensive Background Check' activity that frequently leads to Application Rejected (freq ~1,125) and in some cases to Coding Interview (freq ~974). Background Check -> Extensive Background Check (freq ~1,044) and Screening Interview -> Extensive Background Check (freq ~1,055). This indicates an escalation path when issues are flagged.
9. Decision points and final outcomes:
   - Application Rejected is a very common final state, but the data also shows Application Rejected feeding back into Behavioral Interview (Application Rejected -> Behavioral Interview freq ~740) and into Make Job Offer (Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer freq ~690). There are even self-loops on Application Rejected (freq ~654). These indicate either data-quality artifacts or real rework/appeal flows where rejected cases are reconsidered and re-interviewed or converted to offers.
   - Make Job Offer sometimes repeats (Make Job Offer -> Make Job Offer freq ~861), and Make Job Offer -> Application Rejected (freq ~110) shows offers being rescinded or rejected later.

Decision points with criteria and observed outcomes
- Post-Telephonic Screening decision: recruiter either (a) advance to screening interview or (b) request background check or (c) reject. Criteria in practice appear to be loosely applied (high variability in counts).
- Post-Screening Interview: options observed are (a) advance to coding interview; (b) request background check or escalate to extensive background check; (c) direct rejection. In some cases, background checks are interleaved with technical interviews.
- Post-Coding and technical interviews: typical outcomes are (a) move to next technical interview; (b) move to behavioral; (c) rejection; (d) repeat or loop to another technical track. Criteria appear inconsistent across cases, as shown by many bidirectional transitions.
- Background check escalation: if basic background check flags issues, escalate to Extensive Background Check. Extensive checks frequently end in rejection.

Parallel activities and synchronization
- The Petri net contains tau splits and joins indicating optional parallelism. The DFG and variants show multiple technical interview tracks (ML, Frontend, Backend) that can follow coding interview in various orders and sometimes interleave or repeat — this suggests optional concurrency in scheduling (panels may be scheduled in parallel or sequentially). However, the observed logs show many sequences rather than clear parallel starts; parallelism exists but is not consistently enforced.
- There are invisible transitions (skip_*, tauSplit, tauJoin) in the Petri net that model optional skips and synchronizations: e.g., some candidates skip technical steps and move directly to behavioral or to background checks.

Exception handling, rework loops, escalation procedures
- Reworks are frequent: technical interviews loop across Frontend/ML/Backend (bidirectional flows), Behavioral Interview repeats, and Application Rejected sometimes loops back to interviews or to Make Job Offer. These loops create prolonged case durations.
- Escalation to Extensive Background Check occurs when flags appear; Extensive Background Check has high rejection rate.
- Offers are occasionally rescinded or reversed to rejection (Make Job Offer -> Application Rejected) or made after prior rejection (Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer) — both indicate weak governance or multiple decision authorities.

End conditions and outcomes
- Final marking in the Petri net indicates sink state after tauJoin_15. Observed terminal outcomes are primarily:
  - Application Rejected (majority of cases across many variants).
  - Make Job Offer (frequent in longer multi-stage success variants).
- Many cases end in Application Rejected but with long, resource-consuming prior activity sequences.

Process metrics and performance characteristics (from DFG and variants)
- Volume and frequencies (selected):
  - Hand In -> Telephonic Screening: 8,799 occurrences (dominant initial path).
  - Telephonic Screening -> Background Check: 3,961.
  - Screening Interview -> Coding Interview: 1,517.
  - Behavioral Interview -> Make Job Offer: 1,850.
  - Application Rejected appears very frequently in many positions (1,391; 1,389; 1,201 etc.).
- Performance (observed durations in the DFG): many transitions show extremely large average times (e.g., Coding Interview -> Screening Interview performance ~129,892; Background Check -> Application Rejected ~155,319; Extensive Background Check -> Application Rejected ~171,097). These numbers indicate long delays (likely measured in seconds or minutes depending on logging) and substantial cycle time contribution from background checks and late rejections.
- Variant concentration: The top rejection-heavy variants (Hand In -> Telephonic -> Screening -> Background -> Application Rejected and Hand In -> Telephonic -> Background -> Screening -> Application Rejected) together account for a substantial share of rejections. There are many long successful variants but they are relatively low frequency and have high cumulative performance (very long completions).

Key challenges and pain points (AS-IS)
- Inconsistent sequencing and governance: background checks appear at multiple points (pre- and post-technical), and there is no single gating policy; candidates may be rejected after many expensive technical interviews.
- Significant rework loops: bidirectional flows between technical interviews, repeated behavioral interviews, and cases where rejected candidates re-enter interview tracks.
- Data-quality or policy anomalies: Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer and Application Rejected -> Behavioral Interview suggest either appeals/overturning decisions without formal steps or logging artifacts.
- Long cycle times and bottlenecks: Background checks and extensive checks are slow and often occur late in the process, causing wasted interview effort. Coding-related transitions show very high average durations in some edges (indicating delays between steps or long scheduling waits).
- Lack of parallelization discipline: scheduling technical interviews sequentially in many cases, while in other cases they interleave, producing unpredictable lead times.
- Redundant approvals and repeated 'Make Job Offer' events: offers are sometimes issued multiple times or rescinded.

Summary of AS-IS behavior
- The observed recruiting process starts consistently with Hand In Job Application, goes predominantly through Telephonic Screening, then follows diverging paths that include Screening Interview, Background Check (and its escalation), Coding and multi-track technical interviews, Behavioral Interview(s), and either Application Rejected or Make Job Offer.
- The real process is noisy: high-frequency rejection paths, many loops and re-entries, inconsistent timing and late-stage gating (background checks), and traces that indicate governance and logging problems. These issues generate high cost, long cycle times, and poor candidate experience.

Recommended immediate data/analysis checks (for implementers)
- Verify whether Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer transitions are legitimate (appeals or data errors).
- Confirm the correct sequencing policy for background checks (where they should occur and whether they should gate interviews).
- Identify the cause of repeated behavioral interviews and repeated Make Job Offer events (are duplicates logged or is there a real repeated action?).
