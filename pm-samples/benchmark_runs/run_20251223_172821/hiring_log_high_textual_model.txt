AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: This is the observed hiring / candidate-selection process (recruiting pipeline) as discovered from the event data and the inductive Petri net. The process begins when a candidate submits an application ("Hand In Job Application") and — depending on many optional and branching steps — ends in either an "Application Rejected" outcome or a "Make Job Offer" outcome. The discovered model contains many optional steps, parallel branches (especially technical interviews), loops (repeated interviews and repeated offers), and exception paths where a rejection may be followed by further activity (appeals, reconsideration, data noise, or reprocessing).
- Scope: Selection activities from application submission through screening, background checks, technical interviews, behavioral interview(s), and final decision (reject or offer).

Stakeholders and roles
- The source models do not assign explicit human roles or organizational units to activities. No actors/roles are invented. If mapping to organizations is needed, assign roles (e.g., Recruiter, Screener, Hiring Manager, Technical Interviewer, Background-check team) only after validating with process owners.

Start conditions and triggers
- Start event (inferred): "Hand In Job Application" — the candidate submits an application. All observed traces in the data start with this activity.

End conditions / outcomes (observed final states)
- Primary observed end states:
  1. "Application Rejected" — most frequent terminal-like outcome in many variants.
  2. "Make Job Offer" — observed as a terminal outcome in many traces.
- Important caveat: In the event data the activity "Application Rejected" is not strictly terminal — there are many observed traces where "Application Rejected" is followed by further activities (including Behavioral Interview and even Make Job Offer). The Petri net shows a canonical sink after "Make Job Offer," but the event data contains nonstandard flows that continue after rejection; the AS-IS model must allow those traces.

High-level component tasks (visible activities)
- Hand In Job Application
- Telephonic Screening
- Background Check
- Screening Interview
- Extensive Background Check
- Coding Interview
- Frontend Interview
- ML Interview
- Backend Interview
- Behavioral Interview
- Application Rejected
- Make Job Offer

Detailed step-by-step observed flow (all variants, branches, concurrency, loops preserved)

1) Initial submission
- Step 1: Hand In Job Application (start). Frequency evidence: this activity is the clear start for the observed traces.

2) Telephonic screening (optional branching immediately after submission)
- After submission the dominant next activity is Telephonic Screening.
  - Observed direct follow: Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening (high frequency: 8,799).
  - Alternative: Hand In Job Application -> Application Rejected (direct rejection occurs in many traces: frequency 1,201). That is, some applications are rejected immediately without screening.
- After Telephonic Screening there are three main possibilities (all observed):
  A. Proceed to Screening Interview
  B. Proceed to Background Check
  C. Immediate Application Rejected
- Important: Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview and Telephonic Screening -> Background Check are both frequent. Either may happen alone, both may happen, or one may be skipped. The event data shows:
  - Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview (freq 3,848)
  - Telephonic Screening -> Background Check (freq 3,961)
  - Telephonic Screening -> Application Rejected (freq 990)
- Observed orders between Screening Interview and Background Check:
  - Screening Interview can happen before Background Check, or Background Check can happen before Screening Interview. Both orders are observed and frequent:
    - Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview -> Background Check (observed)
    - Telephonic Screening -> Background Check -> Screening Interview (observed)
  - Both activities may be executed and then synchronize (both completed) before further progression (see synchronization below).
  - Either Screening Interview or Background Check may be skipped entirely in some traces.

Decision point A (after Telephonic Screening)
- Condition outcomes (observed):
  - If Telephonic Screening fails screening criteria -> Application Rejected (observed often).
  - If Telephonic Screening passes -> one or both of Screening Interview and Background Check will be triggered, possibly in either order, possibly in parallel, possibly skipping either.

3) Screening Interview and Background Check — concurrency and synchronization
- The model shows these two steps as a pair of tasks that:
  - May happen in either order.
  - May happen both (either sequentially or concurrently).
  - May have one skipped.
- Synchronization: The Petri net indicates that when both Screening Interview and Background Check are required, their completions synchronize before the next stage (i.e., the process waits for both if both were executed).
- Observed outcomes from these tasks:
  - From Background Check directly to Application Rejected is common (Background Check -> Application Rejected frequency 1,391).
  - From Screening Interview directly to Application Rejected is also common (Screening Interview -> Application Rejected frequency 1,389).
  - From either Screening Interview or Background Check it is possible to continue to an Extensive Background Check (Screening Interview -> Extensive Background Check freq 1,055; Background Check -> Extensive Background Check freq 1,044).
  - From Screening Interview it is also common to continue to Coding Interview (Screening Interview -> Coding Interview freq 1,517), but only in traces where the candidate is advanced for technical assessment.

4) Extensive Background Check (optional escalation)
- When triggered (from Screening Interview or Background Check), Extensive Background Check is an extra verification / escalation step:
  - Observed paths: Screening Interview -> Extensive Background Check and Background Check -> Extensive Background Check.
  - After Extensive Background Check:
    - It commonly leads to Application Rejected (Extensive Background Check -> Application Rejected freq 1,125).
    - In many traces, Extensive Background Check may later lead back into technical assessment (Extensive Background Check -> Coding Interview freq 974). Thus, Extensive Background Check is an optional escalation that can either terminate the case with rejection or feed back into interview stages.

5) Technical assessment: Coding Interview
- When the candidate passes earlier checks, they may be advanced to a Coding Interview (observed after Screening Interview and after Extensive Background Check / Background Check in many variants).
  - Observed transitions: Screening Interview -> Coding Interview (freq 1,517); Background Check -> Coding Interview (freq 1,413); Extensive Background Check -> Coding Interview (freq 974).
- Coding Interview is frequently followed by a set of technical interviews (ML Interview, Backend Interview, Frontend Interview) or the process may be rejected at the coding stage.
  - Coding Interview -> Application Rejected (freq 386) occurs in many traces.

6) Technical interviews (ML, Frontend, Backend): parallelism, ordering variability, skips, and rework
- The model shows that after Coding Interview the process may split into multiple technical interviews. Characteristics observed:
  - The technical interviews (ML Interview, Frontend Interview, Backend Interview) can occur in different orders and sometimes concurrently.
  - Any of these interviews may be skipped in some traces (skip transitions in the Petri net).
  - Observed pairwise transitions and interleavings:
    - Coding Interview -> Frontend Interview (freq 1,513)
    - Coding Interview -> ML Interview (freq 1,304)
    - Coding Interview -> Backend Interview (freq 701)
    - Frontend <-> ML are frequently observed both ways (Frontend Interview -> ML Interview freq 878; ML Interview -> Frontend Interview freq 913). This indicates that Frontend and ML interviews are sometimes repeated and can follow each other in either order.
    - Backend and Frontend also interleave: Backend Interview -> Frontend Interview (freq 487); Frontend Interview -> Backend Interview (freq 529). Backend <-> ML also have some cross flows (Backend -> ML 152; ML -> Backend 121) though at lower frequency.
  - The model allows the following: any combination of ML, Frontend, Backend interviews may occur; the sequence can be:
    - Single technical interview only (e.g., only Frontend), or
    - Two interviews in either order (e.g., Frontend then ML, or ML then Frontend), or
    - All three interviews in any interleaving and with possible repetitions (the data shows loops and cross-calls).
  - Rework/repeat behavior: Technical interviews (especially ML and Frontend) show repeated occurrences in traces (observed mutual transitions and repeats), indicating candidate re-evaluations or re-scheduling.

Decision points / outcomes at technical interview stage
- From any of the technical interviews the following outcomes are observed:
  - Application Rejected (common from Frontend, ML, Backend).
  - Progression to Behavioral Interview.
  - If multiple technical interviews are required, they synchronize (Petri net shows a join where ML/Backend/Frontend outputs are merged) before advancing to subsequent steps.
  - Note: the data shows traces where Application Rejected appears after technical interviews, and also traces where technical interviews continue after an Application Rejected event (see exceptions below).

7) Behavioral Interview (final interview stage, can repeat)
- After technical interviews and/or background checks the Behavioral Interview is commonly executed.
  - Observed Behavior:
    - Behavioral Interview frequently occurs and often repeats (Behavioral Interview -> Behavioral Interview freq 1,032), so the model permits repeated behavioral interviews (reinterviews, follow-ups).
    - Behavioral Interview -> Make Job Offer (freq 1,850) is a common successful path.
    - Behavioral Interview -> Application Rejected (freq 914) occurs as well.
- The Petri net shows Behavioral Interview reachable from a path emanating after the technical interview join; the model allows either the rejection path or behavioral interview path (and sometimes both in different traces).

8) Final decisions and end-of-case behavior (non-determinism and post-rejection activity)
- Make Job Offer:
  - Observed as a final stage; it can occasionally repeat (Make Job Offer -> Make Job Offer freq 861), meaning offers may be updated / renegotiated or multiple offer events recorded.
  - From Make Job Offer, some traces show Make Job Offer -> Application Rejected (freq 110) — recorded retractions or later rejection of an offer.
- Application Rejected:
  - Observed from many upstream activities: directly after Hand In Job Application, Telephonic Screening, Background Check, Screening Interview, Extensive Background Check, Coding Interview, Frontend, ML, Backend, and Behavioral Interview.
  - Application Rejected sometimes appears and the trace terminates; however the event log also contains many cases where Application Rejected is followed by further activities (e.g., Application Rejected -> Behavioral Interview (freq 740), Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer (freq 690), Application Rejected -> Application Rejected (freq 654)). That indicates:
    - Either the recorded "Application Rejected" events do not strictly correspond to the final business decision (data-modeling artifact), or
    - There are formal re-openings / appeals / reconsiderations, or
    - Data quality issues (duplicates / incorrect timestamps).
  - The AS-IS model therefore must permit continued activity after a rejection event because such traces were observed.

9) Loops and rework
- Repeated Behavioral Interviews: common (Behavioral Interview -> Behavioral Interview).
- Repeated Make Job Offer events: common (Make Job Offer -> Make Job Offer).
- Technical interviews may repeat or interleave (ML <-> Frontend loops, etc).
- Background-check loop with Extensive Background Check leads sometimes back into Coding Interview — i.e., an extensive check may be resolved and the candidate then proceeds to coding again.
- These loops are observed frequently and must be considered part of valid behavior.

All valid ordering permutations / interleavings (summary)
- Screening Interview and Background Check:
  - Valid orderings: [Screening -> Background], [Background -> Screening], [Screening only], [Background only], [neither -> direct rejection or skip].
- Technical interviews (post-Coding Interview):
  - Valid orderings include any interleaving of {Coding Interview, Frontend Interview, ML Interview, Backend Interview}, with Coding Interview commonly preceding the technical trio. The trio {Frontend, ML, Backend} can be executed in any order, possibly concurrently, possibly skipping some, and possibly repeating steps.
  - Examples of observed long variant sequences include:
    - Coding -> ML -> Frontend -> Behavioral -> Behavioral -> Make Job Offer -> Make Job Offer
    - Coding -> Frontend -> ML -> Behavioral -> Make Job Offer
    - Coding -> Frontend -> Backend -> Behavioral -> Make Job Offer
  - The event variants include traces with many permutations and with occasional Application Rejected occurrences inserted at different points.
- Rejection and continuation:
  - Observed sequences allow Application Rejected at many points. Some traces have Application Rejected late, others early. Some traces show Application Rejected followed by further interviews and even Make Job Offer — all observed and therefore valid.

Mapping to the Petri net (key control constructs inferred)
- Start: source -> Hand In Job Application.
- After Telephonic Screening there is a split (tauSplit_4) enabling Screening Interview and Background Check in parallel or skipping either (skip transitions present).
- Screening Interview and Background Check join (tauJoin_5) before enabling Extensive Background Check or Coding Interview decisions.
- Coding Interview (when present) triggers a split (tauSplit_9) enabling ML, Backend, Frontend interviews in parallel, with skip transitions available for each interview (any subset possible).
- The technical interviews' outputs are joined (tauJoin_10) and then the process splits again (tauSplit_14) into two branches: one leading toward Application Rejected (through a loop structure) and one toward Behavioral Interview and then to Make Job Offer.
- Several skip and init_loop transitions indicate optionality and possible reactivation and looping between phases (e.g., Extensive Background Check feeding back into Coding Interview, or Application Rejected being followed by skip transitions that enable further steps).
- Sink: eventually (in standard flows) tokens reach a sink via tauJoin_15 after Make Job Offer (the canonical end). But because the event data shows continued behavior after Application Rejected, the model permits non-terminal behavior following rejection.

Process metrics and performance characteristics (observed values from the DFG and variants)
- Frequency observations (selected edges, reflect how common paths are in event data):
  - Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening: 8,799 cases
  - Telephonic Screening -> Background Check: 3,961
  - Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview: 3,848
  - Screening Interview -> Coding Interview: 1,517
  - Background Check -> Coding Interview: 1,413
  - Background Check -> Application Rejected: 1,391
  - Screening Interview -> Application Rejected: 1,389
  - Behavioral Interview -> Make Job Offer: 1,850
  - Application Rejected -> Behavioral Interview: 740
  - Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer: 690
- Performance (given by the DFG; units not specified in input; treat as relative durations/time-lag measures):
  - Long delays observed on edges that lead to Application Rejected: e.g., Background Check -> Application Rejected performance ≈ 155,319 (high), Screening Interview -> Application Rejected ≈ 155,969.
  - Significant time lags before coding transitions: Screening Interview -> Coding Interview ≈ 129,892.
  - Telephonic Screening -> Behavioral outcomes have large durations (~82,400 range).
  - Make Job Offer and Behavioral Interview have relatively short internal loop durations (Make Job Offer -> Make Job Offer ≈ 70.746; Behavioral Interview -> Behavioral Interview ≈ 47.047), indicating frequent quick repeated events.
- Variant-level frequencies (high-level):
  - Many traces end quickly in rejection: e.g., Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview -> Background Check -> Application Rejected and the symmetrical variant where Background Check happens before Screening Interview (each ~1,391 and ~1,389 respectively).
  - Direct rejection from the application (Hand In Job Application -> Application Rejected) occurs in ~1,201 traces.
  - Long success paths that culminate in Make Job Offer (with multiple technical interviews and repeated behavioral interviews) exist but are far less frequent (tens to low hundreds per variant in the abstract variants list).
- Interpretation: There are many fast rejections and a much smaller set of very long, complex paths that lead to offers. The long measured durations on paths to Application Rejected suggest that many rejections are recorded late in the process or after lengthy verifications — this may indicate inefficient screening or lengthy background-check processing.

Key challenges and pain points observed in the current (AS-IS) process
1. High variability and non-determinism
   - Many optional activities, many valid interleavings and permutations of technical interviews, and many skip/loop transitions create a highly non-deterministic pipeline. This complexity complicates planning, resource allocation, and SLA enforcement.

2. Frequently long delays before decisions
   - Several transitions that lead to rejection show very long performance numbers (e.g., >100k in the DFG data). Long waiting times prior to rejection indicate wasted candidate wait time and inefficient processing (especially for unsuccessful candidates).

3. Inconsistent and ambiguous rejection semantics
   - "Application Rejected" appears both as a terminal outcome and as an intermediate event followed by more interviews or even a final "Make Job Offer." This inconsistency suggests:
     - Data-modeling issues (e.g., multiple recorded rejection events),
     - Process ambiguity (cases reopened after rejection), or
     - Procedural problems (decisions overturned, re-evaluations).
   - Business owners should validate what "Application Rejected" represents in the source system.

4. Rework and repeats are common
   - Behavioral interviews and offers have frequent self-loops; technical interviews are repeated or interleaved. Re-scheduling, second interviews, and repeated offer events add overhead and extend cycle times.

5. Complex branching around background checks
   - Background Check frequently triggers an Extensive Background Check which often leads to rejection but sometimes feeds back into the technical-review path. That branching is a hotspot for long case durations and decision reversals.

6. Low signal-to-noise for the "happy path"
   - The canonical ideal path (screen -> coding -> technical interviews -> behavioral -> offer) exists but is rare relative to noisy rejections and alternative patterns. The process is heavily skewed toward early or mid-process rejections.

7. Data-quality and modeling artifacts
   - The presence of transitions such as Application Rejected -> Behavioral Interview and Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer suggests either:
     - events recorded out of order,
     - multiple status updates logged,
     - or true re-openings; all require validation against source-system semantics.

All observed variants (representative classes; NOT exhaustive but inclusive of all observed types)
- Immediate rejection:
  - Hand In Job Application -> Application Rejected
- Rejection after phone screen:
  - Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening -> Application Rejected
- Rejection after screening/background checks:
  - Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview -> Background Check -> Application Rejected
  - Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening -> Background Check -> Screening Interview -> Application Rejected
  - Variants with Extensive Background Check inserted before rejection
- Progression to technical interviews and rejection later:
  - ... -> Coding Interview -> (Frontend / ML / Backend in any order, possibly repeated) -> Application Rejected
- Progression to offer:
  - ... -> Coding Interview -> technical interviews (one or more) -> Behavioral Interview (one or more) -> Make Job Offer (may repeat)
- Complex long traces (observed):
  - Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening -> Background Check -> Screening Interview -> Coding Interview -> ML -> Frontend -> Behavioral -> Behavioral -> Make Job Offer -> Make Job Offer (and many permutations where ML and Frontend orders swap, Backend interleaves, Application Rejected appears at different points, and Extensive Background Check appears before or after coding)
- Re-open / continuation after rejection:
  - Hand In Job Application -> ... -> Application Rejected -> Behavioral Interview -> Make Job Offer (observed in many traces)

Exception handling and escalation
- Extensive Background Check is an explicit escalation when background concerns arise; it commonly leads to rejection but sometimes to further interviews.
- When reviews produce conflicting outcomes, the process shows rework (more interviews, repeated behavioral interviews) or status changes (offer -> withdrawal).
- Multiple offer events may reflect negotiation or versioning of offers; an offer may later be withdrawn (Make Job Offer -> Application Rejected observed).

Synchronization points (where process waits for multiple branches)
- Screening Interview and Background Check synchronize before proceeding to coding or extensive checks (the Petri net shows a join).
- Technical interview outputs (ML / Backend / Frontend) are joined before the process proceeds toward the behavioral stage or decision/finalization.

Constraints and assumptions made in this AS-IS description
- No roles/actors were assigned in the source models; none are invented here.
- The description is conservative: any path allowed by the Petri net or observed in the DFG/variant lists is considered valid AS-IS behavior.
- Where "skip" and "init_loop" transitions appear in the Petri net they are interpreted as optionality and re-activation/loops; their semantic reasons (e.g., cancellation, data artifact) must be clarified with process owners.

Implications for process owners and suggested verification points
- Validate the concrete meaning of "Application Rejected" in the IT systems (is it a final state, an interim status, or logged in multiple steps?).
- Investigate long lead times on edges that eventually lead to rejection — are these background checks waiting on external providers, or are they internal bottlenecks?
- Clarify interview orchestration policies: which technical interviews are required, can they be done in parallel, should reinterviews be allowed, and under which conditions?
- Clean up data-modeling issues where possible (duplicate statuses, out-of-order events) to reduce ambiguity in monitoring and automation.

Summary (one-paragraph)
- The observed hiring process starts at Hand In Job Application, usually proceeds through Telephonic Screening, often involves Screening Interview and Background Check (in either order and sometimes both), then optionally Extensive Background Check and Coding Interview, and from coding proceeds to a flexible and highly variable set of technical interviews (Frontend, ML, Backend in many orders and interleavings). That is followed by one or more Behavioral Interviews and a final decision (Application Rejected or Make Job Offer). The process exhibits high variability, many optional/skip paths, frequent loops (repeated behavioral interviews, repeated offers), long delays (especially on paths that end in rejection), and ambiguous/re-opened rejections (Application Rejected is not always terminal in the event data). All variant sequences observed in the provided data are considered valid AS-IS behavior and are allowed by this description.
