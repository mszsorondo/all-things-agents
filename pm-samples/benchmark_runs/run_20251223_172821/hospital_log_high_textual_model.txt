AS-IS Process Model

1) Process overview and purpose
- Overview: This process describes the observed flow of patient cases from registration through clinical assessment(s), diagnosis, treatment, and discharge. The data and models (Petri net, DFG and discovered variants) show a medical intake/treatment process with two registration entry points, multiple examination types (regular and expert), an optional/thorough examination step, a diagnosis activity, a treatment activity that can succeed or fail, and a discharge at the end. The actual process permits many alternative paths and rework loops (treatment failures that lead back to diagnosis and re-treatment). All variants observed end in Discharge.
- Purpose: Provide care to the presenting patient, reach a diagnosis, apply treatment(s) and eventually discharge the patient when treatment ends (successful or otherwise).

2) Start and end events (inferred)
- Start events (explicit entry points observed):
  - Register at ER (Emergency Room)
  - Register at FD (Front Desk / Facility Desk)
  These are the only observed entry activities (source→Register at ER or Register at FD).
- End event (final state observed):
  - Discharge. All observed traces complete with Discharge (final marking in Petri net is sink reachable only from Discharge).

3) Key stakeholders and roles
- No explicit roles/actors were provided in the input models. The models imply clinical staff and registration staff, but these roles are not named and are therefore not assigned here. Do not invent named actors.

4) Visible activities (tasks/transitions)
- Register at ER
- Register at FD
- Examination
- Expert Examination
- Thorough Examination
- Diagnosis
- Treatment
- Treatment successful (observable outcome activity/state)
- Treatment unsuccessful (observable outcome activity/state)
- Discharge

(There are also invisible/silent transitions in the Petri net — used to model optional/skip and loops — but they are interpreted below as optional/skip behavior, not as new business activities.)

5) High-level, step-by-step process flow (including all observed variants)

Start: Patient registration
1. Start / trigger
   - The process always starts with a registration event: either "Register at ER" or "Register at FD".
   - Both registration activities are observed as valid alternative start nodes.

2. Post-registration branching (observed alternatives)
   After registration (either ER or FD), one of the following observed paths occurs (these are exclusive alternatives in a single trace; some alternatives are rare but observed):
   - Path A: Proceed to Expert Examination
     - Register at ER -> Expert Examination (observed frequently)
     - Register at FD -> Expert Examination (observed frequently)
   - Path B: Proceed to Examination (regular clinical exam)
     - Register at ER -> Examination
     - Register at FD -> Examination
   - Path C: Skip Examination(s) and proceed directly toward the later exam/diagnosis flow
     - The Petri net contains silent "skip" transitions that allow skipping steps; the effect is that some registrations directly enable later steps (this is reflected in the variants where registration can go directly to Diagnosis or to Thorough Examination). These skips are observed but less frequent.
   - Path D (rare): Register at ER/FD -> Thorough Examination (directly) or Register -> Diagnosis (directly). These direct jumps exist (low frequency) in the observed traces.

3. Examination block (detailed branching after registration)
   Observed behavior after registration:
   - If Examination (regular) occurs:
     - From Examination there are two alternatives:
       - Examination -> Thorough Examination (most common)
       - Examination -> skip-thorough (i.e., skip Thorough Examination) then proceed directly toward Diagnosis
   - If Expert Examination occurs:
     - From Expert Examination there are two alternatives:
       - Expert Examination -> Thorough Examination
       - Expert Examination -> Diagnosis (skip Thorough Examination)
   - Thorough Examination can be reached either after Examination or after Expert Examination, or (rarely) directly after registration.

4. Diagnosis
   - Diagnosis occurs after the examination/thorough-examination/ expert examination or, in some traces, directly after registration.
   - Observed direct edges: Register at FD/ER -> Diagnosis (rare) and Expert Examination -> Diagnosis (frequent).
   - In all main variants, Diagnosis is the step immediately before Treatment (except rare traces where Treatment is skipped? — see Treatment->Discharge path below).

5. Treatment and outcomes (core loop and variants)
   - Standard progression: Diagnosis -> Treatment.
   - After Treatment, three observed outcomes are possible (all observed in the data):
     A. Treatment -> Treatment successful -> Discharge
        - This is the most common and “successful” branch.
     B. Treatment -> Treatment unsuccessful -> Diagnosis (rework loop) -> Treatment -> ...
        - When treatment is unsuccessful, the case returns to Diagnosis and then Treatment is attempted again. This pattern may repeat multiple times.
        - Observed behavior includes 1, 2, 3 and even up to 4 consecutive unsuccessful-treatment loops before eventual success in the data.
     C. Treatment -> Discharge (direct), without an intermediate recorded Treatment successful event
        - A subset of traces go directly from Treatment to Discharge (observed frequency ≈ 995 in DFG). These cases do not have an explicit "Treatment successful" activity logged but nonetheless end in Discharge.

   - Important: All three outcomes are valid observed endings from Treatment. Thus the process must allow:
     - immediate successful outcome,
     - unsuccessful outcome(s) leading to re-diagnosis and re-treatment (rework loops), and
     - direct discharge after treatment without explicit success event.

6. Rework loops and their limits (observed)
   - The data contains multiple observed loops of the pattern:
       Treatment -> Treatment unsuccessful -> Diagnosis -> Treatment
     - This loop can repeat. Observed maximum depth in the provided variants is four consecutive Treatment unsuccessful → Diagnosis → Treatment cycles before a Treatment successful → Discharge occurs. (i.e., up to 4 unsuccessful repeats observed).
   - There are many traces with one unsuccessful repetition, and a decreasing number with 2, 3 or 4 repetitions. All of these are valid as-is.

7. End condition(s)
   - The only final activity in observed traces is Discharge.
   - Discharge can be reached from:
     - Treatment successful -> Discharge (typical)
     - Treatment -> Discharge (direct)
     - (the Petri net shows Discharge as the final sink connected only from a place that is reached after Treatment successful or skip paths)
   - Therefore, the business meaning of the end state: the patient case is closed and the patient is released from care for this episode.

6) Decision points, gateways and criteria (explicitly describing all observed options)
- At registration (exclusive choice; one of the two must occur):
  - Register at ER OR Register at FD. Both are mutually exclusive starting activities in a trace.
- Post-registration examination choice (exclusive alternatives per trace, but both options exist overall):
  - After registration the case may go to:
    - Examination
    - Expert Examination
    - Direct/rarely to Thorough Examination or Diagnosis (skip)
  - Criteria: The models do not include explicit business rules; the observed choice is data-driven (likely triage/complexity or documented by staff). We do not invent rules — only note that both routes occur in practice.
- After Examination/Expert Examination:
  - Either proceed to Thorough Examination OR proceed to Diagnosis (skip thorough) — both observed.
- After Diagnosis -> Treatment:
  - After each Treatment there is a decision/outcome:
    - Treatment successful (observed and typically leads to Discharge)
    - Treatment unsuccessful (observed and leads back to Diagnosis; rework loop)
    - Treatment -> Discharge (observed direct discharge without recorded success)
- Loop repeat decision:
  - After a Treatment unsuccessful -> Diagnosis -> Treatment sequence, the outcome decision is evaluated again (i.e., further unsuccessful or successful). This continues until a path to Discharge is taken (success or direct discharge).

7) Parallelism and synchronization
- No parallel (concurrent) activities are observed in the models. All observed flows are sequential choices and loops; there are no forks that create concurrent interleaving of tasks in the given data.

8) Silent/invisible transitions and skips (interpretation)
- The Petri net contains several invisible transitions (skip_1, skip_2, skip_5, skip_6, init_loop_3). Observed implications:
  - skip_1, skip_2, skip_5, skip_6 model optional omission of activities (e.g., skipping Examination → going on, skipping Thorough Examination, skipping Treatment successful step etc.). In the AS-IS description these map to observed direct jumps (e.g., registration directly to diagnosis, treatment directly to discharge).
  - init_loop_3 models re-entry into diagnosis after unsuccessful treatment (the rework loop).
- We do not treat these as new business activities — they represent optional flows and loop mechanics.

9) All observed valid orderings and patterns (exhaustive coverage - grammar-like summary)
- Every valid observed trace starts with:
  - [Register at ER | Register at FD]
- Then zero or one of the following exam flows (exclusive per trace, in observed variants):
  - [Examination] optionally followed by [Thorough Examination] or skip to Diagnosis
  - [Expert Examination] optionally followed by [Thorough Examination] or skip to Diagnosis
  - [Thorough Examination] directly (rare) or skip to Diagnosis
  - [Diagnosis] directly (rare)
- Then:
  - Diagnosis -> Treatment
- After Treatment, one of the following is observed (all permitted):
  - Treatment -> Treatment successful -> Discharge
  - Treatment -> Discharge (no Treatment successful recorded)
  - Treatment -> Treatment unsuccessful -> Diagnosis -> Treatment -> (repeat treatment outcome decision)
- The "Treatment unsuccessful -> Diagnosis -> Treatment" cycle may repeat; observed repeats: 1,2,3,4 times in traces.
- Thus all traces are sequences that can be described by the regular pattern:
  - Register@{ER|FD} -> (Examination | Expert Examination | Thorough Examination | Diagnosis) -> (maybe Thorough Examination) -> Diagnosis -> Treatment -> [ (Treatment successful -> Discharge) | (Treatment -> Discharge) | ((Treatment unsuccessful -> Diagnosis -> Treatment) repeated N times, then Treatment successful -> Discharge) ]
  - N observed up to 4.

11) Exception handling and undesirable behaviors observed
- Frequent rework: Treatment unsuccessful → re-diagnosis → re-treatment is a common exception/rework. This causes long case durations and many variants.
- Missing/ambiguous recording of treatment outcome: some traces go Treatment -> Discharge without Treatment successful. This inconsistency in logging makes outcome analysis and KPIs ambiguous.
- Optional/skipped clinical steps: Examination and Thorough Examination are frequently skipped in some traces (direct jumps to Diagnosis), likely reducing standardization of clinical work-up.
- Multiple alternative paths between registration and diagnosis (ER vs FD; Examination vs Expert Examination vs direct diagnosis) increase variability in care and may mask noncompliance with intended clinical guidelines.

12) Rework loops and escalation procedures (observed only; no formal escalation recorded)
- Observed rework mechanism: when Treatment unsuccessful occurs, the case returns to Diagnosis (Petri net’s init_loop_3 models this). There is no explicit "escalation" activity in the observed models; Expert Examination is an earlier choice (not explicitly triggered by an unsuccessful treatment in the recorded variants). In practice, Expert Examination sometimes occurs early (after registration) and sometimes not at all; there is no observed systematic escalation after treatment failure in the provided logs.
- Multiple unsuccessful treatment cycles were observed; no special activity (e.g., "refer to specialist") was logged in the sequences provided.

13) All observed variants summary (how the model accepts all traces)
- The AS-IS model accepts any trace that:
  - Starts with Register at ER or Register at FD
  - Proceeds with zero or one of Examination or Expert Examination (Thorough Examination optional after either), or occasionally jumps directly to Diagnosis
  - Has Diagnosis -> Treatment
  - From Treatment either:
    - finishes with Treatment successful -> Discharge, or
    - goes Treatment unsuccessful -> Diagnosis -> Treatment (repeatable N times, observed N up to 4), or
    - goes directly Treatment -> Discharge (no Treatment successful logged)
  - Ends with Discharge
- This description is intentionally permissive: every variant in the provided ABSTRACT_VARIANTS listing and every path visible in the Petri net and DFG is accepted.

14) Key challenges, pain points and operational impacts (observed)
- High process variability:
  - Two registration points and multiple clinical routes (expert vs regular exam; optional thorough exam) create many variants, reducing standardization.
- Rework loops (treatment failures) are frequent and costly:
  - Treatment unsuccessful cases (1,746 direct occurrences) create repeated diagnostic and treatment cycles; worst-case observed loops up to 4 unsuccessful cycles dramatically increase case duration.
- Inconsistent logging of outcomes:
  - Treatment -> Discharge occurrences (≈995) indicate either implicit success not recorded or premature discharge without recording success; this poses measurement and compliance problems.
- Long and variable lead times:
  - Performance numbers indicate wide variance; complex traces (many loops) have much larger total performance values (some variant totals in the hundreds of thousands of reported units).
- Rare but existing direct jumps (registration -> diagnosis or registration -> thorough exam) suggest deviations from expected clinical pathways and possible under-documentation of exams.

15) Recommendations for analysts/readers of this AS-IS model
- When analyzing or improving this process, consider:
  - Focusing on reducing Treatment unsuccessful occurrences (improve diagnosis accuracy, review treatment protocols) to reduce rework.
  - Standardizing decision rules for selecting Expert vs regular Examination to reduce variability.
  - Clarifying logging rules so that Treatment successful is consistently recorded or the log-spec is corrected to avoid ambiguous Treatment->Discharge traces.
  - Investigating the small but non-zero frequency of registration → direct diagnosis or registration → direct thorough exam, to determine whether these are valid shortcuts or missing logs.

16) Final note on fidelity
- This AS-IS description intentionally preserves all observed behaviors from the provided Petri net, DFG and abstract variants:
  - It permits every registration route, every observed examination alternative, every inspected direct jump, every Treatment outcome (successful, unsuccessful with rework, direct discharge), and repeated unsuccessful-treatment loops (observed up to 4 times).
  - Any trace matching one of the ABSTRACT_VARIANTS or the DFG edges described above is accepted by this model as a valid observed execution.
