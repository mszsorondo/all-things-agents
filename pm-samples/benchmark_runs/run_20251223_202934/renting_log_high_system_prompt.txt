Business Process Description:
AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- This process describes the observed end-to-end tenant acquisition and tenancy lifecycle as discovered by process mining (inductive miner Petri net, directly-follows graph and abstract variants). It covers: prospective tenant actions (apply for viewing), leasing actions (set appointment, view property), application submission (hand in paperwork), pre-hire checks (check paperwork, screen prospective tenant, optional extensive screening), contracting (sign contract, move in) and post-move tenancy activities (pay rent, missed payments -> warnings -> accept late payment or eviction, tenant cancellations).
- Purpose (as observed): to evaluate applicants, onboard accepted tenants, collect rent and handle non-payment and cancellations. The discovered model reflects real operational deviations, optional bypasses, repeated payment cycles and exception paths (rejections, evictions, cancellations).

Key stakeholders and roles involved (inferred from activities)
- Prospective Tenant (applicant): applies for viewing, attends viewing, hands in paperwork, may cancel before moving in.
- Leasing Agent / Property Staff: sets appointments, shows property, collects paperwork, signs contracts (agent involvement implied by contract & appointments).
- Screening Team / Underwriting: checks paperwork, performs screening and (when needed) extensive screening, issues rejection or moves forward to contract.
- Finance / Accounts: records payments, processes late payments and tracks missed payments; issues warnings and records acceptances of late payments.
- Property Manager / Legal (escalation): issues eviction when non-payment persists; records tenant cancellation and termination.
- Note: roles are inferred from activity names; the model did not explicitly name swimlanes in the source but responsibilities are typical and consistent with the activity semantics.

Detailed step-by-step process flow (AS-IS observed behavior)
Start conditions and triggers
1. Start event: prospective tenant initiates the process by applying for a viewing appointment (Apply for Viewing Appointment). This is the primary observed start.

Main sequence and branches (frequent observed flows)
1. Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Set Appointment
   - Frequency (DFG): 9151 cases follow this directly.
   - Trigger: applicant requests viewing (web form/phone/email). Leasing Agent schedules appointment (Set Appointment).
2. Set Appointment -> View The Property
   - Frequency: 9151.
   - Outcome: applicant attends a property viewing (View The Property).
3. View The Property -> Hand In The Paperwork OR -> Reject Prospective Tenant
   - Frequent continuation: 7441 attendees hand in paperwork; 1710 cases end in rejection right after viewing (candidate not suitable based on first impression; immediate rejection).
4. Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork
   - Frequency: 7441.
   - Check Paperwork activity validates documents, completeness and eligibility criteria.
5. Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant
   - Frequency: 7441.
   - Screening assesses background, references, income, credit checks.
6. Screen Prospective Tenant -> {Reject Prospective Tenant, Extensive Screening, Sign Contract}
   - Observed splits: many applicants are rejected after screening (2856); many are routed to Extensive Screening (2793) for additional verification; some pass directly to Sign Contract (1792).
   - Criteria observed (implicit in data): immediate reject for failing eligibility; extensive screening when there is some risk/uncertainty; sign contract when screening returns clear pass.
7. Extensive Screening -> {Reject Prospective Tenant, Sign Contract}
   - Frequencies: 1815 rejected after extensive screening; 978 proceed to sign contract after extensive screening.
8. Sign Contract -> Move In
   - Frequency: 2770 proceed to Move In after signing.
9. Move In -> Pay Rent
   - After move-in tenants enter the rent-payment lifecycle. Many cases then repeatedly execute Pay Rent (Move In -> Pay Rent frequency 2656; DFG shows Pay Rent self-loop is very frequent: 14819 occurrences in the log overall), indicating recurring payments (monthly rent cycles) and sometimes multiple payments in trace variants.

Post-move decision/exception flow (payments and non-payment)
1. Pay Rent -> Miss Rent Payment
   - Observed: Pay Rent -> Miss Rent Payment (770 cases) indicates tenants occasionally miss a rent due date.
2. Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning (945 cases)
   - Finance or property staff issue a formal warning to the tenant when rent is missed.
3. Issue Warning -> {Accept Late Payment, Evict Tenant}
   - Most often (855 cases) Issue Warning is followed by Accept Late Payment (tenant pays after warning); a small number (90) escalate directly to eviction (Issue Warning -> Evict Tenant).
4. Accept Late Payment -> {Pay Rent, Miss Rent Payment}
   - Accept Late Payment -> Pay Rent (794) indicates warnings are followed by payment and tenant resumes normal payments.
   - Accept Late Payment -> Miss Rent Payment (61) shows reoffending after a late payment acceptance, i.e., tenant misses a future payment even after a late pay was accepted.
5. Evict Tenant -> End (sink)
   - Eviction is a terminal outcome when non-payment is severe or repeated.
6. Tenant Cancels Appartment -> End (sink)
   - Some tenants cancel the apartment (e.g., withdraw application before moving in, or terminate tenancy after move-in). Direct transitions from Pay Rent to Tenant Cancels Apartment (2680 cases) indicate cancellations occurring after payments have been made—likely resignations or move-outs.

Optional bypasses and invisible transitions (observed in the Petri net)
- The inductive miner model contains several 'skip' / invisible transitions that allow optional bypassing of particular stages (skip_x arcs). Observed behavior includes:
  - Some applicants are rejected directly after Apply for Viewing Appointment (apply -> reject: 849 cases), bypassing appointment/viewing entirely.
  - Some applicants skip extensive screening (skip from screening place to sign contract), indicating ad-hoc bypasses where formal extensive screening is not executed even though the model allows it.
  - Move In is followed by an internal loop (init_loop_7) that leads back into the payment/miss-payment cycle; this models repeated monthly rent behavior and the occasional re-entry into missed payment handling.

Variant-level observations
- Abstract variants show many cases where tenants make multiple consecutive Pay Rent events (2..n times) before cancellation. There are many long-tail variants with many repeats of Pay Rent before Tenant Cancels Apartment; this is consistent with tenants paying multiple months and then eventually cancelling (moving out).
- Variants also show a sizable number of applicants who are rejected at different stages (apply->reject, view->reject, screening->reject) and a significant number who proceed through screening to contract and move-in.

End conditions and outcomes
- Observed terminal outcomes in the mined model (sink) are: Reject Prospective Tenant, Tenant Cancels Apartment, Evict Tenant. Some traces may remain open (ongoing tenants with repeated Pay Rent events) but the log's final marking points to sink reached by these end activities.

Key challenges and pain points (AS-IS)
- High manual effort in screening: large share of applicants require extensive screening (2,793) or are rejected after screening (2,856), indicating time-consuming checks and high manual workload for screening team.
- Optional/inconsistent execution: presence of multiple skip/invisible transitions suggests ad-hoc bypasses (e.g., skipping extensive screening or skipping viewing) that reduce process standardization and increase risk.
- Long cycle times: several transitions show large performance numbers — scheduling, viewing and screening delays increase time-to-decision and time-to-move-in.
- Payment handling instability: a non-negligible number of missed payments (770 leading into warning flows) and reoffending after late payment acceptance (61) create friction and risk; eviction is rare (90) but costly.
- Fragmented decision rules: multiple routes to rejection at different stages (apply, view, screening, extensive screening) indicate inconsistent decision criteria and potential waste: applicants may progress through several steps only to be rejected late in the process.
- Rework and repeated manual interactions: frequent Pay Rent self-loops and variants with many repeated payments indicate long-running live tenancies but also create bookkeeping volume. Accept Late Payment is used often as an ad-hoc remediation rather than a standardized automated rule.

Summary of AS-IS behavior (concise)
- The mined process starts when an applicant applies for a viewing and typically proceeds through appointment, viewing, paperwork, checks and screening. From screening the applicant is either rejected, routed to extensive screening or progresses to sign a contract and move in. After moving in there is a recurring rent-payment cycle with occasional misses leading to warnings, accepted late payments or eviction. The model contains many optional skips and long variants with multiple pay events before cancellation.


IDEAL Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- The IDEAL model is a consolidated, streamlined and rules-driven version of the observed process. It preserves the visible activities from the AS-IS model but imposes clear sequencing, standardized decision criteria, automation and SLAs to reduce manual effort, limit rework and reduce the incidence of missed payments and evictions.
- Purpose: enable faster, consistent applicant evaluation; reduce time-to-lease and manual workload; increase on-time rent payments and reduce evictions through early prevention and standardized remediation.

Key stakeholders and roles (aligned with AS-IS)
- Prospective Tenant (applicant)
- Leasing Agent / Property Staff (scheduling, showings, contracts)
- Screening Team (automated + manual risk-based screening rules)
- Finance / Accounts (payments, automated reminders, late-fee processing)
- Property Manager / Legal (eviction as last resort)

Detailed step-by-step process flow (IDEAL)
Start conditions and triggers
1. Start: Prospective tenant submits an application or requests a viewing (Apply for Viewing Appointment). The system validates minimal application completeness and candidate eligibility automatically before booking.

Standardized applicant path (primary flow)
1. Automated Pre-Screening (new step implemented as automation of existing checks)
   - Immediately after Apply for Viewing Appointment, an automated pre-screen runs basic eligibility rules (income threshold, blacklists, previously evicted tenants). If the applicant fails pre-screen, they receive an immediate, documented rejection (fast-fail) to avoid wasted appointments (this covers AS-IS apply->reject cases but makes the decision earlier and consistent).
   - Metric target: reduce unnecessary appointments / no-shows and reduce late rejections by at least 50%.
2. Set Appointment (conditional)
   - Only applicants that pass the automated pre-screen are booked for an in-person or virtual viewing.
   - SLA: appointment scheduled within 48 hours of application.
3. View The Property
   - Leasing Agent conducts the viewing. Agents use a checklist to capture first impressions and flag immediate fails (but do not issue final rejections without screening results).
4. Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork (automated + manual hybrid)
   - Paperwork is uploaded electronically. An automated document validator checks completeness and authenticity; any missing documents trigger an automated request with a 48-hour SLA for completion.
5. Risk-Based Screening (replace/streamline Screen Prospective Tenant & Extensive Screening)
   - Implement a two-tier screening approach: automated screening uses credit, income verification and reference checks. If automated screening returns clear pass -> Sign Contract. If risk indicators exist (marginal credit, conflicting references) -> trigger an explicit Extensive Screening human review.
   - This consolidates the two-step AS-IS screening into a deterministic, auditable decision with clear criteria for escalating to Extensive Screening.
   - SLA: automated screening completes within 4 hours; manual extensive reviews complete within 48 hours.
6. Decision outcomes after screening
   - Reject Prospective Tenant: issued only when automated or manual screening fails pre-defined fail rules; rejections are documented with reason codes and appeal routes.
   - Sign Contract: when screening passes and paperwork is complete, an electronic contract is sent for signature (e-signature). The process enforces a security deposit and first-month rent before Move In (policy change to protect finance).
7. Move In
   - Move In occurs after preconditions are met (signed contract, deposit + first rent processed). Leasing Agent confirms move-in and activates tenant in the payment system.

Post-move payment lifecycle (standardized)
1. Recurring Pay Rent cycle
   - Rent payments are automated where possible (direct debit recommended). The system logs each rent payment (Pay Rent) and uses a scheduled reminder sequence to reduce misses.
2. Missed Payment -> Automated Reminder -> Late Fee & Warning -> Escalation Ladder
   - Immediately upon a missed payment, the system sends an automated reminder (day 1), and after 3 days a formal warning and late fee notice is issued. If payment occurs in the defined cure window (e.g., within 7 days), the system records Accept Late Payment and returns the tenant to the normal cycle.
   - If the tenant misses payment again within a subsequent window or fails to cure multiple times (e.g., 3 or more missed payments within 6 months), the system triggers Property Manager escalation for remediation (payment plan, mediation) and only proceeds to eviction after documented attempts to cure and legal checks.
   - Eviction remains an available outcome but is a controlled, low-frequency activity after standardized escalation.
3. Tenant Cancels Apartment (move-out)
   - Tenant-initiated cancellations are handled via a documented termination flow; early terminations follow contract rules (notice period, deposit accounting). Tenants who cancel before full contract execution are rejected with documentation of cancellation reason.

Optional/parallel activities
- Parallel: while automated screening runs, the document completeness validator and lease-preparation tasks can run in parallel to reduce total lead time.
- Synchronization: contract issuance waits for both screening pass and completed paperwork; Move In waits for signed contract + payment confirmation.

Exception handling, rework loops and escalation procedures (improvements)
- Single source of truth: all rejections and escalation steps are logged with reason codes to avoid rework and inconsistent rejections.
- Appeals: applicants who believe they were wrongly rejected may trigger a secondary review (explicit, auditable manual step) but only on documented grounds.
- Repeated late payments: instead of ad-hoc Accept Late Payment, automated policy applies consistent penalties and offers payment plans; manual acceptance of late payment is permitted only in documented exception cases.
- Eviction: only triggered after automated reminders, warning, documented failed cure attempts and a Property Manager/Legal review. The goal is to reduce eviction occurrences while protecting landlord revenue via consistent deposits and pre-move financial checks.

End conditions and outcomes (IDEAL)
- Terminal outcomes: Reject Prospective Tenant (fast-fail pre-screen or post-screen), Successful Move In (tenant onboarded and payment plan active), Tenant Cancels Apartment (handled per contract), Controlled Eviction (rare, legal-reviewed process).

Process metrics and target performance characteristics (IDEAL targets)
- Screening time: automated pre-screen within 4 hours; manual extensive screening within 48 hours (target).
- Time-to-lease (Apply -> Move In): target average reduced by 30–50% vs AS-IS by running parallel checks and automating validation.
- Missed payment rate: reduce missed payment incidents by 40–60% using automated reminders, direct-debit incentives, and consistent late-fee policy.
- Eviction rate: reduce by >50% by introducing early interventions, payment plans and requiring deposit + first-month rent before Move In.
- Rejection latency: move many apply->reject decisions to immediate automated pre-screen to reduce wasted showings by at least 50%.
- Paperwork completeness: target 90% first-pass acceptance via automated document validation.

Improvements made compared to AS-IS (summary)
- Standardized decision criteria: clear pass/fail rules for screening and explicit criteria for escalating to extensive screening reduce ad-hoc bypasses and inconsistent rejections.
- Automation and parallelization: automated pre-screen, document validation and e-signature reduce manual delays and shorten time-to-decision.
- Consolidation of screening steps: risk-based screening reduces unnecessary extensive screening and concentrates manual effort on genuinely risky cases.
- Payment stabilization: automated reminders, mandatory deposit and first rent, and tiered escalation for missed payments reduce reoffending and evictions.
- Auditable exceptions: all skip/bypass behavior from AS-IS is removed or converted to documented exception workflows requiring approval, ensuring process governance.

Operational governance and next steps for implementation
- Define precise rule thresholds for automated pre-screen (income ratios, credit score thresholds, blacklist rules).
- Configure the property management system to support automated document checks, direct debit, e-signatures, and automated reminders.
- Train Leasing and Screening teams on the new SLAs and the new exception approval mechanism.
- Monitor key KPIs (time-to-lease, missed-payment rate, eviction count, percentage of automated-screen passes) weekly for the first 3 months and adjust thresholds based on measured performance.

Concluding remark
- The IDEAL model retains all visible activities from the mined process but organizes them into a governed, automated, and auditable flow that eliminates ad-hoc skips, reduces manual workload and significantly reduces the business risk associated with late payments and evictions.
