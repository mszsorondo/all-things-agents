Business Process Description:
# AS-IS Process Model

## Process Overview and Purpose
The current AS-IS process model describes the recruitment workflow from the initial application submission to either a job offer or an application rejection. It captures the series of interviews and checks candidates undergo, revealing inefficiencies and areas of non-compliance that impact the overall recruitment speed and candidate experience.

## Key Stakeholders and Roles Involved
- **Candidates**: Individuals applying for the job.
- **Recruiters**: Responsible for initial screenings and managing the interview process.
- **Interviewers**: Employees who evaluate candidates during interviews (e.g., behavioral, technical).
- **HR Managers**: Oversee the recruitment process and make final decisions.

## Detailed Step-by-Step Process Flow
1. **Start Condition**: The process starts when a candidate **Hand In Job Application**.

2. **Application Processing**: Candidates undergo a series of screenings:
   - **Telephonic Screening** (step 2)
   - Options to move to a **Screening Interview** or directly to **Application Rejected**.

3. **Screening Interview**: Candidates who pass telephonic screening can:
   - Proceed to **Background Check**.
   - **Feedback loop**: If not advanced from the screening interview, candidates may return for another screening iteration or get rejected.

4. **Background Check**: This step includes extensive checks:
   - Candidates can either be moved to a **Coding Interview**,
   - Expected to complete a **Behavioral Interview**.
   - Possible outcomes include **Application Rejected** if checks fail.

5. **Coding Interview**: Depending on the background check results,
   - Candidates progress to **Frontend Interview** or **Backend Interview**. 
   - Each interview type can lead to a final offer or rejection as detailed.

6. **Final Decision**: Based on interview performances, candidates may either:
   - Receive a **Make Job Offer**,
   - Get **Application Rejected**.

7. **End Conditions**: The process concludes with either successful hiring or multiple rejection points.

## Decision Points and Criteria
- **Rejections** can occur at various stages (coding, screening, etc.), often triggered by performance evaluations or checks.
- **Feedback loops** exist in the screening phase, allowing candidates to reattempt the interview process if applicable.

## Parallel Activities and Synchronization Points
- Candidates can undergo multiple parallel interviews (e.g., Frontend and Backend) before the final decision, representing a **Merge** where multiple interview results contribute to a single outcome.

## Exception Handling and Error Paths
- If a background check or interview fails, candidates can be routed directly to **Application Rejected**, creating a need for clear communication of feedback.

## Rework Loops and Escalation Procedures
- Candidates might undergo multiple screenings or interviews due to initial failures, creating an inefficient rework system that delays hiring.

## Process Metrics and Performance Characteristics
- The process shows high frequencies of rejections, showcasing areas for improvement. Performance metrics reveal long times in interviews and background checks, significantly affecting candidate throughput and experience.

## Key Challenges and Pain Points
- Frequent application rejections lead to candidate dissatisfaction.
- Extended timelines due to the number of interviews create bottlenecks.
- Lack of clarity in communication regarding application status and next steps.
