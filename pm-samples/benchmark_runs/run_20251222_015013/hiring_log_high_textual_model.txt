AS-IS Process Model

# Process Overview and Purpose

The AS-IS recruitment process represents the current state of candidate screening and hiring within the organization. The process begins when a candidate submits a job application and culminates in either a job offer being extended or the application being rejected. The process is characterized by significant variability in execution paths, multiple rework loops, premature rejections, and inefficiencies including redundant background checks and chaotic interview sequencing.

# Key Stakeholders and Roles Involved

- **Candidates**: Individuals applying for positions within the organization
- **Recruiting Team**: Responsible for initial telephonic screening and coordinating the overall process
- **HR Department**: Conducts screening interviews and manages background checks
- **Technical Interviewers**: Conduct specialized interviews (Frontend, Backend, ML, Coding)
- **Hiring Managers**: Conduct behavioral interviews
- **Background Check Service Providers**: Perform standard and extensive background verification
- **Decision Makers**: Final authority on job offers and rejections

# Detailed Step-by-Step Process Flow

## Start Conditions and Triggers

**Start Event**: The process is triggered when a candidate submits a job application to the organization.

**Initial Activity**: Hand In Job Application
- Candidate completes and submits their application through the organization's application system
- All 8,799 observed cases begin with this activity
- Average time to next activity: ~82,440 seconds (approximately 23 hours)

## Process Activities in Sequence

### Phase 1: Initial Screening (Highly Variable)

**Activity 1: Telephonic Screening (Optional)**
- Frequency: Occurs in 8,799 cases, but 1,201 applications are rejected immediately without screening
- Purpose: Initial phone-based assessment of candidate qualifications
- Average duration to next step: ~81,906 seconds
- **Critical Issue**: 990 candidates are rejected immediately after telephonic screening without further evaluation

**Decision Point A: Post-Application Routing**
The process exhibits chaotic branching after application submission:
- Path 1 (Primary): Proceed to Telephonic Screening (8,799 cases)
- Path 2 (Premature): Direct rejection without any screening (1,201 cases - 13.6% of applications)
- **Issue**: No clear criteria documented for immediate rejection vs. screening

### Phase 2: Background Verification (Disordered Sequence)

**Activity 2a: Background Check**
- Frequency: 3,961 cases
- **Critical Flaw**: Can occur BEFORE or AFTER screening interview
- Timing variations:
  - Sometimes follows Telephonic Screening directly
  - Sometimes follows Screening Interview
  - Creates duplicate work when sequencing is wrong

**Activity 2b: Screening Interview**
- Frequency: 3,848 cases proceed through screening interview
- Purpose: In-depth assessment of candidate qualifications and cultural fit
- **Major Issue**: Order with Background Check is reversed in approximately 50% of cases
- 1,389 cases show pattern: Telephonic → Background → Screening → Rejection
- 1,391 cases show pattern: Telephonic → Screening → Background → Rejection

**Decision Point B: Extensive Background Check Trigger**
- Condition: Some candidates (1,055 from Screening Interview path, 1,044 from Background Check path) are routed to Extensive Background Check
- **Issue**: No clear criteria for when extensive vs. standard background check is required
- Extensive Background Check adds significant time: average 84,234-84,418 seconds additional processing

**Activity 2c: Extensive Background Check**
- Frequency: 2,099 candidates total
- Purpose: More thorough background investigation (reasons unclear from process)
- **Critical Issue**: 1,125 candidates are rejected after completing this expensive activity
- Performance: Average 171,097 seconds before rejection decision

**Rework Loop A: Background Check Repetition**
- The process shows candidates bouncing between Background Check and Screening Interview
- This creates unnecessary delays and duplicated effort
- No clear resolution mechanism documented

### Phase 3: Technical Assessment (Chaotic Interview Sequencing)

**Activity 3a: Coding Interview**
- Frequency: 1,517 cases from Screening Interview; 1,413 from Background Check; 974 from Extensive Background Check
- Total: Approximately 2,904 candidates reach coding interview
- Purpose: Assess programming and problem-solving capabilities
- Average time to next activity: 80,785-129,892 seconds (highly variable)
- **Issue**: 386 candidates rejected immediately after Coding Interview

**Decision Point C: Technical Interview Paths (Parallel/Optional - Poorly Controlled)**
Following Coding Interview, candidates may proceed to multiple technical interviews in various sequences:

**Activity 3b: Frontend Interview**
- Frequency: 1,513 cases directly from Coding Interview
- Can also occur after ML Interview (878 cases) or Backend Interview (487 cases)
- **Major Issue**: 653 candidates rejected after Frontend Interview
- Creates circular patterns with ML Interview (913 forward, 878 backward)

**Activity 3c: ML Interview**
- Frequency: 1,304 cases from Coding Interview
- Frequently interleaved with Frontend Interview in chaotic patterns
- Can precede or follow Frontend Interview (no standard sequence)
- **Issue**: 546 candidates rejected after ML Interview
- Circular loop with Frontend Interview suggests rework or indecision

**Activity 3d: Backend Interview**
- Frequency: 701 cases from Coding Interview
- Less frequently used than other technical interviews
- Shows circular pattern with Frontend Interview (529 forward, 487 backward)
- Can also receive candidates from ML Interview (152 backward, 121 forward)
- **Issue**: 295 candidates rejected after Backend Interview

**Rework Loop B: Technical Interview Cycling**
- Candidates cycle between Frontend ↔ ML Interview repeatedly
- Backend Interview sometimes inserted mid-cycle
- No documented criteria for when cycling stops
- Creates significant delays (interviews are spread across weeks)
- Pattern suggests indecisive hiring committees or unclear role requirements

### Phase 4: Behavioral Assessment (With Rejection Interference)

**Activity 4: Behavioral Interview**
- Frequency: Core path shows 853 from Frontend, 754 from ML, 417 from Backend
- **Critical Anomaly**: 740 cases show rejected candidates proceeding to Behavioral Interview
- Purpose: Assess cultural fit, communication skills, and soft skills
- **Severe Issue**: Self-loop detected with 1,032 instances of repeated Behavioral Interviews
- Average loop time: 47 seconds (suggests system error or multiple interview rounds not properly modeled)

**Rework Loop C: Behavioral Interview Repetition**
- 1,032 cases show Behavioral Interview repeating
- Could indicate:
  - Multiple behavioral rounds with different interviewers
  - System logging errors creating duplicate records
  - Candidates being re-interviewed due to inconclusive results
- **Major Process Issue**: 914 candidates rejected after completing behavioral interview stage

**Decision Point D: Post-Behavioral Routing (Broken)**
After Behavioral Interview:
- Path 1: Make Job Offer (1,850 cases - intended path)
- Path 2: Application Rejected (914 cases - waste of significant resources)
- **Critical Flaw**: No documented decision criteria; high rejection rate after extensive investment

### Phase 5: Final Decision (Chaotic Terminal States)

**Activity 5a: Make Job Offer**
- Frequency: 1,850 primary cases reach job offer stage
- **Anomaly**: 690 additional offers made after Application Rejected status
- **Severe Issue**: Self-loop showing 861 instances of repeated job offers
- Average loop time: 70.746 seconds
- This suggests:
  - Offer renegotiations
  - System errors in recording
  - Multiple offer letters being sent
  - Candidates declining and counter-offers being extended

**Activity 5b: Application Rejected**
- Appears as terminal state for majority of applications
- **Critical Issues**:
  - Can occur at ANY stage of the process
  - 654 cases show self-loop (Application Rejected → Application Rejected)
  - Average self-loop time: 9,901 seconds (2.75 hours)
  - Suggests multiple rejection notifications or appeals process
  - 740 cases show rejected candidates continuing to Behavioral Interview
  - 690 cases show rejected candidates eventually receiving job offers

**Rework Loop D: Rejection Reversal**
- Pattern shows: Application Rejected → Behavioral Interview → Make Job Offer
- Frequency: 740 cases enter behavioral interview after rejection
- Suggests:
  - Appeals process not formally documented
  - Manager overrides of rejection decisions
  - Errors in rejection processing
  - Reconsideration workflows triggered ad-hoc

## Exception Handling and Error Paths

### Exception 1: Premature Rejection
- 1,201 candidates rejected immediately after application submission
- No screening performed
- No documented criteria
- Represents 13.6% of all applications

### Exception 2: Post-Screening Rejection
- 2,780 candidates (1,391 + 1,389) rejected after completing initial screening and background check
- Substantial time investment wasted (average 319,390-320,030 seconds = ~89 hours per candidate)
- High-frequency exception path indicates poor screening criteria

### Exception 3: Post-Extensive-Background-Check Rejection
- 1,125 candidates rejected after most expensive verification step
- Represents massive waste of resources
- Indicates background checks should occur earlier or not at all

### Exception 4: Mid-Technical-Interview Rejection
- 386 post-Coding, 653 post-Frontend, 546 post-ML, 295 post-Backend
- Total: 1,880+ rejections during technical phase
- Suggests inadequate earlier screening or unrealistic job requirements

### Exception 5: Rejection After Complete Process
- 914 rejections after Behavioral Interview (final stage before offer)
- Complete process waste for these candidates
- Average total time: 530,000+ seconds (6+ days of processing time)

### Exception 6: State Inconsistencies
- Application Rejected status does not consistently end the process
- 740 continue to interviews
- 690 eventually receive offers
- 654 receive multiple rejection notifications
- Indicates broken process state management

## Synchronization Points

**Merge Point 1: Pre-Background-Check Convergence**
- Multiple paths converge before background verification activities
- Includes: direct from Telephonic Screening, from Screening Interview
- No formal synchronization control; paths processed as they arrive

**Merge Point 2: Pre-Coding-Interview Convergence**
- Background Check, Screening Interview, and Extensive Background Check paths merge
- Timing highly variable (124,295 to 129,892 seconds average)
- No coordination mechanism visible

**Merge Point 3: Pre-Behavioral-Interview Convergence**
- Frontend, Backend, and ML interview paths should converge here
- **Critical Issue**: Also receives Application Rejected cases (740 instances)
- No proper synchronization; chaotic arrival patterns

**Merge Point 4: Terminal State Convergence**
- Both Make Job Offer and Application Rejected intended as final states
- **Issue**: States are not terminal; loops and cross-transitions exist
- Process sink not properly enforced

## End Conditions and Outcomes

### Outcome 1: Successful Hire (Job Offer Accepted)
- Frequency: Approximately 1,850 primary cases + 690 post-rejection offers = 2,540 potential offers
- Further reduced by 110 cases where offer leads back to rejection
- Net successful hires: approximately 2,430 candidates
- Average total process time: 530,000+ seconds (6.1+ days)
- Process completion rate: approximately 27.6% of applications result in offers

### Outcome 2: Application Rejected (Multiple Patterns)
- Total rejections: Highly variable across all stages
- Early rejection: 1,201 + 990 = 2,191 (immediate and post-phone-screen)
- Post-initial-screening: 2,780 candidates
- Post-extensive-background: 1,125 candidates
- Post-technical-interviews: 1,880+ candidates
- Post-behavioral: 914 candidates
- **Total rejection rate**: Approximately 72.4% of applications
- Many rejections after substantial time and resource investment

### Outcome 3: Process Abandonment (Inferred)
- Process variants show incomplete paths
- Some candidates likely drop out during long wait times
- Not explicitly tracked in current process model

## Process Metrics and Performance Characteristics

### Volume Metrics
- Total applications processed: 8,799 (base applications entering the system)
- Applications proceeding to telephonic screening: 8,799 (100%)
- Applications reaching background checks: 3,961 (45%)
- Applications reaching technical interviews: ~2,904 (33%)
- Applications reaching behavioral interviews: ~2,024 (23%)
- Job offers extended: ~2,540 (28.9%)
- Final rejections: ~6,369 (72.4%)

### Timing Metrics (Average Durations)
- Hand In Application → Telephonic Screening: 82,440 seconds (22.9 hours)
- Telephonic Screening → Background Check: 81,906 seconds (22.8 hours)
- Screening Interview → Coding Interview: 129,892 seconds (36.1 hours)
- Coding Interview → Frontend Interview: 80,785 seconds (22.4 hours)
- Technical Interviews → Behavioral Interview: 80,294 seconds (22.3 hours)
- Behavioral Interview → Make Job Offer: 80,430 seconds (22.3 hours)
- **Total average process time for successful candidates: ~530,000 seconds (6.1 days)**
- **Total average process time for rejected-after-screening: ~320,000 seconds (3.7 days)**

### Rework Metrics
- Behavioral Interview self-loops: 1,032 instances
- Make Job Offer self-loops: 861 instances
- Application Rejected self-loops: 654 instances
- Frontend ↔ ML Interview cycles: 1,791 transitions (913+878)
- Backend ↔ Frontend cycles: 1,016 transitions (529+487)
- Rejection reversals (rejected → behavioral → offer): 740 instances
- **Total rework instances: 5,894+ (representing significant wasted effort)**

### Quality Metrics
- Premature rejection rate: 13.6% (rejected without screening)
- Post-screening rejection rate: 31.6% (rejected after initial interviews)
- Late-stage rejection rate: 10.4% (rejected after behavioral interview)
- Process inconsistency rate: 16.8% (loops, reversals, state errors)
- Interview sequencing compliance: <50% (chaotic ordering)

### Resource Utilization Issues
- Extensive background checks performed on 2,099 candidates, 1,125 subsequently rejected (53.6% waste)
- Technical interviews conducted on ~2,904 candidates, 1,880+ subsequently rejected (64.7% waste)
- Behavioral interviews conducted with 914+ unnecessary rejections following
- Estimated interviewer time waste: thousands of hours across all rejection categories

## Key Challenges and Pain Points

### 1. Premature and Late Rejections
**Challenge**: Applications are rejected both too early (without proper evaluation) and too late (after extensive resource investment).
- 1,201 applications rejected without telephonic screening
- 2,780 rejected after initial screening/background check phase
- 914 rejected after completing all interviews
- No clear rejection criteria at any stage

**Impact**:
- Poor candidate experience
- Wasted recruiter and interviewer time
- Damage to employer brand
- High cost-per-hire due to process inefficiency

### 2. Chaotic Interview Sequencing
**Challenge**: Technical interviews (Frontend, ML, Backend) occur in random order with frequent cycling between them.
- No standard sequence defined
- 1,791 instances of Frontend ↔ ML cycling
- 1,016 instances of Backend ↔ Frontend cycling
- Candidates repeat interviews or wait for scheduling

**Impact**:
- Extended time-to-hire (6+ days average)
- Candidate frustration and dropout
- Interviewer scheduling conflicts
- Inconsistent evaluation standards

### 3. Background Check Placement Issues
**Challenge**: Background checks occur at inconsistent points in the process—sometimes before, sometimes after screening interviews.
- 50% of cases show reverse ordering
- Extensive background checks triggered without clear criteria
- 1,125 candidates rejected after expensive extensive checks

**Impact**:
- Unnecessary costs for background checks on unsuitable candidates
- Process delays while waiting for background results
- Compliance risks if checks not completed appropriately

### 4. Rework Loops and State Management Failures
**Challenge**: Multiple activities show self-loops and impossible state transitions.
- 1,032 Behavioral Interview repeats
- 861 Make Job Offer repeats
- 654 Application Rejected repeats
- 740 candidates continue interviewing after rejection
- 690 candidates receive offers after being rejected

**Impact**:
- Process tracking unreliability
- Confusion for candidates (conflicting communications)
- Unable to generate accurate metrics
- Potential legal/compliance issues
- System integrity questions

### 5. Lack of Clear Decision Criteria
**Challenge**: No documented rules for routing decisions at any process stage.
- When to skip telephonic screening
- When to require extensive vs. standard background check
- Which technical interviews are required for which roles
- When to reject vs. proceed to next stage
- When to reverse rejection decisions

**Impact**:
- Inconsistent candidate treatment
- Potential discrimination claims
- Manager/recruiter discretion leads to bias
- Unable to optimize or improve process
- Training new recruiters is difficult

### 6. Poor Process Governance
**Challenge**: Process allows contradictory states and lacks enforcement of proper sequencing.
- Rejected candidates continuing in process
- Multiple terminal states (offer and rejection) not mutually exclusive
- No role-based access controls evident
- No approval gates before key decisions

**Impact**:
- Process chaos and unpredictability
- High error rates
- Candidate experience suffers
- Difficulty in auditing decisions
- Compliance and legal exposure

### 7. Excessive Process Duration
**Challenge**: Average successful hiring takes 6.1 days of processing time, with multiple 22+ hour gaps between steps.
- Long wait times between each stage
- Top candidates likely accept other offers
- Process complexity creates delays

**Impact**:
- Loss of qualified candidates to competitors
- Extended time-to-fill for critical positions
- Increased cost per hire
- Business unit productivity suffers from vacancies

### 8. High Rejection Rate with Late Discovery
**Challenge**: 72.4% of applications are rejected, with many rejections occurring late in the process.
- Resources spent on candidates who will ultimately be rejected
- Poor screening criteria at early stages
- Interview panels not aligned on requirements

**Impact**:
- Massive resource waste (interviewer time, background check costs)
- Low recruiter productivity
- High cost-per-hire for successful candidates
- Interviewer fatigue and disengagement

### 9. Inadequate Telephonic Screening
**Challenge**: Telephonic screening fails to filter out unsuitable candidates effectively.
- 990 candidates rejected immediately after phone screen
- 2,780 more rejected shortly after (but after additional checks/interviews)
- Suggests phone screen adds little value

**Impact**:
- Waste of recruiter time conducting ineffective screens
- Unsuitable candidates progress too far into process
- Higher downstream interview loads

### 10. Technical Interview Panel Disagreement
**Challenge**: Cycling between technical interviews suggests lack of consensus on candidate suitability.
- Candidates shuttled between Frontend, ML, and Backend interviews
- Multiple rounds suggesting "get another opinion" pattern
- No clear stopping criteria

**Impact**:
- Extended candidate evaluation time
- Candidate frustration
- Interviewer time wasted on redundant assessments
- Lack of hiring manager accountability for decisions

This AS-IS process represents a severely dysfunctional recruitment workflow with systemic issues in sequencing, decision-making, state management, and resource efficiency. The process requires comprehensive redesign to eliminate waste, enforce proper sequencing, establish clear decision criteria, and improve candidate experience while reducing time-to-hire and cost-per-hire.
