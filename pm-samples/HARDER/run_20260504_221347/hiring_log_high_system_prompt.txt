You are an expert business process analyst. You will be given a business process description and a trace (sequence of activities).

Business Process Description:
AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Scope and goal: This process covers how a company receives and evaluates job applications, conducts screenings/interviews and background checks, and then either issues one or more job offers or records one or more rejections. The AS-IS reflects all behaviors observed in the event log, including optional steps, parallel work, rework, and contradictory outcomes (e.g., offer followed by rejection).
- Process volume: 10,000 applications observed. Core steps are widely executed but not always; some activities repeat within a single case.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
(derived from observed resources; responsibilities are those consistently suggested by the log)
- Applicants: submit applications (Hand In Job Application).
- HR staff (HR-pro, HR-dummy, Front Desk): conduct Telephonic Screening and coordinate interviews.
- Senior Partner: appears in decision-heavy steps and in Behavioral Interview panels; involved in offers.
- HR-pro and Senior Partner (joint resource): performs Behavioral Interview in some cases.
- Coding Interviewer: conducts Coding Interview and may coordinate technical interviews.
- Online System: supports intake and scheduling steps; may trigger automated statuses.
- deepResearch Inc.: executes (extensive) background checks.

Detailed step-by-step process flow

Start conditions and triggers
- Start event: Hand In Job Application (exactly once per case). This initializes the process.

Core flow (all observed paths included; optionality and orderings explicitly stated)

1) Application intake
- Task: Hand In Job Application (Applicant; Online System).
- Always first. Triggers downstream screening and/or decisions.

2) Early triage (optional)
- Decision: Optional Telephonic Screening (HR). Exactly 0 or 1 occurrence per case.
  - Valid paths after application:
    - Skip Telephonic Screening entirely and move toward final decisions.
    - Perform Telephonic Screening, then either:
      - End the triage and move toward final decisions (no further screenings), or
      - Continue with structured assessments (below).

3) Structured assessments (optional; if entered, certain activities are required/parallel)
- Parallel screening block (if this block is taken):
  - Background Check and Screening Interview both occur, in parallel. All valid interleavings are allowed:
    - Background Check before Screening Interview
    - Screening Interview before Background Check
    - Interleaving of both (observed as instantaneous directly-follows in both directions)
- Optional additional vetting:
  - Extensive Background Check may occur once after the parallel screening block (optional).
- Optional technical assessment branch:
  - Coding Interview may occur (optional).
  - If Coding Interview occurs, then any subset of the following technical interviews may be conducted, in any order and with any interleaving (parallel behavior):
    - ML Interview (optional)
    - Backend Interview (optional)
    - Frontend Interview (optional)
  - Valid orderings among ML/Backend/Frontend include:
    - Any single one alone
    - Any two, in either order, including immediate alternations
    - All three, in any order and interleaving (e.g., ML → Frontend → Backend; Backend ↔ Frontend back-to-back; ML ↔ Backend; etc.)
  - If Coding Interview does not occur, none of ML/Backend/Frontend take place.

4) Finalization phase (runs as two optional, concurrent threads; their actions can interleave)
- This phase contains two optional components that, if both chosen, execute in parallel and may interleave arbitrarily:
  A) Rejection thread
     - Application Rejected may occur zero or more times.
     - Rejections can appear:
       - Immediately after application (Application → Application Rejected)
       - After Telephonic Screening (Telephonic Screening → Application Rejected)
       - After Background/Screening (both orders observed)
       - After Extensive Background Check
       - After technical interviews
       - Before, between, or after Behavioral Interview(s) and/or Make Job Offer(s)
     - Rejection may repeat within the same case (rework loop).
  B) Evaluation-and-offer thread
     - Behavioral Interview may occur zero or more times.
       - If multiple, they occur before any Make Job Offer in that case (no Behavioral Interview observed after Make Job Offer starts in the same case).
     - Make Job Offer may occur zero or more times, but only after all Behavioral Interview occurrences (if any) for the case.
       - Offers may repeat (rework loop).
- Because the rejection and evaluation/offer threads are parallel and optional, the following interleavings are all valid and observed:
  - Only rejections (one or many), no behavioral interviews, no offers.
  - Only behavioral interview(s), then offer(s), with no rejections.
  - Rejections before any behavioral interview.
  - Rejections between multiple behavioral interviews.
  - Rejections after behavioral interview(s) but before any offer.
  - Rejections after one or more offers (Make Job Offer → Application Rejected).
  - Rejections followed by subsequent offers (Application Rejected → Make Job Offer).
  - Multiple alternations (e.g., Application Rejected → Application Rejected → Behavioral Interview → Make Job Offer; or Behavioral Interview → Behavioral Interview → Make Job Offer → Application Rejected → Make Job Offer).

Decision points with criteria and possible outcomes
- After intake:
  - Exclusive choice: Perform Telephonic Screening or skip it.
- After Telephonic Screening:
  - Exclusive choice: Proceed directly to finalization or run the structured assessment block.
- Structured assessment block:
  - Parallel split and join: Background Check and Screening Interview must both happen if the block is entered; order is unconstrained; join after both complete.
  - Exclusive choices (optional steps in sequence):
    - Option to add Extensive Background Check.
    - Option to run Coding Interview. If Coding Interview occurs, branch to a parallel split among optional ML/Backend/Frontend (any subset, any order), then join.
- Finalization:
  - Parallel split into:
    - Rejection thread (optional, repeatable)
    - Behavioral/Offer thread (Behavioral Interview repeatable; then Make Job Offer repeatable)
  - No enforced mutual exclusion between rejection and offer; both may co-occur and alternate.

Parallel activities and synchronization points
- Background Check || Screening Interview: both required if block is taken; explicit synchronization before moving to possible Extensive Background Check.
- Within the technical track (after Coding Interview): ML, Backend, and Frontend interviews are optional and parallel; synchronization after all chosen ones complete.
- Finalization has two optional concurrent threads (Rejection; Behavioral→Offer). No synchronization between them is forced; they may end in any order.

Exception handling and error paths (as observed behavior)
- Rework/loops:
  - Application Rejected can repeat within the same case.
  - Behavioral Interview can repeat back-to-back.
  - Make Job Offer can repeat back-to-back.
- Contradictory outcomes:
  - Make Job Offer may be followed quickly by Application Rejected (offer rescinded/declined or data correction).
  - Application Rejected may be followed later by Make Job Offer (status reversal or escalation).
- Skips:
  - Entire structured assessment segment (background/screening/technical) may be skipped after Telephonic Screening or even immediately after application.
  - Coding Interview and the specialized technical interviews are optional.
  - Both finalization threads are optional in the discovered model; some cases may end without a logged rejection or offer (interpreted as pending or missing closure in the log).

Rework loops and escalation procedures
- Rework loops are allowed (and observed) for:
  - Application Rejected (multiple rejections in one case).
  - Behavioral Interview (multiple behavioral rounds).
  - Make Job Offer (multiple offers).
- Escalation-like patterns (observed as path alternations):
  - Application Rejected → Behavioral Interview → Make Job Offer.
  - Make Job Offer → Application Rejected → Make Job Offer.
  - Technical interviews do not repeat in loops by model structure, but the combination and order among ML/Backend/Frontend are flexible due to parallelism.

End conditions and outcomes
- The process instance completes when both optional finalization threads (if activated) have no further activities to execute.
- Observed terminal events:
  - Application Rejected (often final in rejection-only paths, but may also appear after an offer).
  - Make Job Offer (may be the last event; acceptance is not logged in this dataset).
  - In some traces, completion occurs without a rejection or an offer being logged (allowed by the model; may indicate cases closed administratively or pending/out-of-scope outcome logging).

All valid orderings and optionality (explicit summary)
- Telephonic Screening: optional; at most once; can be skipped entirely.
- If structured assessments are taken:
  - Background Check and Screening Interview: both must occur; any order; can interleave.
  - Extensive Background Check: optional; if present, follows after both Background Check and Screening Interview.
  - Coding Interview: optional.
    - If present, ML/Backend/Frontend interviews are each optional and may execute in any order and in parallel; any subset including none is valid.
- Finalization:
  - Application Rejected: optional; can occur zero or multiple times; can appear before, between, and after behavioral/offer actions.
  - Behavioral Interview: optional; can occur zero or multiple times; must precede any Make Job Offer in a case.
  - Make Job Offer: optional; can occur zero or multiple times; can interleave with Application Rejected in either order but not with additional Behavioral Interviews after offers start.

Key challenges and pain points observed in the current process
- Fragmented finalization and conflicting statuses
  - Rejections and offers are not mutually exclusive; both can occur, and can alternate. Offers followed immediately by rejection (or vice versa) appear, suggesting status reversals, rescinds, declines, or logging corrections. This creates ambiguity for case outcome reporting.
- Rework and loops
  - Multiple Application Rejected entries in the same case and repeated Make Job Offer and Behavioral Interview events suggest rework loops and potential governance gaps (e.g., unclear exit criteria, repeated negotiations).
- Delays before technical assessments
  - Notable waiting times from screening/background to technical interviews (~36 hours on average), and longer when extensive background check is included (~+47 hours to rejection on average).
- Optionality leading to variability
  - Many alternative paths exist, including straight-to-rejection without any screening, partial technical assessments, and parallel ordering variability. While flexible, this variability complicates forecasting and SLA management.
- Data completeness and closure
  - The discovered model allows cases to complete without a logged rejection or offer; even if rare, this indicates potential missing closure events or off-system decisions.
- Inconsistencies across mined perspectives
  - Declarative constraints (e.g., “Exactly 1: Application Rejected”) contradict observed rework and repeats. This suggests mining noise or that certain constraints only hold for filtered subsets. The AS-IS (procedural) model captures the broader observed behavior.
