# AS-IS Process Model

## Process Overview and Purpose
The process described is a healthcare treatment workflow involving patient registration, examination, diagnosis, treatment, and discharge. Its primary purpose is to ensure that patients receive appropriate medical care, from initial consultation to eventual discharge. Additionally, the process accommodates various examination types and treatment outcomes, enabling flexible responses to patient needs and complications.

## Key Stakeholders and Roles Involved
- **Patients**: Individuals seeking medical treatment, either through a direct visit to the emergency room (ER) or via a formal appointment at a family doctor (FD).
- **Healthcare Practitioners**: Medical professionals conducting examinations and treatments, which may include general practitioners, specialists, and nurses.
- **Administrative Staff**: Responsible for managing patient registrations and referrals to specialists.

## Detailed Step-by-Step Process Flow

### Start Conditions and Triggers
The process begins with the registration of a patient. This can occur through one of two channels:
1. **Register at FD**: Patients can register at a family doctor's office.
2. **Register at ER**: Patients can register directly at the emergency room.

### Process Activities/Tasks in Sequence
1. **Register at FD or ER**: Patient registration is recorded.
   - Outcomes:
     - Leads to **Expert Examination** or **Examination** depending on the initial registration.
2. **Expert Examination (if registered at FD or ER)**: A thorough assessment by a specialist is conducted.
   - Leads to:
     - **Thorough Examination**.
3. **Thorough Examination**: In-depth evaluation of the patient's condition.
   - Leads to:
     - **Diagnosis**.
4. **Diagnosis**: The healthcare practitioner determines the patient's condition.
   - Leads to:
     - **Treatment**.
5. **Treatment**: The established treatment plan is applied to the patient.
   - Outcomes:
     - Can lead to either **Treatment successful** or **Treatment unsuccessful**.
6. **Treatment Successful**: If the treatment is effective.
   - Leads to:
     - **Discharge**: The patient is formally released post-treatment.
7. **Treatment Unsuccessful**: If the treatment fails.
   - Handles:
     - May lead back to **Diagnosis** for reevaluation and further treatment or a loop back to **Treatment** for additional attempts.
   
### Decision Points with Criteria and Possible Outcomes
- **Outcome of Treatment**:
  - If **Treatment Successful**: Proceed to discharge.
  - If **Treatment Unsuccessful**: Must reassess the diagnosis and reformulate an approach.
- **During Examination**:
  - Based on findings, further action may include conducting a **Thorough Examination** or revisiting the **Diagnosis**.

### Parallel Activities and Synchronization Points
- Both **Register at FD** and **Register at ER** can occur concurrently, with both leading to the same series of examinations and treatments. There is no dependency on which registration path is chosen.

### Exception Handling and Error Paths
- In the event of **Treatment Unsuccessful**, additional diagnostics are needed, involving loops back to previous steps like **Diagnosis** or re-attending the **Thorough Examination** for additional insights.
  
### Rework Loops and Escalation Procedures
- Rework occurs typically at the **Diagnosis** step when treatment outcomes do not meet expectations, leading to necessary adjustments in the treatment plan.
- Continued treatments and diagnoses may also lead to multiple cycles until a satisfactory outcome is achieved.

### End Conditions and Outcomes
The process ends when the patient is successfully discharged after a treatment deemed successful. The following conditions confirm the completion:
- The patient exhibits satisfactory recovery per established health metrics.
- Documentation for discharge is prepared.

## Process Metrics and Performance Characteristics
- The process has distinct metrics, indicating high volumes of patients treated, with notable frequencies such as:
  - 11,746 cases navigating from **Diagnosis** to **Treatment**.
  - A success rate for **Treatment** leading to **Discharge** in up to 9,905 scenarios.
- Performance metrics suggest an average time for completion of treatments and diagnoses of around 40,000 - 100,000 milliseconds, depending on the complexity.

## Key Challenges and Pain Points Observed in the Current Process
- The existence of multiple rework paths highlights inefficiencies; particularly:
  - Patients re-entering the examination phase leads to delays.
  - Certain pathways (like **Treatment Unsuccessful**) may cause bottlenecks or overutilization of resources.
- The complexity arising from simultaneous registration processes may lead to miscommunication or delays in patient handling within the healthcare setting.

This AS-IS process model reflects the current state of operations accurately, illustrating the comprehensive workflow from patient registration through treatment, along with identified metrics and issues requiring attention for improvement.
