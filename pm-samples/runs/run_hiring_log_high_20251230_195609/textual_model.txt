AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: Manage end-to-end candidate evaluation from application through interviews, background checks, decisioning (rejection or offer), and finalization.
- Source models: Inductive-miner Petri net with start at Hand In Job Application and end at a join to sink; DFG and abstract variants show concurrency, optional steps, rework, and loops.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- The source models do not assign roles. Activities suggest categories (e.g., screening/interviewing staff, background-check service, decision/offer management), but no definitive role mapping is provided. Therefore, responsibilities are left unassigned in this description.

Detailed step-by-step process flow
Start conditions and trigger
- Trigger: Candidate submits “Hand In Job Application”. This marks the start.

Core activities and control flow
1) Initial screening (optional)
   - Telephonic Screening may occur or be skipped. The Petri net includes a silent skip path allowing direct progression without this step.

2) Parallel assessment: Screening Interview and Background Check
   - After (or skipping) Telephonic Screening, the process AND-splits into two concurrent activities:
     - Screening Interview.
     - Background Check.
   - Execution order is unconstrained; either can occur first. Both must complete, then an invisible join synchronizes the flow.

3) Post-screening background diligence and technical gate
   - Extensive Background Check may occur or be skipped. Whether executed or not, the process proceeds to the technical evaluation stage.
   - Coding Interview is optional. If performed, it may lead into specialized technical interviews; otherwise the flow can skip straight past this bundle.

4) Specialized technical interviews (optional, parallel-capable, each at most once)
   - Following Coding Interview (when executed), the model creates three parallel-optional branches:
     - ML Interview (may execute or be skipped).
     - Backend Interview (may execute or be skipped).
     - Frontend Interview (may execute or be skipped).
   - These branches can occur in any order (observed DFG contains zero-duration directly-follows between pairs, indicative of concurrency) and are synchronized by an invisible join before moving on.

5) Divergence into two high-level outcome branches that run in parallel but are individually skippable
   - The model AND-splits into:
     A) Rejection branch
        - Application Rejected can occur zero or more times (explicit loop present). After any rejection, the branch can either repeat another rejection or signal completion of the branch without further action.
     B) Behavioral/Offer branch
        - Behavioral Interview can occur zero or more times (explicit self-loop present).
        - Make Job Offer can occur zero or more times (explicit self-loop present). Offers can be repeated.
        - This branch can be entirely skipped as well.
   - Both branches (A and B) deposit a completion token either by performing their activities or by taking embedded skip transitions. A final invisible join waits for both branches to complete, then the process ends.

Decision points with criteria and outcomes
- Optionality decisions:
  - Telephonic Screening: optional.
  - Extensive Background Check: optional.
  - Coding Interview: optional.
  - ML/Backend/Frontend Interviews: each optional and can run concurrently.
  - Behavioral Interview and Make Job Offer: each optional and may repeat.
- Outcome choices are not exclusive in this model: the Rejection branch and the Behavioral/Offer branch can both execute within the same case due to the parallel split with skippable sub-branches.

Parallel activities and synchronization points
- Parallel split 1: Screening Interview and Background Check run concurrently and are synchronized by a join before proceeding.
- Parallel split 2: Optional, parallel-capable ML, Backend, Frontend interviews with a join afterward.
- Parallel split 3: Rejection branch and Behavioral/Offer branch proceed in parallel, each with internal loops and independent skip/complete options; a join ends the process.

Exception handling and error paths observed (from models and DFG)
- Contradictory sequences occur because branches run in parallel:
  - Make Job Offer followed by Application Rejected (frequency 110 observed).
  - Application Rejected followed by Behavioral Interview or even Make Job Offer (frequencies 740 and 690, respectively).
- Multiple successive rejections can occur (Application Rejected -> Application Rejected, frequency 654).
- Repeated Behavioral Interview loops (Behavioral -> Behavioral, frequency 1032) and repeated offers (Make Job Offer -> Make Job Offer, frequency 861).
- Technical-interview pairs appear in both orders with near-zero measured lag, indicating concurrency rather than rework (e.g., ML <-> Frontend, Frontend <-> Backend, Backend <-> ML).

Rework loops and escalation
- Behavioral Interview: unbounded loop; may repeat many times before progressing or ending.
- Make Job Offer: unbounded loop; offers can be re-issued multiple times.
- Application Rejected: unbounded loop; multiple rejections possible within a single case.
- No explicit escalation activities are modeled; loops implicitly allow extended cycling.

End conditions and outcomes
- The process ends when both high-level branches (Rejection and Behavioral/Offer) have either completed their actions (possibly multiple times) or have been skipped, and the final join fires. There is no distinct terminal label such as “Hired”.

Process metrics and performance characteristics (from DFG and variants)
- High-frequency paths (examples):
  - Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview -> Background Check -> Application Rejected (freq 1391).
  - Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening -> Background Check -> Screening Interview -> Application Rejected (freq 1389).
  - Direct early rejection: Hand In Job Application -> Application Rejected (freq 1201).
  - Telephonic Screening -> Application Rejected (freq 990).
- Progressions to offer often traverse long durations; example composite paths to Make Job Offer show total performance around ~530,000 time units.
- Notable segment durations (performance values from DFG; illustrative samples):
  - Telephonic Screening -> Background Check: ~81,906.
  - Screening Interview <-> Background Check: 0 (bidirectional), consistent with concurrency.
  - Screening Interview -> Coding Interview: ~129,892.
  - Coding Interview -> ML/Frontend/Backend interviews: ~80,000 each.
  - Background Check -> Application Rejected: ~155,320.
  - Extensive Background Check -> Application Rejected: ~171,097.
- Loop indicators:
  - Behavioral Interview -> Behavioral Interview: freq 1032.
  - Make Job Offer -> Make Job Offer: freq 861.
  - Application Rejected -> Application Rejected: freq 654.

Key challenges and pain points
- Governance inconsistency at the end of the process:
  - Rejection and offer activities can co-occur or alternate due to a parallel split, creating contradictory candidate communications (e.g., offer after rejection, rejection after offer).
- Unbounded rework loops:
  - Repeated Behavioral Interviews and Offers extend cycle time and create variability; multiple rejection notices per case are also possible.
- Early and repeated background checks:
  - Background Check and Extensive Background Check are positioned before and after technical steps and may contribute significant delay (e.g., ~155k–171k time units before rejection), potentially performing costly diligence on non-finalists.
- High variance and optionality:
  - Many optional branches (telephonic, coding, specialized interviews) without explicit gating criteria increase variant spread and unpredictability.
- Ambiguous end-state semantics:
  - No explicit “hired/accepted” terminal event; completion is a technical join, not a business outcome, complicating reporting and control.
