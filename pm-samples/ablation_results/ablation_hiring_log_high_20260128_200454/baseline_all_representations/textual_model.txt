AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: End-to-end recruitment funnel from candidate application through screening, assessment, decisioning, and outcome. The model reflects all observed behaviors, including optional steps, concurrency, rework, and inconsistent outcomes.
- Scope: From “Hand In Job Application” to case completion with either “Make Job Offer” and/or “Application Rejected” (both may co-occur and may repeat).

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Candidate (implicit)
- HR roles observed in the log: HR-pro, HR-dummy, Front Desk, HR-pro and Senior Partner (combined), Senior Partner
- Technical interviewers: Coding Interviewer
- External/automated actors: Online System, deepResearch Inc. (likely external background-check provider)
- Note: The event log lists these resources; specific activity-to-role assignments are not fully specified. The above are the roles that appear in the log.

Detailed step-by-step process flow

Start conditions and triggers
- Start: Exactly one “Hand In Job Application” per case. This is the entry event for all process instances.

Core flow and control logic
1) Application intake
   - Activity: Hand In Job Application (mandatory, once)
   - Immediate options (exclusive choice):
     - Direct early termination by “Application Rejected” (observed in ~12% of cases).
     - Proceed to Telephonic Screening (most common, ~88% of cases based on 8799/10000).
     - Skip Telephonic Screening and move toward decisioning (rejection/behavioral/offer path) without preliminary assessments (allowed and observed).

2) Telephonic Screening (optional; exactly 0 or 1)
   - If performed, two behaviors are possible:
     a) Proceed to assessment split (most common):
        - Parallel split into both of the following, executed in any order (often effectively concurrent), with synchronization required before proceeding:
          - Screening Interview (0 or 1; commonly present when Telephonic Screening happens)
          - Background Check (0 or 1; commonly present when Telephonic Screening happens)
        - Valid orderings among the two (all observed and valid):
          - Screening Interview then Background Check
          - Background Check then Screening Interview
          - Interleaved/parallel execution (no enforced order)
        - Synchronization point: Both must complete before the next optional step (Extensive Background Check).
     b) Bypass the split and go directly toward decisioning (Telephonic Screening -> Application Rejected is observed; Telephonic Screening can also lead to post-assessment decisioning without completing the two checks).

3) Extensive Background Check (optional; 0 or 1)
   - Occurs after BOTH Screening Interview and Background Check complete, or may be skipped.
   - If performed, it can lead either to a Coding Interview or to rejection; it is also observed after either Screening Interview or Background Check individually in some traces (indicating the step is treated as an additional, discretionary deep-dive).

4) Coding Interview (optional; 0 or 1)
   - If performed, it enables a set of technical interviews that can occur in any subset and any order (including none):
     - ML Interview (optional; 0 or 1)
     - Backend Interview (optional; 0 or 1)
     - Frontend Interview (optional; 0 or 1)
   - Control logic of the technical set:
     - Inclusive-parallel pattern: Any subset of the three may be selected; if multiple are selected, they may be executed concurrently with any interleaving.
     - All valid inter-orderings among the three are allowed and observed, for example (not exhaustive):
       - ML -> Frontend; Frontend -> ML
       - Frontend -> Backend; Backend -> Frontend
       - ML -> Backend; Backend -> ML
       - Concurrent/interleaved ML, Backend, and Frontend
     - Any of these interviews may be skipped.
     - After all selected technical interviews finish, the stream rejoins for decisioning.
   - Coding Interview itself may also be skipped entirely (some cases go to decisioning without any coding/technical interviews).

5) Post-assessment decisioning (two parallel, independent substreams with loops)
   - After the early/assessment phases (which may be fully or partially skipped), cases enter a decisioning region composed of two parallel substreams that can proceed independently and interleave arbitrarily:

   Substream A: Application Rejected (rejection loop)
   - Application Rejected can occur zero, one, or multiple times.
   - After any rejection, the process may:
     - End the rejection substream (i.e., choose to stop recording further rejections), or
     - Loop and record another rejection later (self-loop observed), or
     - Allow activities in Substream B (Behavioral/Offer) to occur before or after rejections.
   - Observed valid positions for Application Rejected (examples; all are valid):
     - Immediately after Hand In Job Application (early denial)
     - After Telephonic Screening
     - After Screening Interview and/or Background Check
     - After Extensive Background Check
     - After Coding Interview
     - After any of the technical interviews (ML/Backend/Frontend)
     - After Behavioral Interview
     - After Make Job Offer (Offer -> Rejection observed; quick reversals also observed)
   - The number of rejections per case can be more than one (self-loop present with non-zero wait time).

   Substream B: Behavioral Interview and Make Job Offer (behavioral/offer loop)
   - Behavioral Interview is repeatable: can occur zero, one, or multiple times in sequence (self-loop observed).
   - Make Job Offer is also repeatable: can occur zero, one, or multiple times (self-loop observed).
   - Control logic (sequence on this substream):
     - Any number of Behavioral Interviews may occur.
     - Make Job Offer can be issued after any number of Behavioral Interviews; offers can occur multiple times and can alternate with behavioral interviews.
     - It is also permitted to make offers without additional assessments in this region (observed variants allow zero Behavioral Interviews before offers).
     - The substream can end either with an offer (last) or with no offers at all.
   - Examples of valid sequences on this substream:
     - Behavioral -> Behavioral -> Make Job Offer -> Make Job Offer
     - Make Job Offer -> Behavioral -> Make Job Offer
     - Behavioral -> (no offer)
     - (no behavioral) -> Make Job Offer -> Make Job Offer

   - Because Substream A and Substream B run in parallel, the following cross-substream interleavings are all valid and observed:
     - Rejection(s) before any Behavioral/Offer
     - Behavioral repetitions, then Rejection
     - Offer(s) then later Rejection(s)
     - Alternations such as: Rejection -> Behavioral -> Rejection -> Offer -> Rejection -> Offer
     - Cases may end with either Application Rejected, Make Job Offer, or with both having occurred (the final recorded event can be either).

End conditions and outcomes
- The case completes once both parallel substreams have reached their chosen end (i.e., no more rejections are issued and no more behavioral/offer actions are taken).
- End events observed:
  - Make Job Offer (frequent end state; offers may repeat)
  - Application Rejected (frequent end state; rejections may repeat)
- Both end states may co-exist in the same case prior to completion (e.g., Offer followed by Rejection, or Rejection followed by Offer). The last event dictates the immediate terminal status for that case.

Decision points with criteria and outcomes
- After application: immediate denial vs. moving forward (criteria not present in data; operational discretion).
- After Telephonic Screening: proceed to both Screening Interview and Background Check in parallel vs. bypass to decisioning.
- Optionality decisions:
  - Perform Extensive Background Check or skip
  - Perform Coding Interview or skip
  - Select any subset of ML/Backend/Frontend Interviews; decide ordering or concurrency
  - Conduct Behavioral Interview(s) or skip
  - Issue Make Job Offer(s) or skip
  - Record Application Rejected one or more times (may interleave with other steps)
- Joins/synchronizations:
  - If Telephonic Screening leads to the assessment split, both Screening Interview and Background Check must complete before Extensive Background Check (or further steps) can proceed.
  - If Coding Interview is done and technical interviews are chosen, all selected technical interviews must complete before returning to decisioning.
  - Final completion requires both post-assessment substreams (Rejection loop and Behavioral/Offer loop) to be closed.

Parallel activities and synchronization points
- Screening Interview and Background Check run in parallel (if that branch is taken), join before proceeding.
- ML, Backend, and Frontend Interviews form an inclusive-parallel set: any subset, any order; join after completion of all selected.
- Post-assessment decisioning comprises two parallel substreams (Rejection loop and Behavioral/Offer loop) that may interleave and only synchronize for overall completion.

Exception handling, deviations, and error paths (as actually observed)
- Early termination: Hand In Job Application -> Application Rejected without any screening.
- Telephonic Screening followed by immediate Application Rejected (skipping checks/interviews).
- Offers issued after one or more rejections; rejections recorded after offers (reversals).
- Multiple rejections within the same case.
- Multiple offers within the same case.
- Repeated Behavioral Interviews (up to at least three times observed).
- Technical interview set executed in highly variable orders, with some or all skipped.

Rework loops and escalation procedures
- Behavioral Interview loop: Can repeat multiple times, often used around late-stage decisioning and prior to/after offers.
- Make Job Offer loop: Multiple offers to the same candidate may be recorded, potentially reflecting renegotiations or corrections.
- Application Rejected loop: Multiple rejections can occur, potentially reflecting re-entries or status corrections.
- Technical interviews can be re-visited in different orders (not strict rework of the same activity instance, but flexible ordering among ML/Backend/Frontend).

Process metrics and performance characteristics (from observed data)
- Activity occurrence (approximate share of cases; using event frequencies vs. 10,000 applications):
  - Telephonic Screening: ~88%
  - Screening Interview: ~78%
  - Background Check: ~78%
  - Extensive Background Check: ~21%
  - Coding Interview: ~39%
  - ML Interview: ~23%
  - Frontend Interview: ~29%
  - Backend Interview: ~13%
  - Behavioral Interview: ~38%
  - Make Job Offer: ~34%
  - Application Rejected: frequent but may repeat; occurs both as terminal and intermediate event
- Directly-follows timing (average times between steps; selected):
  - Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening: ~22.9 hours
  - Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview: ~22.8 hours
  - Telephonic Screening -> Background Check: ~22.8 hours
  - Screening Interview -> Coding Interview: ~36.1 hours
  - Background Check -> Coding Interview: ~36.1 hours
  - Extensive Background Check -> Coding Interview: ~34.5 hours
  - Behavioral Interview -> Make Job Offer: ~22.3 hours
  - Background Check -> Application Rejected: ~43.1 hours
  - Screening Interview -> Application Rejected: ~43.3 hours
  - Telephonic Screening -> Application Rejected: ~39.8 hours
  - Make Job Offer -> Application Rejected: ~1.1 minutes (indicates quick reversals in some cases)
- Rework indicators:
  - Behavioral Interview self-loop present (very frequent, short cycle time ~47 seconds between repeats)
  - Make Job Offer self-loop present (~71 seconds between repeats)
  - Application Rejected self-loop present (~2.75 hours between repeats)
- Main observed high-frequency variants (illustrative):
  - Hand In -> Telephonic -> Background Check -> Screening Interview -> Application Rejected (~13%)
  - Hand In -> Telephonic -> Screening Interview -> Background Check -> Application Rejected (~13%)
  - Hand In -> Application Rejected (~12%)

Key challenges and pain points observed
- High variability and permissiveness:
  - Many optional steps and parallel paths produce a large number of valid variants, complicating predictability and standardization.
- Inconsistent end-state integrity:
  - Co-existence of Make Job Offer and Application Rejected within the same case, including reversals (Offer -> Rejection and Rejection -> Offer), indicates policy/process control gaps or data quality issues.
- Rework and repetition:
  - Frequent loops in Behavioral Interview and repeated Make Job Offer events suggest late-stage indecision/renegotiation.
  - Multiple Application Rejected events within the same case point to status oscillations or re-evaluations.
- Coordination overhead in concurrent assessments:
  - Parallel execution of Screening Interview and Background Check requires synchronization; delays in either can stall progress.
  - Inclusive-parallel technical interviews (ML/Backend/Frontend) increase coordination complexity; any ordering/interleaving is allowed, which can delay consolidation.
- Bottlenecks and waiting times:
  - Significant waiting before Coding Interview (~36 hours from Screening or Background Check).
  - Long waits to rejection after screening (~40–43 hours), indicating slow decision turnaround.
- Early exits:
  - A notable proportion of applicants are rejected immediately or shortly after Telephonic Screening without completing preliminary assessments.
- Data quality/control signals:
  - Immediate Offer-to-Rejection transitions and multiple offers/rejections within the same case suggest either operational exceptions or missing governance on status transitions.

Summary of all valid behavioral variants (what is permitted and observed)
- Telephonic Screening is optional; if executed, Screening Interview and Background Check are both executed (in any order/parallel) unless Telephonic is explicitly bypassed to decisioning.
- Extensive Background Check is optional.
- Coding Interview is optional; if executed, any subset of ML/Backend/Frontend Interviews may occur, in any order, possibly concurrently, then rejoin.
- Application Rejected can occur:
  - Right after application,
  - After Telephonic Screening,
  - After any screening/technical/behavioral step,
  - After Make Job Offer,
  - Multiple times (loop).
- Behavioral Interview can occur zero or many times, at late stage, and may alternate with offers and with rejections (due to parallelism of substreams).
- Make Job Offer can occur zero or many times and may appear before or after rejections.
- The process ends when no further actions are taken in either the rejection loop or the behavioral/offer loop; the last event may be either Application Rejected or Make Job Offer.

This AS-IS description captures all observed variants and control-flow behaviors: optionality, concurrency, all valid orderings among parallel sets, loops and rework, early exits, and the coexistence and alternation of end-state events.
