AS-IS Process Model

1) Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: This is the observed recruitment / candidate selection process used to screen applicants, evaluate technical and behavioral fit, perform background checks, and reach an outcome (hire – "Make Job Offer" – or reject – "Application Rejected"). The model below describes how candidates progress from submitting an application through a mix of screening activities, background checks, technical interviews and final decisions. It is an observational (AS‑IS) description: it contains all variants, rework, repeated activities, and exception flows recorded in the logs and discovered models.

2) Key stakeholders, systems and observed actors
- Observed resources in the event log (these actors appear in the data and are associated with activities; where resource names clearly indicate an activity, that association is noted as "observed performing"):
  - Online System (observed) — strongly associated with "Hand In Job Application".
  - HR-dummy, HR-pro (observed) — perform HR screening and administrative tasks (telephonic screening, coordination).
  - Front Desk (observed) — participates in telephonic screening and initial contact.
  - Coding Interviewer (observed) — performs "Coding Interview" (explicit resource name).
  - Senior Partner (observed) and "HR-pro and Senior Partner" (observed) — participate in behavioral/decision activities.
  - deepResearch Inc. (observed) — external vendor likely performing extended background checks (name suggests vendor involvement).
- Note: The log lists these resources; explicit per-activity assignment is only sometimes obvious from resource names (e.g., Coding Interviewer -> Coding Interview). Where assignment is not explicit in the source, the description states responsibilities as "observed" rather than inventing roles.

3) Start and end conditions (inferred)
- Start: Every case begins with "Hand In Job Application" (initialization constraint and all discovery artifacts).
- Observed end events / final outcomes:
  - "Make Job Offer" (hire outcome) — cases may end with one or multiple offer events.
  - "Application Rejected" (reject outcome) — cases often end here but rejection is not strictly terminal because, in observed data, cases sometimes continue after rejection (appeals, reversals or subsequent events).
- Note: The process is permissive: both "Make Job Offer" and "Application Rejected" are observed end outcomes; cases may contain multiple occurrences of either event before final closure.

4) High-level summary of the process structure
- Main backbone (most frequent): Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview and Background Check (both executed, order varies) -> optional Extensive Background Check -> Coding Interview (some cases) -> one or more technical interviews (ML, Frontend, Backend in various orders/interleavings) -> Behavioral Interview(s) -> Make Job Offer or Application Rejected.
- But many alternative and exception paths exist. Notably:
  - Immediate Application Rejected directly after application submission exists and is common.
  - Many activities are optional and may be skipped in some traces (Extensive Background Check, Coding Interview, some technical interviews, Behavioral Interview may repeat or be absent).
  - Rejection and offer decisions can occur at many points (after telephonic screening, after background check, after coding/technical interviews, after behavioral), and rework (reversed rejects, offers rescinded) is observed.

5) Detailed step-by-step process flow (including every observed variant and ordering)

A. Entry
1. Step 0 (Start): Candidate submits application — "Hand In Job Application".
   - This activity is always present as the first recorded event.
   - Observed performer(s): Online System (common), HR roles may record receipt.

B. Immediate decision branch after application
2. Decision A (immediate rejection vs proceed):
   - Observed outcomes:
     - Immediate Application Rejected (observed frequently): Some cases end or move to rejection immediately after submission.
       - Frequency evidence: Direct Hand In -> Application Rejected observed many times.
     - Proceed to Telephonic Screening (observed in the majority): Hand In -> Telephonic Screening (very frequent).
   - No single explicit business rule is provided in data; the branching is empirical (some candidates are rejected at intake, others proceed).

C. Telephonic Screening
3. Telephonic Screening (if not immediately rejected)
   - Observed performer(s): Front Desk, HR-pro, HR-dummy.
   - After Telephonic Screening, three alternative observed outcomes (not mutually exclusive in the data):
     - Telephonic Screening -> Background Check (frequently observed).
     - Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview (frequently observed).
     - Telephonic Screening -> Application Rejected (some candidates are rejected at this stage).
   - Important behavioral detail: Background Check and Screening Interview usually both occur for a case, but the order between them varies; both orders are observed with similarly high frequency:
     - Valid orderings after Telephonic Screening:
       - Telephonic Screening -> Background Check -> Screening Interview
       - Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview -> Background Check
     - Both Background Check and Screening Interview may be performed (co‑existence strongly supported by multiple discoveries), but either one may be skipped in some traces.

D. Screening Interview and Background Check interplay
4. Screening Interview and Background Check
   - Observed behavior:
     - They commonly both appear in a case (log-skeleton and heuristics net indicate equivalence / co-occurrence).
     - Order is not fixed: both orders (Screening->Background and Background->Screening) are frequent.
     - Either activity may occasionally be absent in some traces (Activity Occurrences allow 0 or 1 for both).
   - Decision B (post-screening/background):
     - After these, several options are observed:
       - Application Rejected (often occurs after these checks).
       - Proceed to Extensive Background Check (optional) — occurs for some candidates.
       - Proceed to Coding Interview (for technical roles) — many cases proceed to coding.
   - Observed frequencies show that many candidates are rejected after background/screening checks.

E. Extensive Background Check (optional)
5. Extensive Background Check
   - Optional; when present it follows Background Check or Screening Interview in many traces.
   - Observed outcome(s):
     - Leads either to Application Rejected or to Coding Interview (or back into workflow).
   - Observed performer(s): external vendors (e.g., deepResearch Inc.) or HR.

F. Coding Interview (optional; technical evaluation)
6. Coding Interview
   - Occurs in a subset of cases (Coding Interview frequency lower than initial steps).
   - Observed behavior after Coding Interview:
     - Multiple follow-on paths are observed with strong support:
       - Coding Interview -> Frontend Interview
       - Coding Interview -> ML Interview
       - Coding Interview -> Backend Interview
       - Coding Interview -> Application Rejected
     - These follow-ons are not exclusive; any combination and order of technical interviews can be observed after coding. Coding Interview may be skipped in some traces (coding optional for non-technical roles).
   - Notes on rework and branching:
     - Coding Interview can lead to immediate rejection, to a sequence of technical interviews, or to further background checks in rare cases.

G. Technical interviews: ML, Frontend, Backend (variable, possibly multiple, interleaved)
7. Technical interviews (ML Interview, Frontend Interview, Backend Interview)
   - Observed patterns:
     - Technical interviews may occur singly or in multiple combinations; the set {ML, Frontend, Backend} can appear in varying subsets and orders.
     - Interleavings and reciprocal orderings are observed (e.g., ML -> Frontend and Frontend -> ML both frequently observed; Backend <-> Frontend both orders also observed). This indicates these interviews can happen in different orders and sometimes be repeated or revisited.
     - Valid example orderings observed in event traces (non-exhaustive but representative of allowed permutations):
       - Coding -> ML -> Frontend -> Behavioral
       - Coding -> Frontend -> ML -> Behavioral
       - Coding -> Backend -> Frontend -> Behavioral
       - Coding -> Frontend -> Backend -> ML -> Behavioral
       - Coding -> ML -> Backend -> Frontend -> Behavioral
     - In many cases, one or two technical interviews are used rather than all three.
   - Observed transitions between technical interviews:
     - ML <-> Frontend show frequent back-and-forth sequences (both directions recorded).
     - Backend Interview can appear before or after Frontend/ML (both directions observed).
   - Outcomes from technical stage:
     - Proceed to Behavioral Interview.
     - Application Rejected (some candidates are removed after technical interviews).
     - In some traces, technical interviews are followed by further technical interviews (rework, retesting).

H. Behavioral Interview(s)
8. Behavioral Interview
   - Usually occurs after technical interviews but may occur earlier or multiple times.
   - Observed characteristics:
     - Behavioral Interview may repeat (behavioral interview self-loop observed: frequency indicates repeated behavioral interviews in some cases).
     - Outcomes:
       - Usually leads toward Make Job Offer when successful (Behavioral Interview -> Make Job Offer frequently observed).
       - May also lead to Application Rejected (some behavioral interviews result in rejection).
   - Behavioral interview frequency is substantial but not guaranteed (Activity Occurrences allow 0..3).

I. Make Job Offer and post-offer behavior
9. Make Job Offer
   - Observed as a final outcome in many cases.
   - Observed anomalies:
     - Make Job Offer may occur multiple times in a case (self-loop observed) — offers can be re-issued or logged multiple times.
     - There are observed cases where Make Job Offer is followed by Application Rejected (offer rescinded or re-classified), and cases where Application Rejected is followed later by Make Job Offer (reversal of rejection). Thus "Make Job Offer" and "Application Rejected" are not strictly terminal in the observed data.
   - Valid observed sequences around offer/reject:
     - ... -> Behavioral Interview -> Make Job Offer (then maybe stop)
     - ... -> Application Rejected -> Behavioral Interview -> Make Job Offer (reinstatement)
     - Make Job Offer -> Application Rejected (rescinded)

J. Application Rejected (occurs at many points)
10. Application Rejected
   - Observed at multiple points in the flow:
     - Immediately after Hand In Job Application.
     - After Telephonic Screening.
     - After Background Check / Extensive Background Check.
     - After Coding or technical interviews.
     - After Behavioral Interview.
   - Observed behaviors:
     - Application Rejected may repeat (self-loops).
     - Application Rejected sometimes precedes further activities (e.g., Behavioral Interview, Make Job Offer), indicating case rework or reversed decisions.
   - Therefore, rejection is not always final in observed traces.

K. Loops, rework and reversals (summary)
11. Observed loops and rework:
   - Repeated Behavioral Interview occurrences in the same case (0..3 times observed).
   - Repeated Make Job Offer occurrences (0..3).
   - Repeated Application Rejected occurrences (0..3).
   - Technical interviews (ML/Frontend/Backend) can alternate (sequence A then B then A observed) — short loops/alternating patterns exist.
   - Reversal flows: Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer and Make Job Offer -> Application Rejected are observed; re-evaluation of cases occurs.

6) Decision points and criteria (as inferred from observed behavior)
- Decision 1 (post-submission): Accept to screen vs immediate reject.
  - Observed criterion: not explicit in model; likely administrative filter or automated screening.
- Decision 2 (post-telephonic): Send candidate to Screening Interview and/or Background Check, or reject.
  - Observed behavior: Some candidates receive both Screening Interview and Background Check (co‑existence); others are rejected here or only receive one.
- Decision 3 (post-background/screening): Whether to perform Extensive Background Check or proceed to technical interviews or reject.
  - Observed behavior: Extensive Background Check is optional; often leads to either rejection or progression to coding.
- Decision 4 (based on Coding / technical interview results): Reject, continue with additional technical interviews, or progress to Behavioral Interview.
  - Observed behavior: Coding often branches to any of the technical interviews, or rejection; order and selection of technical interviews vary by candidate.
- Decision 5 (post-behavioral): Offer vs reject.
  - Observed behavior: Behavioral Interview commonly leads to Make Job Offer or Application Rejected; both outcomes observed and sometimes reversed later.

Note: The logs and discovered declarative constraints give no deterministic decision rules — the process description is behavioral: decisions result in observed branching but the precise business rules for these choices were not provided and must be inferred from frequencies only.

7) Parallelism and concurrency
- Screening Interview and Background Check: frequently co‑occurring; ordering between them is flexible (both orders occur). They behave as concurrent/independent checks from a behavioral point of view (the process accepts both interleavings).
- Technical interviews among ML, Frontend, Backend: these can be performed in multiple orders and sometimes concurrently (interleavings observed), therefore the process permits many interleavings of these tasks.
- The model allows all interleavings observed in the logs; the process is permissive and treats several points as parallel or loosely ordered rather than strictly sequential.

8) All observed valid orderings (explicitly listing major families)
- After Hand In Job Application:
  - Hand In -> Application Rejected (immediate)
  - Hand In -> Telephonic Screening -> Application Rejected
  - Hand In -> Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview -> Background Check -> Application Rejected
  - Hand In -> Telephonic Screening -> Background Check -> Screening Interview -> Application Rejected
  - Hand In -> Telephonic Screening -> (Screening & Background in any order) -> (optional Extensive Background Check) -> Coding Interview -> any ordering of technical interviews (ML, Frontend, Backend) -> Behaviorals -> Make Job Offer
  - Hand In -> Telephonic Screening -> Background/Screening -> Coding -> ML -> Frontend -> Behavioral -> Make Job Offer
  - Hand In -> Telephonic Screening -> Background/Screening -> Coding -> Frontend -> ML -> Behavioral -> Make Job Offer
  - (Variants where Application Rejected occurs at any point, and where rejection is later followed by Behavioral Interview or Make Job Offer)
- Note: The Abstract Variants section contains many exact sequences observed in the logs. The AS‑IS description allows any trace that matches any discovered variant; examples above represent the families of observed variants, not an exhaustive enumeration of every low-frequency permutation.

9) Exception handling and error paths
- Application Rejected is the primary exception/outcome activity and is observed at virtually every stage.
- Reversals: Application Rejected is sometimes not terminal — cases can continue and later reach Make Job Offer (reinstatement) or additional interviews. Conversely, Make Job Offer can be followed by Application Rejected (offer rescind).
- Repeated activities: Multiple behavioral interviews, repeated offers, and repeated rejections reflect exception handling and rework (appeals, re-evaluations).
- Incomplete cases: Some traces end early (e.g., Hand In -> Application Rejected) representing quick closure.

10) Synchronization points and merges
- After parallel/co‑occurring Screening Interview and Background Check, the flow converges before optional Extensive Background Check or Coding Interview. The observed artifacts include invisible/silent transitions (in inductive/ILP Petri nets) that synchronize these branches; behaviorally, both checks typically need to be completed (in either order) before proceeding to later stages, but the process allows skipping either.

11) Process termination
- Cases end when recorded events stop. The most common observed terminal activities are:
  - Application Rejected (most frequent final activity)
  - Make Job Offer
- Because the log shows multiple occurrences of end activities and reversals, termination is defined by the recorded trace end rather than a business rule in logs. The net discovery indicates both are accepted final states.

7) Key challenges and pain points observed in the current process (AS‑IS)
- High variability and complexity:
  - Many alternative branches, optional steps, and interleavings lead to a highly variable candidate experience and process complexity.
- Long lead times:
  - Average times between core steps (e.g., initial screening to interviews or decisions) are large (days to weeks), suggesting bottlenecks or slow handoffs.
- Frequent early rejections and multiple rework paths:
  - High number of Application Rejected events (many early rejections after Hand In or Telephonic Screening) combined with cases where rejections are later reversed indicates inconsistent decision criteria and rework overhead.
- Unclear decision rules:
  - The empirical model shows branching decisions at multiple stages but no single deterministic rule; inconsistent application of criteria likely causes re-evaluation and repeated activities.
- Reversals and offer rescinds:
  - Observed Make Job Offer -> Application Rejected and Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer flows indicate unstable finalization and possible communication/administrative problems (e.g., offers rescinded, misclassification).
- Repeated activities:
  - Multiple behavioral interviews and multiple offer/reject events for some cases increase cost and delay.
- Coordination across actors and vendors:
  - Different internal resources and external vendors (e.g., deepResearch Inc.) are involved; coordination of background checks and interviews appears to create complex orchestration that produces high variation in flow and timing.
- Parallel/loose ordering of crucial checks:
  - Screening Interview and Background Check often run in parallel or alternate in order; this flexibility can be positive but also leads to synchronization challenges (cases waiting for the slower check).

8) Additional behavioral observations and fidelity assurances
- The AS‑IS description preserves all observed behavior: every sequence and variant recorded in the provided representations is allowed by this model. Specifically:
  - All permutations and interleavings of Screening Interview and Background Check observed are permitted.
  - All observed orders and combinations of Coding Interview and subsequent technical interviews (ML, Frontend, Backend) are permitted and may repeat or alternate.
  - Application Rejected and Make Job Offer can occur multiple times and may appear in non-final positions; the model accepts traces where a rejection is later followed by further interviews or by an offer, and traces where an offer is later followed by rejection.
  - Optional activities (Extensive Background Check, Coding Interview, technical interviews, Behavioral Interview) are allowed to be skipped in traces where they did not appear.
  - The model allows repeated Behavioral Interviews (0..3) and multiple offer/reject events per case as seen in logs.

9) Responsibilities & mapping (observed)
- Observed resource/activity associations (from log attributes):
  - Hand In Job Application — Online System (frequently observed).
  - Telephonic Screening — Front Desk, HR-pro, HR-dummy.
  - Coding Interview — Coding Interviewer.
  - Behavioral Interview / final decision coordination — Senior Partner, HR-pro and Senior Partner.
  - Extensive/Background Check — deepResearch Inc. and HR staff.
- Note: The log provides resource names; exact role-to-activity binding in every trace is not fully enumerated in the discovery outputs, therefore the above are observed associations rather than prescriptive assignments.

10) Practical implications for stakeholders (final notes)
- Any improvements or remodelling must respect the high variability currently present: the process is not strictly sequential and many branches and interleavings will need explicit policy or automation to reduce rework.
- Key levers for improvement to consider (not prescriptive in AS‑IS but implied by problems):
  - Clarify decision criteria (when to reject vs continue).
  - Reduce long wait times by improving coordination between background checks and interviews.
  - Reduce rework loops (re-review, repeated behavioral interviews, offer rescinds) by improving decision governance.
  - Better logging of decision reasons could help diagnose why rejections get reversed or offers rescinded.
