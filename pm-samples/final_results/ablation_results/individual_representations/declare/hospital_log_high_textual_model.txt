AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- This declarative model represents a single clinical care episode constrained to a strict, linear path: Diagnosis → Treatment → Treatment successful → Discharge.
- Purpose: ensure that every case goes from an initial diagnostic step through one treatment cycle, reaches a successful outcome, and then is discharged. The model encodes compliance-style requirements that strictly forbid deviations or additional steps.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Roles/actors are not specified in the provided model. Activities are described at a process level without lane/role assignments.

Detailed step-by-step process flow

Start conditions and triggers
- Start event is implicit in the constraints. The first recorded activity in a conformant trace is Diagnosis.
- Trigger: a new clinical case is opened (implicit); no explicit initialization activity is modeled.

Activities and sequence flow
1) Diagnosis (must occur; exactly once)
   - Constraint set mandates that Diagnosis occurs exactly one time per case.
   - Chain succession and alternate constraints require that Treatment immediately follows Diagnosis; no other modeled activities can occur between them.

2) Treatment (must occur; exactly once)
   - Treatment occurs exactly once per case.
   - Chain succession requires that Treatment successful immediately follows Treatment.
   - Negative constraint: Treatment unsuccessful must not occur (Absence).

3) Treatment successful (must occur; exactly once)
   - Occurs exactly once per case.
   - Chain succession requires that Discharge immediately follows Treatment successful.

4) Discharge (must occur; exactly once)
   - Occurs exactly once per case.
   - Ends the case.

Decision points and gateways
- None allowed. The constraint set enforces a single permissible ordering with no branching.
- All coexistence and succession constraints imply that if any one of the four activities occurs, then all four occur exactly once in that exact order.

Parallel activities and synchronization points
- None. The model forbids concurrency and enforces immediate adjacency between each consecutive activity.

Exception handling and error paths
- None encoded. Any deviation (e.g., a second diagnosis, an unsuccessful treatment outcome, an intermediate step between the mandated pairs) violates constraints and is nonconformant.

Rework loops and escalation procedures
- None. The model enforces “Exactly 1” for Diagnosis, Treatment, Treatment successful, and Discharge and permits no loops or repetitions.

End conditions and outcomes
- Single end state: Discharge following Treatment successful.
- No alternative end states are permitted.

Process metrics and performance characteristics
- Permitted trace pattern: exactly one trace structure is valid: Diagnosis → Treatment → Treatment successful → Discharge.
- Cardinality: each activity occurs exactly once; Treatment unsuccessful is prohibited.
- Temporal adjacency: immediate succession constraints (chain) require that:
  - Treatment immediately follows Diagnosis
  - Treatment successful immediately follows Treatment
  - Discharge immediately follows Treatment successful
- Variability: zero; no optionality, no branching, no rework, and no parallelism exist within the modeled scope.
- Implicit dependency: strong co-existence—if any of the four activities occurs, all four must occur.

Key challenges and pain points
- Overly rigid and potentially unrealistic for clinical practice:
  - Prohibits representing unsuccessful treatments; adverse outcomes cannot be recorded.
  - No allowance for re-diagnosis, treatment adjustments, or multiple treatment cycles.
  - Immediate adjacency (chain) forbids any other modeled steps between the four activities (e.g., observation, counseling, or documentation) within the modeled scope.
  - No exception handling for complications, contraindications, or patient-driven decisions.
  - Only a single successful path exists; this may incentivize non-reporting of unsuccessful outcomes or out-of-model workarounds.
- Governance risk: the model encodes only “successful” care and discharge; it lacks controls for monitoring repeated interventions or clinical review when success is not reached.

IDEAL Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: preserve the core care path while adding realistic, governed flexibility:
  - Permit explicit recording of unsuccessful outcomes (Treatment unsuccessful).
  - Enable controlled rework loops (re-diagnosis and/or additional treatment cycles) until success or a clinically appropriate termination.
  - Maintain clear termination via Discharge after either a successful or unsuccessful outcome, as clinically justified.
- Scope remains within the given activity set: Diagnosis, Treatment, Treatment successful, Treatment unsuccessful, Discharge.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Roles/actors are not specified in the provided model. The process is described at a process level without lane/role assignments.

Detailed step-by-step process flow

Start conditions and triggers
- Start event: a new clinical case is opened (implicit).
- Initial activity: Diagnosis is the first modeled activity of a case.

Activities and sequence flow
1) Diagnosis (at least once; may repeat when clinically indicated)
   - Establish or refine the clinical understanding of the case.
   - On completion, proceed to Treatment. The strict “immediate next” requirement is relaxed; non-modeled operational steps (e.g., waiting, documentation) may occur in time, but within this model the next recorded activity is Treatment.

2) Treatment (one or more cycles)
   - Administer an intervention aligned to the current diagnosis.
   - After each Treatment, record the outcome using one of the two modeled result activities:
     a) Treatment successful
     b) Treatment unsuccessful

3a) If Treatment successful
   - Proceed to Discharge.
   - The “immediate adjacency” constraint is relaxed; however, within this model the next modeled step is Discharge.

3b) If Treatment unsuccessful
   - A governed rework loop is allowed using only existing activities:
     - Option 1: Re-diagnosis (repeat Diagnosis) to reassess and then perform another Treatment cycle.
     - Option 2: Repeat Treatment directly without re-diagnosis when clinically appropriate.
   - This loop may repeat until success is achieved or termination criteria are met.
   - Exception termination: when clinically indicated (e.g., no further beneficial interventions anticipated), proceed to Discharge after an unsuccessful outcome.

4) Discharge (case termination)
   - Ends the case following either:
     - Treatment successful, or
     - Treatment unsuccessful (when further intervention is not indicated).

Decision points and gateways
- Exclusive decision after Treatment:
  - Outcome is either Treatment successful or Treatment unsuccessful.
- Inclusive/iterative behavior:
  - Following Treatment unsuccessful, either re-diagnose, re-treat, or (if indicated) discharge. The loop continues until a terminating outcome is reached.

Parallel activities and synchronization points
- None modeled. The process remains sequential within the provided activity set.

Exception handling and error paths
- Explicitly modeled unsuccessful outcome path (Treatment unsuccessful).
- Termination without success is allowed via Discharge when further treatment is not appropriate.
- The process supports multiple iterations to manage complications or non-response.

Rework loops and escalation procedures
- Rework loop:
  - Treatment unsuccessful → (Diagnosis optional) → Treatment → outcome decision.
  - Loop continues until Treatment successful or Discharge after unsuccessful.
- Governance considerations (policy-level, non-activity constraints):
  - Define maximum number of allowed treatment cycles before mandatory reassessment (carried by the Diagnosis activity).
  - Define clinical criteria for discharging after unsuccessful outcomes.
  - These controls shape the loop without introducing new activity labels.

End conditions and outcomes
- Discharge after Treatment successful (desired path).
- Discharge after Treatment unsuccessful (clinically justified termination).
- All cases terminate with Discharge; the path taken is explicitly recorded.

Process metrics and performance characteristics
- Cycle-aware metrics:
  - Number of treatment cycles per case.
  - Rate of Treatment successful vs Treatment unsuccessful.
  - Time from first Diagnosis to Discharge.
  - Time and number of cycles to first success.
- Conformance metrics:
  - No cases with Discharge before any Treatment outcome.
  - All Treatment executions are followed by an outcome recording (successful or unsuccessful).
- Variability:
  - Controlled variability via loop; Diagnosis and Treatment may repeat; outcomes determine path length.

Improvements made vs. AS-IS
- Restored the clinically necessary outcome branch by allowing Treatment unsuccessful (previously forbidden).
- Introduced a controlled rework loop using only existing activities (Diagnosis and Treatment), replacing the rigid “Exactly 1” constraints.
- Relaxed immediate adjacency requirements so that necessary operational steps can occur between modeled activities without violating process logic.
- Clarified termination conditions: Discharge is permitted after success or, when appropriate, after an unsuccessful outcome.
- Enhanced transparency and governance: unsuccessful outcomes are explicitly recorded; process supports iterative care and accountable termination, improving quality monitoring and realism.
