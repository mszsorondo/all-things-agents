AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: Manage a patient episode from initial registration, through clinical assessment, diagnosis, treatment, and eventual discharge.
- Scope captured from the discovered model: Two alternative entry points (Register at ER or Register at FD), an initial assessment (either Examination or Expert Examination), a mandatory Thorough Examination, a synchronized Diagnosis, Treatment, and an outcome that either loops back to Diagnosis (Treatment unsuccessful) or allows Discharge after Treatment successful.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Specific roles are not named in the source. Activities are described generically as performed by the clinical care team and registration/in-take staff associated with the activity labels.

Detailed step-by-step process flow

1) Start conditions and triggers
- Trigger: Patient episode begins upon arrival and registration.
- Start event: A single start enables exactly one of the following mutually exclusive activities:
  - Register at ER
  - Register at FD

2) Registration (exclusive start choice)
- Activity options (exclusive OR):
  - Register at ER
  - Register at FD
- Effect of registration:
  - Provides the prerequisite token/condition used later for Diagnosis.
  - Enables the initial clinical assessment options (Examination or Expert Examination).
  - Pre-enables Thorough Examination in conjunction with the initial assessment (see synchronization below).

3) Initial clinical assessment (exclusive choice)
- Exactly one of the following is performed after registration:
  - Examination
  - Expert Examination
- Behavioral effect:
  - Completes one of the prerequisites needed for both Thorough Examination (enables it) and, ultimately, Diagnosis.

4) Thorough Examination (mandatory before first Diagnosis)
- Execution constraints:
  - Thorough Examination cannot occur until BOTH:
    - Registration is completed, and
    - Exactly one of Examination or Expert Examination has been performed.
- Behavioral effect:
  - Completes the second prerequisite needed for Diagnosis.
  - In this discovered behavior, Thorough Examination is required for every case prior to the first Diagnosis (there is no bypass around Thorough Examination).

5) Synchronized Diagnosis (AND-join of preconditions)
- Diagnosis fires only when ALL of these conditions are satisfied:
  - Case has been registered (Register at ER or Register at FD),
  - An initial assessment is complete (Examination OR Expert Examination),
  - A Thorough Examination is complete.
- Notes:
  - This is an explicit synchronization point: Diagnosis is gated until all required prior assessments are done.

6) Treatment
- Performed immediately after Diagnosis.
- Produces two outcome-precondition tokens that must be resolved by subsequent transitions (see outcome logic below).

7) Outcome decision and case continuation
- Two transitions are enabled by Treatment; how they combine determines the next step:
  - Treatment successful:
    - When selected, Discharge becomes enabled (together with the post-Treatment completion token).
  - Treatment unsuccessful:
    - When selected, the process loops back to Diagnosis.
    - The model directly re-enables Diagnosis without requiring the examinations again; “Treatment unsuccessful” supplies the necessary prerequisites for Diagnosis.
- Discharge:
  - Can occur only after Treatment successful has occurred.
  - Represents the end of the clinical episode.

8) Rework loops and escalation procedures
- Rework loop:
  - Treatment unsuccessful → directly back to Diagnosis (no repeat of Examination or Thorough Examination is required by the model for re-diagnosis).
  - Then Treatment is performed again, with the same outcome logic.
  - This loop may repeat multiple times until a Treatment successful outcome is achieved.
- There is no explicit escalation activity in the discovered model; repeated unsuccessful outcomes simply re-trigger Diagnosis.

9) Exception handling and error paths
- The model does not include explicit exception paths for early discharge, skip-to-discharge, or administrative cancellations.
- There is no modeled path to Discharge without Treatment successful; discharge is contingent on success.

10) Parallel activities and synchronization points
- The model ultimately requires the following synchronization before the first Diagnosis:
  - Registration completed,
  - Exactly one of Examination or Expert Examination completed,
  - Thorough Examination completed.
- There is no concurrent execution of Examination and Expert Examination (exclusive choice).
- Thorough Examination is enabled only after the initial assessment and registration and must be completed before Diagnosis.

End conditions and outcomes
- End event: Discharge.
- Discharge can only be reached after Treatment successful has occurred (no direct discharge without success).

Process metrics and performance characteristics (as evidenced by structure)
- Lead time components:
  - Registration to initial assessment,
  - Initial assessment to Thorough Examination,
  - Thorough Examination to Diagnosis,
  - Diagnosis to Treatment,
  - Treatment to outcome (success/unsuccessful) and, if successful, to Discharge.
- Rework intensity:
  - Potentially unbounded loop count for (Treatment → Treatment unsuccessful → Diagnosis → Treatment).
  - No modeled requirement to repeat examinations in the loop, which shortens re-diagnosis time at the cost of potential under-assessment.
- Flow rigidity:
  - Thorough Examination is mandatory before the first Diagnosis, adding time and resource load to all cases.
- Outcome gating:
  - Discharge rate is tightly coupled to Treatment successful frequency.

Key challenges and pain points
- Mandatory Thorough Examination for all cases:
  - Increases time-to-diagnosis and resource consumption even for straightforward cases.

  - Exactly one of Examination or Expert Examination can be performed; the other cannot be used complementarily for the same case prior to Diagnosis.

- Potential bottlenecks:
  - Thorough Examination acts as a global gate for all cases before the first Diagnosis.

IDEAL Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: Deliver a clinically sound, efficient episode-of-care process from registration through assessment, diagnosis, treatment, and discharge.
- Design goals:
  - Reduce unnecessary mandatory steps (make Thorough Examination conditional rather than universal),
  - Preserve clinical safety by synchronizing only the information actually required for Diagnosis,
  - Improve loop governance when treatments fail, enabling targeted re-assessment.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Roles are not specified in the source. This design describes responsibilities by activity name without assigning new actors.

Detailed step-by-step process flow

1) Start conditions and triggers
- Trigger: Patient arrives and registers.
- Entry options (exclusive):
  - Register at ER
  - Register at FD
- IDEAL governance:
  - Either registration path creates a unified “registered case” status used downstream; both paths feed the same subsequent clinical flow.

2) Initial clinical assessment (triage-informed, exclusive choice)
- Decision gateway: Select the appropriate initial assessment based on presenting signs, severity, or availability:
  - Examination (standard)
  - Expert Examination (specialist-level)
- Rationale:
  - Maintain exclusivity (as-is behavior) but formalize selection criteria so the choice is consistent and auditable.

3) Conditional Thorough Examination
- Decision gateway: “Is a Thorough Examination required for safe and accurate Diagnosis?”
  - Yes → Perform Thorough Examination.
  - No → Bypass Thorough Examination and proceed toward Diagnosis.
- Synchronization rule for Diagnosis:
  - Diagnosis requires:
    - Registration completed, and
    - At least one suitable assessment path that satisfies clinical information needs:
      - Either: Expert Examination alone if sufficient, or
      - Examination + Thorough Examination if the case warrants, or
      - Examination alone if protocols deem it sufficient for straightforward cases.
- Rationale:
  - Converts the universal requirement for Thorough Examination into an evidence-based, conditional step, eliminating waste for uncomplicated cases while keeping it mandatory when needed.

4) Diagnosis (information-driven AND-join)
- Inputs:
  - Registered case,
  - Required clinical information (produced by the selected assessment path as above).
- Behavior:
  - Proceed once the chosen assessment path has provided the necessary information for a confident diagnosis.

5) Treatment
- Performed after Diagnosis.
- Outcome evaluation immediately follows.

6) Outcome decision, completion, and loop governance
- Decision gateway:
  - Treatment successful → Discharge.
  - Treatment unsuccessful → Re-diagnose.
- Re-diagnosis loop (improved governance):
  - On Treatment unsuccessful, perform Diagnosis again.
  - Optional, targeted re-assessment before re-diagnosis:
    - Use existing assessment activities (Examination, Expert Examination, and/or Thorough Examination) only if new or clarifying information is required, based on defined clinical criteria.
  - Loop continues until Treatment successful is achieved.
- Discharge:
  - Occurs only after Treatment successful (as in as-is), ensuring clinical completion.

7) Exception handling and error paths
- If repeated unsuccessful treatments occur:
  - Enforce escalation rules within existing activity choices:
    - Prefer Expert Examination on re-assessment if standard Examination was previously chosen and proved insufficient.
    - Prefer Thorough Examination when uncertainty persists after initial assessments.
- Early administrative terminations, transfers, or alternative dispositions are not added as new activities; the model remains clinically focused and ends at Discharge upon success.

8) Parallel activities and synchronization points
- No concurrent use of Examination and Expert Examination for the same diagnosis cycle (exclusive choice maintained).
- Synchronization before Diagnosis is simplified:
  - Registration + “sufficient clinical information present” rather than Registration + (Initial Assessment) + (Mandatory Thorough Examination).
- Thorough Examination remains available but is only synchronized in when required.

End conditions and outcomes
- End event: Discharge, reachable only after Treatment successful.
- The process supports multiple Diagnosis–Treatment cycles, with structured criteria guiding when additional assessments are needed.

Process metrics and performance characteristics (target state)
- Lead time reduction:
  - Fewer mandatory Thorough Examinations reduce time to first Diagnosis for straightforward cases.
- Diagnostic adequacy:
  - Conditional use of Expert Examination and Thorough Examination guided by explicit criteria strengthens diagnostic quality without universal overhead.
- Rework management:
  - Track the number of Diagnosis–Treatment cycles per case; trigger defined reassessment criteria upon each unsuccessful outcome.
- Throughput and capacity:
  - Reduced unnecessary thorough exams free specialist resources, improving overall throughput.
- Compliance and auditability:
  - Documented decision criteria for assessment selection improve consistency and governance.

Key improvements vs AS-IS
- Conditional Thorough Examination:
  - Eliminates the blanket requirement; kept only when clinically justified.
- Formalized assessment selection:
  - Clear criteria for choosing Examination vs Expert Examination minimize misrouting and rework.
- Smarter re-diagnosis:
  - After Treatment unsuccessful, re-assess only as needed using existing activities; avoids blind loops and under-informed decisions.
- Streamlined synchronization:
  - Diagnosis requires “sufficient information,” not a fixed set of all prior assessments, reducing delays while preserving safety.
- Outcome clarity:
  - Discharge remains contingent on Treatment successful (no unsafe early exits), but the path to success is made more efficient and governed.
