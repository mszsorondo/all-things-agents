Business Process Description:
# AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- This process describes the end-to-end residential leasing lifecycle as mined from event data: from a prospective tenant applying for a viewing, through property viewing, paperwork, screening, decision (sign or reject), move-in, and recurring rent collection, up to termination (tenant cancellation or eviction). The model captures both the mainline flow and all observed deviations and loops, including late payments and warnings.

Key stakeholders and roles involved (inferred from activity names)
- Prospective Tenant: applies, views the property, submits paperwork, signs, moves in, pays rent, can cancel.
- Property Management/Leasing Team: sets appointments, checks paperwork, screens, rejects or accepts, issues warnings, accepts late payments, performs evictions.
(Note: Roles are inferred from activity names; actual organizational titles may vary.)

Detailed step-by-step process flow
Start conditions and triggers
- Trigger: A prospective tenant initiates the process by submitting “Apply for Viewing Appointment” (start place → Apply for Viewing Appointment).

Pre-leasing pipeline (application to decision)
1) Apply for Viewing Appointment
   - Primary observed path: to “Set Appointment” (frequency ≈ 9,151).
   - Alternate early-exit path: directly to “Reject Prospective Tenant” without appointment (frequency ≈ 849). This captures pre-appointment screening/eligibility checks implicitly represented by a gateway (via silent transitions in the Petri net).

2) Set Appointment → 3) View The Property
   - Observed frequency: Set Appointment → View The Property ≈ 9,151.
   - Ordering constraint (clarification): “View The Property” must occur before any paperwork; “Hand In The Paperwork” cannot precede “View The Property” (no reordering of these steps).

4) Post-viewing decision
   - Branch A (stop): “Reject Prospective Tenant” directly after viewing (frequency ≈ 1,710).
   - Branch B (continue): proceed to documentation.

5) Hand In The Paperwork (frequency from View → Hand In ≈ 7,441)
6) Check Paperwork → 7) Screen Prospective Tenant
   - Hand In → Check Paperwork (≈ 7,441)
   - Check Paperwork → Screen Prospective Tenant (≈ 7,441)
   - Ordering constraint (clarification): The paperwork/screening subchain is strictly ordered as View → Hand In → Check → Screen; “Screen Prospective Tenant” must not occur before “Check Paperwork”, and no paperwork activity can occur before viewing.

8) Screening decision
   - Option 1: Reject Prospective Tenant (directly after screening) (≈ 2,856)
   - Option 2: Extensive Screening (≈ 2,793) then
       • Extensive Screening → Reject Prospective Tenant (≈ 1,815), or
       • Extensive Screening → Sign Contract (≈ 978)
   - Option 3: Screen Prospective Tenant → Sign Contract (≈ 1,792)
   - Ordering constraint (clarification): “Extensive Screening”, when present, occurs immediately after “Screen Prospective Tenant” (it never precedes “Screen Prospective Tenant” and is not interleaved with other pre-leasing steps).
   Notes about structural skips (from Petri net): silent transitions allow bypassing some intermediate steps to reach the decision place. While logs do not prominently show “Apply → Sign Contract” directly, the mined control-flow allows such bypasses. For conformance in this AS-IS model we do not permit such skips to “Sign Contract”: “Sign Contract” must be preceded by “Screen Prospective Tenant” (optionally followed immediately by “Extensive Screening”) and by the earlier steps in their fixed order.

9) Sign Contract → 10) Move In
   - Sign Contract → Move In (≈ 2,770)
   - Ordering constraint (clarification): “Sign Contract” is the final step of the pre-leasing pipeline; no pre-leasing activity (e.g., “View The Property”, “Hand In The Paperwork”, “Check Paperwork”, “Screen Prospective Tenant”, “Extensive Screening”) may occur after signing.

Post-leasing recurring rent cycle (repeats until termination)
11) Recurring month-to-month gateway (implicit via loop transitions)
   - Path A: Pay Rent
       • Move In → Pay Rent (≈ 2,656)
       • Pay Rent → Pay Rent (self-loop, very frequent: ≈ 14,819) indicating repeated rent cycles.
       • From Pay Rent, a termination branch to “Tenant Cancels Appartment” (≈ 2,680) ends the case.
   - Path B: Miss Rent Payment
       • From Move In, a smaller fraction goes to Miss Rent Payment (≈ 114), and from ongoing cycles Pay Rent → Miss Rent Payment (≈ 770).
       • Miss Rent Payment → Issue Warning (≈ 945)
       • Issue Warning branches:
           – Accept Late Payment (≈ 855), typically → Pay Rent (≈ 794), but can → Miss Rent Payment again (≈ 61), creating a rework loop.
           – Evict Tenant (≈ 90) → end.
   - Structural notes (from Petri net): after Accept Late Payment, silent routing allows either returning to the regular payment place or re-entering the miss-payment branch, enabling repeated late cycles. After each paid cycle, a silent choice can lead either to another Pay Rent, to cancellation, or (less commonly) to a missed payment in the next cycle.

Exception handling and error paths
- Early rejections: permissible at multiple points (after Apply; after View; after Screening; after Extensive Screening).
- Late payments: handled via Issue Warning, with possible acceptance of late payment or escalation to eviction.
- Evictions: rare but terminal (≈ 90 after warning).
- Tenant cancellations: occur after paid cycles (observed Pay Rent → Tenant Cancels Appartment ≈ 2,680) and terminate the process.

Rework loops and escalation procedures
- Rent cycle rework: Accept Late Payment → Pay Rent, with occasional return to Miss Rent Payment (61), indicating repeated delinquency for some tenants.
- Ongoing Pay Rent self-loop (14,819) captures normal monthly recurrence.

End conditions and outcomes
- Reject Prospective Tenant (terminal) — can occur after Apply, View, Screen, or Extensive Screening.
- Tenant Cancels Appartment (terminal) — most commonly after one or more paid rent cycles.
- Evict Tenant (terminal) — after Miss Rent Payment → Issue Warning, if unresolved.

Key challenges and pain points
- High attrition: ≈ 72% of applicants are rejected, many after substantial effort (viewing, paperwork, screening), causing wasted capacity.
- Structural bypasses: the Petri net allows skipping directly to the decision place, creating a governance risk that a contract could be signed without full completion of viewing/paperwork/screening (even if rarely observed). For conformance purposes in this model, such skips to “Sign Contract” are not allowed; the pre-leasing order must be respected (see ordering constraints above).
- Late-payment rework: repeated late payments are accepted frequently (≈ 855 warnings leading to ≈ 794 accepted late payments), creating a loop that can perpetuate delinquency; escalation to eviction is inconsistent (≈ 90) relative to warnings issued.
- Cycle-time hotspots: Appointment scheduling and viewing latency (~82k per edge), paperwork-to-screening latency (~38–44k), and extensive screening adds further delay, impacting applicant experience.
- Termination management: Tenant cancellations are numerous and occur after paid cycles; there is no explicit control in the observed model to ensure all balances are settled before cancellation (silent routing may allow cancellation despite recent misses).
