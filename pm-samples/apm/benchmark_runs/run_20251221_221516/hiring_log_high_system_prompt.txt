Business Process Description:
AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- This document describes the observed (AS-IS) candidate selection process mined from event-log / Petri-net and DFG outputs. The process begins when an applicant hands in a job application and ends when the case reaches one of the terminal outcomes (Make Job Offer or Application Rejected). The purpose of the process is to screen applicants, assess technical and behavioral fit, perform background checks, and either make an offer or reject the candidate.

Key stakeholders and roles involved (inferred from activity names)
- Applicant: submits application and participates in interviews and checks.
- Recruiter / HR screener: performs Telephonic Screening and coordinates scheduling (Telephonic Screening, Screening Interview, scheduling background checks).
- Screening Interviewer / HR: conducts the initial screening interview.
- Technical Interviewers: Coding Interview, Frontend Interview, Backend Interview, ML Interview (teams or designated technical interviewers).
- Behavioral Interviewer / Hiring Manager: conducts behavioral interview(s) and final assessment.
- Background Check Team / Vendor: executes Background Check and Extensive Background Check.
- Hiring Decision Authority: makes the final decision and sends the Make Job Offer action (may be Hiring Manager or HR depending on case).
- Applicant Tracking System (ATS) / Process Owner (systemic): records application events and state transitions.

Detailed step-by-step process flow (observed behavior)
Start conditions and trigger
1. Start: Hand In Job Application is the observed start event (source->Hand In Job Application in the Petri net; DFG shows Hand In -> Telephonic Screening frequency = 8799).
   - From the data provided, total Hand In outgoing count appears to be 10,000 cases (Hand In -> Telephonic Screening 8,799; Hand In -> Application Rejected 1,201). This document uses these counts as the basis for relative discussion.

Main observed routing and activities
2. Telephonic Screening (Telephonic Screening receives 8,799 direct follows from Hand In)
   - Telephonic Screening is the primary gate: from Telephonic Screening candidates proceed to:
     - Background Check (direct follows = 3,961)
     - Screening Interview (direct follows = 3,848)
     - Direct Application Rejected (direct follows = 990)
   - Observed split: roughly 45% to Background Check, 44% to Screening Interview, 11% rejected at phone-screen.

3. Screening Interview
   - Screening Interview has several observed next steps (DFG):
     - Screening Interview -> Background Check (freq = 3,961)
     - Screening Interview -> Coding Interview (freq = 1,517)
     - Screening Interview -> Application Rejected (freq = 1,389)
     - Screening Interview -> Extensive Background Check (freq = 1,055)
   - Note: because Background Check <-> Screening Interview appear in both directions in the DFG and in the Petri net there are tau splits and skip transitions, the process shows interleaving and rework between screening and background checks.

4. Background Checks
   - Background Check (standard) is observed to lead to:
     - Coding Interview (freq = 1,413)
     - Extensive Background Check (freq = 1,044)
     - Application Rejected (freq = 1,391)
   - Extensive Background Check has strong direct-follows to Application Rejected (freq = 1,125) but also a significant number to Coding Interview (freq = 974), indicating rework/parallelism or escalation behaviours.
   - There are observed optional/skip transitions (Petri net skip_* nodes) implying that background checks may be skipped or retried in many cases.

5. Technical interview sequence (Coding, Frontend, Backend, ML)
   - Common technical progression paths include:
     - Screening Interview -> Coding Interview (1,517)
     - Coding Interview -> Frontend Interview (1,513)
     - Coding Interview -> Backend Interview (701)
     - Coding Interview -> ML Interview (1,304)
     - Frontend <-> Backend and Frontend <-> ML interchanges are also frequent (Frontend -> ML 878; ML -> Frontend 913; Frontend -> Backend 529; Backend -> Frontend 487; Backend -> ML 152; ML -> Backend 121), showing non-linear ordering and re-sequencing of technical interviews.
   - Many longer candidate journeys include several technical rounds in various orders and combinations (see variants list where long sequences with Coding->Frontend->ML->Behavioral appear). These long multi-interview variants are less frequent but have very high performance values (long cycle times).

6. Behavioral Interview and repetitions
   - Behavioral Interview often appears late in the sequence and frequently repeats (Behavioral Interview -> Behavioral Interview freq = 1,032). Behavioral Interview also directly goes to Make Job Offer (freq = 1,850).
   - There are many cases where Behavioral Interview is called after Application Rejected (Application Rejected -> Behavioral Interview freq = 740): this is a major data / process anomaly (either re-evaluation after rejection or mislabelled events in the log).

7. Decision and outcomes
   - Make Job Offer is an observed terminal action (Make Job Offer -> Make Job Offer self-loop freq = 861; Make Job Offer -> Application Rejected freq = 110). Repeated Make Job Offer or Make Job Offer -> Application Rejected indicates offer retraction, duplicate records, or follow-up administrative steps.
   - Application Rejected is a frequent terminal event (many variants end in Application Rejected). There are also anomalous transitions where Application Rejected is followed by Make Job Offer (freq = 690) or by Behavioral Interview (740) or self-loops (654), indicating inconsistent decision enforcement or data quality issues.

Parallelism, optional steps and invisible transitions (from Petri net)
- The Petri net shows multiple tau splits and joins (tauSplit_4, tauSplit_9, tauSplit_14, many skip_* transitions). These represent optional branches and invisible routing.
- Observed optional behaviors include skipping of interviews or checks and multiple initial loop constructs indicating rework or retries (init_loop nodes).
- The presence of many skip_* transitions indicates that many activities (coding, backend, frontend, ML interviews, background checks) are optional and combined in many different permutations — this causes high variability.

Exception handling, rework loops and escalation
- Rework loops are visible as technical interviews re-ordered (e.g., Frontend -> ML -> Backend -> Frontend), repeated Behavioral interviews, and repeated Make Job Offer events.
- Escalations to Extensive Background Check occur frequently after a standard Background Check or Screening Interview; this creates long additional delay and frequently ends in rejection.
- There is evidence of manual overrides (Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer) and appeals or log inconsistencies (Application Rejected -> Behavioral Interview). These are exceptions that bypass the expected terminal semantics.

End conditions and outcomes
- Terminal outcomes observed in the data: Make Job Offer (offer made, may be repeated) and Application Rejected (candidate rejected). The Petri net final marking is sink; tauJoin_15 -> sink indicates multiple pathways can converge into completion.

Process metrics and performance characteristics (observed values from DFG and variants)
- Base counts (inferred): total Hand In Job Application ~ 10,000 cases (8,799 progressed to Telephonic Screening; 1,201 were rejected immediately).
- Telephonic Screening -> Background Check: freq = 3,961 (approx. 45% of phone screens). Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview: freq = 3,848 (~44%). Telephonic Screening -> Application Rejected = 990 (~11%).
- Key direct-follows with high frequency: Behavioral Interview -> Make Job Offer = 1,850; Screening -> Background Check = 3,961; Coding -> Frontend = 1,513; Coding -> ML = 1,304.
- Long-running multi-interview variants (rare) have very high performance numbers (values ~530,000 in the variants list). Many direct-follows show high performance (e.g., Coding Interview -> Screening Interview shows 129,892.231) indicating significant waiting / throughput time when rework occurs.
- Rework indicators: self-loops and bidirectional edges (Behavioral repeated 1,032; Make Job Offer repeated 861; Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer 690) point to process instability and data or decision inconsistencies.

Key challenges and pain points (derived from process evidence)
- High variability and lack of standardized interview sequences: many permutations of technical interviews (coding, frontend, backend, ML) cause scheduling complexity and candidate drop-off.
- Costly and late background checks: Background Check and Extensive Background Check are interleaved with interviews and sometimes executed multiple times, leading to waste when performed before final selection.
- Long cycle times and bottlenecks for multi-round candidates: the long variants have very high performance values, indicating that candidates in these pathways wait a long time or experience rework.
- Decision inconsistencies and data-quality issues: transitions such as Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer and Application Rejected -> Behavioral Interview indicate either manual overrides without proper logging, appeals, or corrupted logs.
- Repeated behavioral interviews and duplicate offers: behavioral interview self-loops and Make Job Offer self-loops indicate inefficient re-scheduling or administrative duplication.
- Poor gating: Telephonic Screening sometimes leads simultaneously to both Screening Interview and Background Check or to neither (skip transitions), making throughput unpredictable.

Observability and data quality notes
- The Petri net contains many invisible transitions (skip_*, tauSplit_*, tauJoin_*) — these correspond to choices and optional skips in the process. Some of these invisible transitions likely reflect logging gaps rather than real silent steps.
- The DFG and variant counts should be interpreted as direct-follows counts from the log; conditional probabilities derived from them may be skewed by loops and back-and-forth transitions (non-Markovian behavior).

Summary of AS-IS conclusions
- The observed hiring process is highly flexible but also highly variable, leading to long cycle times for complex candidate journeys, duplicated activities, and inconsistent decision outcomes.
- Main sources of waste: rework across interviews, early/duplicated background checks, manual decision reversals, and lack of a firm gating policy enforcing terminal states.

Recommendations (high-level, to be refined in IDEAL model)
- Standardize and gate rounds to reduce permutations (define a canonical technical assessment flow).
- Move full background checks to after a conditional offer or restrict Extensive Background Check to flagged cases only.
- Enforce decision terminality (Application Rejected should be final unless a formal appeal/rehire process is recorded).
- Reduce duplicate behavioral interviews and duplicate offer events by centralizing scheduling and Offer creation in the ATS.
