### AS-IS Process Model

#### Process Overview and Purpose
The AS-IS process model captures the observed behavior of an emergency medical treatment process, as discovered from event logs using the inductive miner (Petri net), directly-follows graph (DFG), and abstract process variants. This process handles patient arrivals at an Emergency Room (ER) or via Fire Department (FD), involving registration, multiple optional examinations, diagnosis, iterative treatment with potential rework loops due to unsuccessful treatments, and eventual discharge. The model reflects real-world variability, including skips, alternative paths, and rework, leading to inefficiencies such as prolonged cycles in cases with multiple treatment failures.

**Purpose**: To provide emergency care, examination, diagnosis, and treatment for patients arriving at ER or transported by FD, culminating in discharge upon resolution.

#### Key Stakeholders and Roles Involved
Roles are not explicitly specified in the source models. Activities are performed by undifferentiated medical staff (e.g., receptionists for registration, nurses/doctors for examinations, treatments).

#### Detailed Step-by-Step Process Flow
**Start Conditions and Triggers**: Patient arrival at ER or FD triggers the process. Initial marking in Petri net: source place with 1 token.

1. **Registration (Exclusive Gateway - XOR Split)**:
   - Register at ER (frequency in variants: high, e.g., many paths start here).
   - OR Register at FD (similar frequency).
   - Both lead to a common point (p_3 in Petri net). Performance: ~40,000 time units.

2. **Initial Assessment/Examination Phase (Inclusive/Optional Branches with Skips)**:
   - From post-registration:
     - Branch to **Examination** → leads to decision for Thorough Examination.
     - OR **Expert Examination** (often for FD/ER patients, freq ~2888/2866 in DFG) → joins to same point (p_4).
     - OR silent skip (skip_1) directly to p_4.
   - From p_4:
     - **Thorough Examination** (freq high in variants, e.g., 5922 DFG) → p_5.
     - OR silent skip_2 → p_5.
   - Performance for examinations: ~40,000-60,000 time units.
   - Note: Expert Examination sometimes skips Thorough (DFG: Expert → Diagnosis freq 2117), indicating skips.

3. **Loop Entry (via init_loop_3 silent transition from p_5 to p_7)**:
   - Enters the diagnosis-treatment loop.

4. **Diagnosis-Treatment Rework Loop (Loop Construct)**:
   - **Diagnosis** (freq 11746 in DFG to Treatment) → p_9 → **Treatment** → p_8.
     - Performance: Diagnosis ~60,000; Treatment ~60,000.
   - From p_8 (post-Treatment decision):
     - **Treatment unsuccessful** (freq 1746) → back to p_7 (loop to Diagnosis). Can repeat multiple times (variants show 1-4+ loops, e.g., up to 480,000-800,000 total time).
     - OR silent skip_5 → p_6.
   - From p_6:
     - **Treatment successful** → p_11.
     - OR silent skip_6 → p_11.
   - Observed direct **Treatment → Discharge** (DFG freq 995, performance ~100,000), likely via combined skips bypassing success/failure declaration.
   - Loop allows rework; variants show paths with 0- multiple iterations (e.g., freq 9005 successful without loop, 1746 with one failure).

5. **Finalization**:
   - Convergence at p_11 → **Discharge** → sink (final marking).
   - Discharge freq high after successful (9005).

**Decision Points**:
- Registration: ER vs FD (no clear criteria; both common).
- Post-registration: Examination vs Expert vs skip (criteria implicit: severity?).
- Post-Examination: Thorough vs skip.
- Post-Treatment: Success (proceed), Unsuccessful (rework), or skip to success/Discharge.

**Parallel Activities and Synchronization**: No explicit parallels; all sequential with XOR/inclusive choices and joins (e.g., p_4 merges paths).

**Exception Handling and Error Paths**: Unsuccessful treatment triggers rework loop (no escalation shown). Rare deep loops (e.g., 3+ failures, low freq like 6-42).

**Rework Loops and Escalation Procedures**: Primary loop: Unsuccessful → Diagnosis → Treatment (up to multiple times). No explicit escalation; prolonged cases via repeated loops.

**End Conditions and Outcomes**:
- Positive: Treatment successful → Discharge (most common, ~9005 freq).
- Alternative: Direct Treatment → Discharge (~995), possibly incomplete cases.
- Sink reached upon Discharge.

#### Process Metrics and Performance Characteristics
- Total variants: 30+ abstract paths, dominated by successful no-rework (e.g., top path freq 1539).
- Average case time: ~260,000-320,000 time units for straight paths; up to 800,000+ for multi-loop.
- Bottlenecks: Treatment-Diagnosis loop (high freq unsuccessful 1746); Examination sequences.
- Throughput: High volume (e.g., Diagnosis-Treatment 11746).

#### Key Challenges and Pain Points
- High variability: Multiple examination paths (Exam/Expert/Thorough with skips), leading to inconsistent care.
- Frequent rework: Treatment unsuccessful loops (add ~120,000-200,000 time per iteration), impacting cycle time (tail cases 640,000+).
- Skips and shortcuts: Allow bypassing success checks or exams, risking quality (e.g., direct Discharge post-Treatment).
- No standardization: Dual registration points; optional exams without criteria.
- Potential compliance issues: Direct discharges without success confirmation.
- Long-tail variants: Rare but time-intensive multi-failure paths (e.g., 4 loops, freq 5-6).
