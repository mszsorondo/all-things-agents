AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: End-to-end hiring pipeline from candidate application through assessments, background checks, and either rejection or job offer. The description reflects observed behavior from the mined Petri net, DFG, and abstract variants, including optionality, concurrency, rework loops, and contradictory paths that occur in practice.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Roles are not explicitly specified in the source material. Activities suggest participation by recruiting/coordination staff, technical interviewers, and background check providers; however, no formal role assignments are defined in the input.

Detailed step-by-step process flow
1) Start conditions and trigger
- Trigger: Candidate submits "Hand In Job Application" (start of every case).

2) Early triage options (observed)
- After application, one of the following patterns is observed:
  a) Immediate rejection:
     - Direct transition to "Application Rejected" (frequency ≈ 1,201).
  b) Telephonic Screening:
     - "Hand In Job Application" → "Telephonic Screening" (frequency ≈ 8,799; median performance on this edge ≈ 82k time units).
     - From Telephonic Screening, two observed routes emerge toward initial assessment: either to "Screening Interview" first or to "Background Check" first, with similar volumes in both directions.

3) Initial assessment (Screening Interview and Background Check running in parallel)
- From Telephonic Screening, the model splits to allow both "Screening Interview" and "Background Check" to proceed in any order; these two activities synchronize afterward (parallel split and join):
  - Telephonic → Background Check → Screening (frequency ≈ 3,961; time along edges ≈ 82k; the pair completes with zero-lag join, per DFG).
  - Telephonic → Screening → Background Check (frequency ≈ 3,848; symmetric behavior).
- Outcomes after the parallel join:
  - Optional "Extensive Background Check" may be run. It frequently leads to rejection (edge to rejection frequency ≈ 1,125; long lead time ≈ 171k).
  - Alternatively, proceed toward technical evaluation or, in some traces, skip directly toward the late-stage decisioning split (see Step 5).
- Rejection may occur from either screening or background check outcomes:
  - "Background Check" → "Application Rejected" (frequency ≈ 1,391; ≈ 155k time units).
  - "Screening Interview" → "Application Rejected" (frequency ≈ 1,389; ≈ 156k time units).

4) Technical evaluation (optional and configurable)
- "Coding Interview" may occur after the initial assessments:
  - Screening → Coding (frequency ≈ 1,517; ≈ 130k time units).
  - Background Check → Coding (frequency ≈ 1,413; ≈ 130k time units).
  - Extensive Background Check → Coding (frequency ≈ 974; ≈ 124k time units).
- From Coding, the following are observed:
  - Direct rejection (Coding → Application Rejected; frequency ≈ 386; ≈ 81k).
  - One or more role-specific technical interviews: "ML Interview", "Frontend Interview", "Backend Interview". The Petri net indicates an inclusive parallel split allowing any subset of these three, with each also being skippable. The DFG exhibits immediate back-and-forth between these interviews (e.g., ML ↔ Frontend, Frontend ↔ Backend) indicating concurrent or repeated scheduling/order changes (zero performance edges suggest back-to-back execution or concurrency capture).
- Synchronization point after role-specific interviews: all chosen/active branches converge before the process proceeds.

5) Late-stage split into two concurrent branches (contradictory but observed)
- After the technical stage (or earlier if upstream steps were skipped), the model performs a silent split into two parallel branches that are expected to complete before the case ends:

  Branch A: Application Rejection loop
  - The activity "Application Rejected" can be executed zero or more times.
  - A self-loop on "Application Rejected" is present (frequency ≈ 654), indicating repeated rejection markings on the same case.
  - At any point, this branch can be exited without performing a rejection (skip) or after one/multiple rejections, to allow overall process completion once the other branch also completes or is skipped.

  Branch B: Behavioral Interview and Offer loop
  - "Behavioral Interview" may occur and is observed to loop on itself (frequency ≈ 1,032; very short loop times in logs, suggesting re-interviews/reschedules).
  - After or instead of Behavioral Interview, "Make Job Offer" may occur. "Make Job Offer" also exhibits a self-loop (frequency ≈ 861), consistent with iterative or renegotiated offers.
  - This branch can be skipped entirely, or it can complete after one or more Behavioral and Offer iterations.

- Synchronization and end:
  - Both branches must be resolved (either executed and finished or skipped) before the process ends. A silent join synchronizes Branch A (rejection loop branch) with Branch B (behavioral/offer branch), after which the case terminates.

6) Exception handling and error paths (observed)
- Multiple mid-process rejections: Rejection can occur at several stages (post-screening, post-background, post-coding, or during the late-stage rejection branch). Rejection events may recur on the same case (self-loop).
- Contradictory sequences observed: "Application Rejected" followed later by "Behavioral Interview" and/or "Make Job Offer" (e.g., variants where Application Rejected is followed by Behavioral Interview or even a job offer). This indicates that the rejection mark is not terminal in the AS-IS behavior.
- Interview rework: Technical sub-interviews (ML/Frontend/Backend) appear to repeat or be reordered, suggesting rescheduling or widening/narrowing focus mid-process.
- Offer rework: Multiple offers can be generated in succession in the same case, implying renegotiation or corrective issuance.

7) Rework loops and escalation procedures (observed)
- Behavioral Interview → Behavioral Interview loop (frequency ≈ 1,032) without explicit cap.
- Make Job Offer → Make Job Offer loop (frequency ≈ 861) without explicit cap.
- Application Rejected self-loop (frequency ≈ 654), enabling repeated rejection markings.
- Technical tracks show bidirectional flows (e.g., ML ↔ Frontend, Frontend ↔ Backend) implying repeated or concurrent technical evaluations before synchronization.

8) End conditions and outcomes
- The process ends when the two late-stage branches are synchronized (silent join). Because one branch can be skipped, the practical end states realized include:
  - Rejected only: one or more rejections on Branch A, Branch B skipped → end.
  - Offered only: Behavioral/Offer branch completed, rejection branch skipped → end.
  - Both occurred: at least one rejection event and later behavioral/offer events also occurred, followed by join → end (contradictory but observed in logs and supported by the net structure).

Process metrics and performance characteristics (from DFG and variants)
- High-volume early path: Application → Telephonic Screening (≈ 8,799).
- Early rejections:
  - Application → Application Rejected (≈ 1,201; ≈ 146k time units).
  - Telephonic → Application Rejected (≈ 990; ≈ 143k).
- Screening/Background concurrency:
  - Telephonic → Background → Screening path (≈ 3,961), and Telephonic → Screening → Background (≈ 3,848), both with ≈ 82k on the edges and zero-lag join.
- Rejection edges and latencies:
  - Background → Application Rejected (≈ 1,391; ≈ 155k).
  - Screening → Application Rejected (≈ 1,389; ≈ 156k).
  - Extensive Background → Application Rejected (≈ 1,125; ≈ 171k).
  - Coding → Application Rejected (≈ 386; ≈ 81k).
- Technical track transitions:
  - Coding → Frontend (≈ 1,513; ≈ 81k).
  - Coding → ML (≈ 1,304; ≈ 81k).
  - Coding → Backend (≈ 701; ≈ 81k).
  - Inter-interview handoffs show zero-time edges (e.g., ML ↔ Frontend, Frontend ↔ Backend), indicating concurrency or immediate successive scheduling.
- Later-stage loops:
  - Behavioral Interview self-loop (≈ 1,032; ≈ 47 time units on the loop edge).
  - Make Job Offer self-loop (≈ 861; ≈ 71 time units on the loop edge).
- Long-cycle variants:
  - Representative end-to-end variants that reach offer stages often show ≈ 530k cumulative performance before apparent completion; long-tail cases include additional rejections or repeated interviews.

Key challenges and pain points
- Non-terminal rejection: "Application Rejected" appears mid-process and can be followed by interviews or even offers; this undermines data integrity and governance.
- Parallel contradiction: A parallel split enables the rejection branch and the behavioral/offer branch to co-exist, producing traces where a candidate is both rejected and later advanced.
- Unbounded rework: Repeated Behavioral Interviews and repeated Offers are permitted without cap, driving long cycle times and resource contention.
- Over-early screening checks: Background checks (and even Extensive Background Checks) occur before core technical fit is established, contributing to avoidable cost and delay when candidates are later rejected for skills.
- Fragmented technical track: Optional, inclusive parallel interviews (ML/Frontend/Backend) with frequent reordering/repetition hamper predictability.
- Latency hotspots: Large performance durations around rejection edges (≈ 146k–171k) and long end-to-end cycles (≈ 530k) denote bottlenecks and rework overhead.
