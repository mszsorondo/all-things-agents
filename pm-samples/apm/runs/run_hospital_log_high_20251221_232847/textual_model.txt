# AS-IS Process Model

## Process Overview and Purpose
The current process represents the patient treatment workflow in a healthcare setting, detailing the sequence of actions taken from patient registration to discharge. This model captures the decision-making points, variations in treatment, and the potential for rework or examination based on both successful and unsuccessful treatment outcomes.

## Key Stakeholders and Roles Involved
- **Patients**: Individuals seeking medical treatment who initiate the process.
- **Front Desk (FD)**: Responsible for registering patients and directing them to appropriate examinations or expert evaluations.
- **Emergency Room (ER)**: Alternative registration point that directs patients to evaluations and examinations.
- **Healthcare Professionals**: Staff involved in conducting examinations, diagnosing patients, and administering treatment.

## Detailed Step-by-Step Process Flow

### Start Conditions and Triggers
The process begins when a patient registers either at the Front Desk (FD) or the Emergency Room (ER).

### Process Activities/Tasks in Sequence

1. **Registration**:
   - **Register at FD** (optional)
   - **Register at ER** (optional)

2. **Examinations**:
   - After registration, the patient undergoes either:
     - **Expert Examination**
     - **Examination**
   
3. **Further Evaluation**:
   - From both *Expert Examination* and *Examination*, the following may occur:
     - **Thorough Examination** (mandatory following initial examinations)
     - **Diagnosis** (follows Thorough Examination)
     - **Skipping examinations or evaluations** is allowed; this can lead directly to subsequent steps but may impact treatment quality.

4. **Treatment**:
   - Upon diagnosis, the patient progresses to **Treatment**.
     - Here, the treatment can lead to:
       - **Treatment successful**: Transition to discharge.
       - **Treatment unsuccessful**: May result in re-diagnosis and the necessity of further treatment.

5. **Discharge**:
   - Successful treatment leads directly to **Discharge**.
   - If treatment is unsuccessful, it may necessitate returning to **Diagnosis** and looping back into further treatment actions, potentially repeating the Treatment until successful.

### Decision Points with Criteria and Possible Outcomes
- **After Diagnosis**:
  - if Treatment is **Successful**: Proceed to Discharge.
  - if Treatment is **Unsuccessful**: 
    - Revert to Diagnosis for reevaluation, then return to Treatment.
  
### Parallel Activities and Synchronization Points
- The model allows patients to undertake both the registration at FD and ER simultaneously, as well as to engage in examinations and expert evaluations in parallel. 
- Both types of examinations can lead into thorough examinations and subsequently into diagnosis.

### Exception Handling and Error Paths
- Paths can split into several outcomes based on the treatment success:
  - Emergency scenarios may escalate directly to advanced examinations and treatments without initial examinations if warranted.
  - Unsuccessful treatments require looping back to diagnosis.

### Rework Loops and Escalation Procedures
- Repeat Diagnosis after unsuccessful Treatment.
- Option to undergo additional assessments during the treatment phase if initial diagnostics are not conclusive.

### End Conditions and Outcomes
- The process officially ends once the patient is discharged after a successful treatment unless complications arise requiring reevaluation.

## Process Metrics and Performance Characteristics
- Documented frequencies of each task completion, highlighting common paths taken:
  - For instance, high instances of "Register at FD" leading to "Expert Examination" and high-performance metrics on treatment timelines were observed.
- Overall, the process demonstrates several common treatment pathways, where the average completion metrics show variability based on the route taken - fluctuations noted further differentiate between successful and unsuccessful treatment durations.

## Key Challenges and Pain Points Observed in the Current Process
- High frequency of treatment unsuccessful cases indicating a need for a more rigorous diagnostic path.
- Potential for significant delays caused by rework loops, particularly when unsuccessful treatments lead to repeated diagnosis and treatment.
- Variability in patient experience based on choice of registration point (FD vs. ER) leading to differing wait times and treatment outcomes.

This AS-IS process model captures all observed variants ensuring full fidelity to the actual process behaviors and needs while providing insights into performance and potential areas for improvement.
