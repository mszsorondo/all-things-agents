IDEAL Process Model

# Process Overview and Purpose

The IDEAL process model represents an optimized, standardized healthcare patient treatment process that addresses the inefficiencies, inconsistencies, and quality issues identified in the AS-IS model. This improved process maintains the necessary flexibility to handle both emergency and non-emergency cases while establishing clear protocols, reducing rework, improving diagnostic accuracy, and ensuring consistent quality of care.

The IDEAL process achieves:
- Differentiated pathways based on patient acuity and condition complexity
- Standardized examination protocols with clear decision criteria
- Proactive treatment planning to reduce failure rates
- Intelligent rework handling with escalation mechanisms
- Complete documentation and outcome tracking
- Reduced cycle times through elimination of unnecessary waits and bottlenecks
- Improved patient outcomes through evidence-based pathway selection

# Key Stakeholders and Roles

**Front Desk Registration Staff**: Register and perform initial triage for non-emergency patients, assign priority levels, and route to appropriate pathways.

**Emergency Room Triage Nurse**: Perform rapid assessment of emergency patients, assign acuity levels, and route to appropriate care pathways.

**Triage Coordinator**: Oversee pathway assignment decisions and ensure appropriate resource allocation based on patient needs.

**General Examination Physician**: Conduct standard medical examinations for lower-complexity cases.

**Specialist Physician**: Perform expert examinations for complex cases requiring specialized assessment.

**Diagnostic Team**: Conduct thorough examinations and diagnostic workups when required by clinical protocols.

**Senior Diagnostic Physician**: Responsible for diagnosis formulation, treatment plan development, and quality assurance of diagnostic accuracy.

**Treatment Team**: Execute evidence-based treatment protocols tailored to specific diagnoses.

**Treatment Monitoring Nurse**: Track treatment progress, identify early warning signs of treatment complications or failures.

**Senior Medical Officer**: Review treatment failures, conduct root cause analysis, and approve alternative treatment protocols.

**Multidisciplinary Case Review Team**: Convened for complex or repeatedly failing cases to determine optimal care pathway.

**Discharge Planning Coordinator**: Ensure complete documentation, arrange follow-up care, and coordinate safe discharge.

**Quality Assurance Team**: Monitor process compliance, track outcomes, and identify improvement opportunities.

# Detailed Step-by-Step Process Flow

## Start Conditions and Triggers

The process initiates when a patient presents for care at one of two entry points, with enhanced triage at each:

1. **Register at FD (Front Desk) with Initial Triage**
   - Patient arrives for scheduled appointment or non-emergency walk-in
   - Registration staff collect demographic and insurance information
   - Brief initial assessment conducted (5-10 minutes)
   - Patient assigned priority level: Routine, Moderate Priority, or Urgent
   - Target: Complete registration and triage within 20 minutes

2. **Register at ER (Emergency Room) with Acuity Assessment**
   - Patient arrives requiring emergency evaluation
   - Triage nurse performs rapid assessment using standardized acuity scale
   - Vital signs measured and documented
   - Patient assigned acuity level: Critical, Emergent, Urgent, Semi-urgent
   - Target: Complete triage within 10 minutes for emergent/critical, 15 minutes for others

Both pathways include structured decision support that determines initial routing.

## Phase 1: Intelligent Examination Pathway Routing

After registration and triage, patients are routed to appropriate examination pathways based on standardized clinical criteria:

### Decision Point: Examination Type Selection

**Criteria-Based Routing Logic**:
- **Patient acuity level** (from triage assessment)
- **Presenting symptoms complexity** (using standardized complexity scoring)
- **Known medical history** (chronic conditions, previous treatments)
- **Specialist availability** (real-time scheduling system)

**Routing Outcomes**:

**Route A: Standard Care Pathway** (for routine, straightforward cases - approximately 35% of patients)
- Lower acuity (Routine, Semi-urgent)
- Single-system symptoms
- No complex medical history
- → Proceed to General Examination

**Route B: Complex Care Pathway** (for cases requiring specialized assessment - approximately 45% of patients)
- Moderate to high acuity (Urgent, Emergent)
- Multi-system involvement or specialized condition
- Significant medical history
- → Proceed to Expert Examination

**Route C: High-Complexity Pathway** (for critical or diagnostically challenging cases - approximately 20% of patients)
- Critical acuity
- Life-threatening presentation
- Rare or complex diagnostic picture
- → Proceed directly to Thorough Examination (comprehensive diagnostic workup)

## Phase 2: Structured Examination Process

### Option A: Standard Care Pathway

**Step 2A.1: General Examination**
- Performed by general examination physician
- Focused clinical assessment based on presenting complaint
- Duration: 30-45 minutes
- Documentation: Structured examination template completed
- Output: Clinical findings, preliminary assessment

**Step 2A.2: Complexity Re-Assessment**
- Physician evaluates if findings require escalation
- Decision criteria:
  - Unexpected findings suggesting complex condition
  - Symptoms don't match initial assessment
  - Diagnostic uncertainty requiring specialist input

**Decision Point A**:
- **If straightforward case confirmed** → Proceed directly to Diagnosis (approximately 60% of Route A patients)
- **If complexity identified** → Escalate to Thorough Examination (approximately 40% of Route A patients)

### Option B: Complex Care Pathway

**Step 2B.1: Expert Examination**
- Performed by specialist physician appropriate to condition
- Comprehensive assessment using specialized techniques
- Duration: 45-60 minutes
- Documentation: Detailed specialist assessment report
- Output: Expert clinical findings, differential diagnosis list

**Step 2B.2: Diagnostic Confidence Assessment**
- Specialist evaluates diagnostic certainty
- Decision criteria:
  - Clear diagnostic picture vs. ambiguous presentation
  - Need for additional diagnostic procedures
  - Risk stratification

**Decision Point B**:
- **If high diagnostic confidence** → Proceed directly to Diagnosis (approximately 40% of Route B patients)
- **If additional workup needed** → Proceed to Thorough Examination (approximately 60% of Route B patients)

### Option C: High-Complexity Pathway

**Step 2C: Thorough Examination (Comprehensive Diagnostic Workup)**
- Performed by diagnostic team with appropriate specialists
- Comprehensive multi-system evaluation
- May include imaging, laboratory studies, specialized testing
- Duration: 60-90 minutes (plus test processing time)
- Documentation: Complete diagnostic workup report
- Output: Comprehensive clinical data, refined differential diagnosis
- → Proceeds directly to Diagnosis (100% of Route C patients)

**Convergence Point: All Pathways Lead to Diagnosis**

All three examination pathways converge before the diagnosis phase, but each carries forward different levels of clinical information completeness.

## Phase 3: Evidence-Based Diagnosis and Treatment Planning

**Step 3.1: Diagnosis Formulation**
- Senior diagnostic physician reviews all examination findings
- Applies evidence-based diagnostic criteria
- Utilizes clinical decision support systems
- Formulates primary diagnosis and considers differential diagnoses
- Duration: 30-45 minutes
- Documentation: Structured diagnosis report with confidence level

**Step 3.2: Treatment Plan Development**
- Physician develops evidence-based treatment plan
- Plan includes:
  - Primary treatment protocol based on diagnosis
  - Expected outcomes and success criteria
  - Monitoring parameters
  - Alternative treatment options if primary fails
  - Escalation triggers
- Treatment plan reviewed and approved by senior physician
- Duration: 20-30 minutes
- Target: Diagnosis to treatment initiation within 2 hours

**Step 3.3: Treatment Risk Assessment**
- Evaluate patient factors that may affect treatment success:
  - Contraindications or complications
  - Patient comorbidities
  - Resource availability for optimal treatment
  - Patient capability for treatment adherence
- Risk stratification: Low, Moderate, High risk of treatment failure
- High-risk cases flagged for enhanced monitoring

## Phase 4: Treatment Execution with Active Monitoring

**Step 4.1: Treatment Initiation**
- Treatment team executes approved treatment protocol
- Baseline measurements documented
- Patient and family education provided
- Duration varies by treatment type: typically 4-8 hours for acute treatments
- Documentation: Treatment administration records, patient response

**Step 4.2: Active Treatment Monitoring**
- Treatment monitoring nurse tracks patient response in real-time
- Regular assessment against expected response parameters
- Early warning system alerts for:
  - Adverse reactions
  - Lack of expected improvement
  - Unexpected deterioration
- Enables early intervention before complete treatment failure

**Step 4.3: Treatment Outcome Assessment**
- Clinical evaluation of treatment effectiveness
- Objective measurement against success criteria defined in treatment plan
- Documentation: Treatment outcome report with evidence

**Decision Point: Treatment Effectiveness Evaluation**

**Outcome A: Treatment Successful (Target: 90% of cases)**
- Patient shows expected clinical improvement
- Success criteria met
- No concerning complications
- → Proceed to Discharge Planning (primary pathway)

**Outcome B: Treatment Partially Successful / Uncertain (Target: <5% of cases)**
- Some improvement but incomplete response
- Success criteria partially met
- May benefit from treatment extension or modification
- → Trigger: Enhanced Monitoring Protocol
  - Extended observation period (4-6 hours)
  - Repeat assessment
  - If improvement continues → Proceed to Discharge Planning
  - If improvement plateaus or reverses → Route to Treatment Failure Protocol

**Outcome C: Treatment Unsuccessful (Target: <5% of cases)**
- No improvement or deterioration
- Success criteria not met
- → Route to Intelligent Rework Protocol (see Phase 5)

## Phase 5: Intelligent Rework Protocol (for Treatment Failures)

**This enhanced protocol replaces the simple loop-back in the AS-IS model.**

**Step 5.1: Treatment Failure Root Cause Analysis**
- Senior Medical Officer convened immediately
- Rapid review conducted (target: 30 minutes):
  - Was diagnosis accurate?
  - Was treatment protocol appropriate?
  - Were there patient-specific factors?
  - Were there execution issues?
  - Is this a treatment-resistant case?
- Documentation: Root cause analysis report

**Step 5.2: Failure Type Classification**

**Type 1: Diagnostic Uncertainty**
- Original diagnosis questioned
- → Re-evaluation pathway:
  - Additional diagnostic tests ordered
  - Specialist consultation if not already involved
  - Return to enhanced diagnostic assessment
  - New diagnosis formulated
  - Alternative treatment plan developed

**Type 2: Treatment Protocol Issue**
- Diagnosis confirmed correct
- Treatment selection or execution questioned
- → Protocol revision pathway:
  - Review alternative treatment options
  - Select second-line therapy
  - Consider combination approach
  - Modify for patient-specific factors

**Type 3: Treatment-Resistant Case**
- Diagnosis correct, protocol appropriate, but patient not responding
- → Escalation pathway:
  - Convene Multidisciplinary Case Review Team
  - Consider experimental or specialized treatments
  - Evaluate need for higher level of care or specialist facility transfer
  - Develop comprehensive alternative care plan

**Step 5.3: Alternative Treatment Execution**
- Implement revised treatment plan based on root cause analysis
- Enhanced monitoring protocol applied automatically
- Senior physician oversight required
- Duration: Variable based on treatment type
- Documentation: Revised treatment plan and rationale

**Step 5.4: Alternative Treatment Outcome Assessment**
- Same rigorous evaluation as initial treatment
- Decision Point:
  - **If successful** → Proceed to Discharge Planning
  - **If unsuccessful (second failure)** → Automatic escalation to Multidisciplinary Case Review Team
  
**Step 5.5: Multidisciplinary Case Review (for repeated failures)**
- Team composition: Senior physicians, relevant specialists, treatment team, care coordinator
- Comprehensive case review (target: 2-hour meeting within 24 hours of second failure)
- Decision outcomes:
  - Pursue advanced treatment option at current facility
  - Transfer to specialized treatment center
  - Palliative or supportive care pathway
  - Experimental protocol enrollment (with ethics approval)
- No patient experiences more than 2 treatment failures without multidisciplinary review

**Success Metrics for Rework Protocol**:
- First rework success rate: >85%
- Cases requiring second rework: <2%
- Cases requiring third rework: <0.5%
- Rework cycle time: 50% faster than initial treatment cycle due to focused approach

## Phase 6: Comprehensive Discharge Planning

**Step 6.1: Discharge Readiness Assessment**
- Multi-criteria evaluation:
  - Clinical stability confirmed
  - Treatment success documented
  - Patient understands care instructions
  - Home environment safe and appropriate
  - Follow-up appointments scheduled
  - Medications reconciled and provided
- Discharge coordinator ensures all criteria met
- No discharge without formal success documentation

**Step 6.2: Discharge Documentation**
- Complete medical record finalized
- Discharge summary prepared including:
  - Final diagnosis
  - Treatment provided and outcomes
  - Follow-up care plan
  - Patient education materials
  - Red flag symptoms requiring immediate return
- Duration: 30-45 minutes for documentation completion

**Step 6.3: Discharge Execution and Follow-up Arrangement**
- Patient and family education session (15-30 minutes)
- Written discharge instructions provided
- Follow-up appointments scheduled before discharge
- Primary care physician notification sent
- Care transition coordinator assigned for high-risk patients
- Patient provided with direct contact for questions

**Step 6.4: Post-Discharge Follow-up**
- 48-hour telephone follow-up for all patients
- 7-day follow-up appointment for high-risk or complex cases
- 30-day outcome tracking
- Readmission monitoring
- Satisfaction survey

## End Conditions and Outcomes

**Successful Process Completion**:
- Patient discharged with documented treatment success
- All clinical criteria met
- Complete documentation in medical record
- Follow-up care arranged
- Patient education completed

**Quality Metrics Captured**:
- Total cycle time from registration to discharge
- Treatment success rate (first attempt and overall)
- Number of rework cycles required
- Patient satisfaction score
- 30-day readmission rate
- Pathway adherence rate

# Exception Handling and Error Paths

## Emergency Deterioration During Process
**Trigger**: Patient condition deteriorates unexpectedly at any point

**Response Protocol**:
- Immediate medical response team activation
- Patient stabilized
- Escalation to appropriate level of care (ICU, OR, etc.)
- Original process suspended
- New care pathway initiated based on emergency condition
- Documentation of event and transition

## Diagnostic Test Failures or Delays
**Trigger**: Required diagnostic tests cannot be completed (equipment failure, patient factors, etc.)

**Response Protocol**:
- Alternative diagnostic methods evaluated
- Specialist consultation for diagnosis formulation with incomplete data
- Risk-benefit analysis for proceeding vs. delaying
- Documentation of limitation and clinical reasoning
- Enhanced monitoring if proceeding with uncertainty

## Treatment Resource Unavailability
**Trigger**: Optimal treatment cannot be provided due to resource constraints (medication shortage, equipment unavailable, specialist not available)

**Response Protocol**:
- Senior physician evaluates alternative treatments
- Risk assessment for alternative vs. delayed optimal treatment
- If safe alternative exists → Proceed with modification, document rationale
- If no safe alternative → Patient transferred to facility with resources
- No improvised treatments without senior physician approval

## Patient Refuses Treatment
**Trigger**: Patient declines recommended treatment

**Response Protocol**:
- Physician-patient discussion of risks and alternatives
- Documentation of informed refusal
- Alternative treatment options explored
- If patient maintains refusal → Supportive care pathway
- Discharge against medical advice protocol if appropriate
- Follow-up arranged for reconsideration

## Misrouting or Pathway Error
**Trigger**: Patient routed to inappropriate examination pathway

**Response Protocol**:
- Error identified through clinical review checkpoints
- Patient re-routed to appropriate pathway
- Additional assessments conducted as needed
- Incident report filed for process improvement
- No patient penalized for system error (no restart from beginning)

# Key Improvements from AS-IS Model

## 1. Intelligent Triage and Routing
**Improvement**: Structured decision criteria for examination pathway selection
**Impact**:
- Eliminates 50+ process variants down to 3 standardized pathways
- Ensures appropriate resource allocation
- Reduces unnecessary specialist consultations
- Accelerates straightforward cases
- Ensures complex cases receive appropriate attention

**Mechanism**:
- Standardized triage protocols with scoring
- Clinical decision support at routing points
- Real-time resource availability integration
- Regular protocol review and refinement

## 2. Proactive Treatment Planning
**Improvement**: Evidence-based treatment protocols with pre-planned alternatives
**Impact**:
- Reduces first-attempt failure rate from 23% to <10%
- Accelerates response to treatment failures
- Improves resource preparation
- Enhances patient communication

**Mechanism**:
- Clinical pathways for common diagnoses
- Treatment plan templates with built-in alternatives
- Risk assessment integrated into planning
- Peer review for high-risk cases

## 3. Intelligent Rework Protocol
**Improvement**: Root cause analysis and differentiated response to treatment failures
**Impact**:
- Reduces repeat failure rate by 50%+
- Accelerates rework cycle time by 50%+
- Eliminates indefinite looping
- Ensures escalation for complex cases
- Reduces total rework cases from 18% to <8%

**Mechanism**:
- Mandatory root cause analysis
- Failure type classification
- Escalation triggers and protocols
- Multidisciplinary team review for repeated failures
- Maximum 2 failures before automatic escalation

## 4. Elimination of Silent Transitions and Workarounds
**Improvement**: All process steps documented and justified

**Impact**:
