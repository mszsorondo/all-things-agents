IDEAL PROCESS MODEL

================================================================================
HEALTHCARE PATIENT JOURNEY PROCESS - TARGET STATE
================================================================================

PROCESS OVERVIEW AND PURPOSE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This IDEAL process model represents the optimized and standardized patient journey through the healthcare facility from initial registration through diagnosis, treatment, and discharge. The process maintains two entry channels (Emergency Room and Front Desk) but implements clear routing logic, standardized examination protocols, improved treatment effectiveness, controlled rework with escalation procedures, and mandatory outcome evaluation.

The target process eliminates inappropriate shortcuts, reduces treatment failure rates through improved diagnosis, implements escalation pathways for complex cases, and ensures consistent, high-quality patient care while reducing overall cycle time and resource consumption.

KEY STAKEHOLDERS AND ROLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- **Registration Staff (ER)**: Emergency patient intake and initial triage assessment
- **Registration Staff (FD)**: Non-emergency patient intake and scheduling
- **Triage Nurses**: Initial patient assessment and routing decisions
- **General Practice Physicians**: Conduct standard examinations
- **Medical Specialists**: Perform expert examinations for complex cases
- **Senior Diagnosticians**: Responsible for accurate patient diagnosis
- **Treatment Physicians**: Administer primary treatments
- **Specialist Treatment Team**: Handle complex or escalated cases
- **Quality Review Board**: Review cases with treatment failures for escalation
- **Discharge Coordinators**: Process patient discharge and follow-up care instructions

DETAILED STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS FLOW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

START CONDITIONS AND TRIGGERS
The process is triggered when a patient arrives at the healthcare facility requiring medical attention. An initial triage assessment determines the appropriate entry point:
- Emergency Room (ER) registration for urgent/emergency cases requiring immediate attention
- Front Desk (FD) registration for scheduled, routine, or non-emergency cases

STEP 1: PATIENT REGISTRATION WITH TRIAGE (EXCLUSIVE GATEWAY)
The process begins with one of two mutually exclusive registration activities based on patient acuity:

**Option 1A: Register at Emergency Room (ER)**
- Activity: Register at ER with Emergency Triage
- Trigger: Patient presents with urgent/emergency symptoms or arrives via ambulance
- Responsible: ER Registration Staff and Triage Nurses
- Inputs: Patient identification, chief complaint, vital signs
- Process:
  1. Capture patient demographics and insurance information
  2. Perform rapid triage assessment (acuity level 1-5)
  3. Document presenting symptoms and vital signs
  4. Assign preliminary urgency classification
- Duration Target: 15 minutes (900 seconds)
- Output: Patient registered with triage level, routed to appropriate examination path
- KPI: Registration completion within 15 minutes for 95% of patients

**Option 1B: Register at Front Desk (FD)**
- Activity: Register at FD with Initial Assessment
- Trigger: Patient has scheduled appointment or presents with non-emergency condition
- Responsible: FD Registration Staff
- Inputs: Patient identification, appointment details, chief complaint
- Process:
  1. Capture patient demographics and insurance information
  2. Verify appointment or assess walk-in appropriateness
  3. Document reason for visit
  4. Assign to appropriate examination track
- Duration Target: 10 minutes (600 seconds)
- Output: Patient registered and routed to appropriate examination path
- KPI: Registration completion within 10 minutes for 95% of patients

STEP 2: STANDARDIZED EXAMINATION ROUTING (RULE-BASED EXCLUSIVE GATEWAY)
After registration, patient routing follows standardized clinical protocols based on documented criteria:

**Decision Point: Examination Path Selection**
- Responsible: Triage Nurse (ER) or Clinical Intake Coordinator (FD)
- Decision Criteria:
  * **Expert Examination Path**: Complex cases, specialty conditions, multi-system complaints, high-risk patients
  * **Standard Examination Path**: Routine cases, single-system complaints, follow-up visits with new symptoms
  * **Direct to Diagnosis Path**: Follow-up visits with known condition, medication reviews, test result reviews

**Option 2A: Expert Examination Path (40-45% of cases - optimized down from 53%)**
- Activity: Expert Examination by Medical Specialist
- Trigger: 
  * Complex multi-system presentation
  * Specialty condition identified in triage
  * High-risk patient (comorbidities, age, severity)
  * ER patients with acuity level 1-2
- Responsible: Board-certified Medical Specialist (relevant specialty)
- Inputs: Registration data, triage assessment, available medical history
- Process:
  1. Review patient history and triage notes
  2. Perform specialized physical examination
  3. Order appropriate diagnostic tests
  4. Document findings and clinical reasoning
  5. Determine if Thorough Examination is required
- Duration Target: 30 minutes (1,800 seconds)
- Outcomes: 
  * Proceed to Thorough Examination (if comprehensive assessment needed)
  * Proceed directly to Diagnosis (if clinical picture clear)
- Quality Gate: Specialist must document justification for thorough examination or skip

**Option 2B: Standard Examination Path (45-50% of cases - optimized up from 33%)**
- Activity: Examination by General Practice Physician
- Trigger:
  * Routine presentations
  * Single-system complaints
  * ER patients with acuity level 3-4
  * FD patients with standard complaints
- Responsible: General Practice Physician or Internal Medicine Physician
- Inputs: Registration data, triage assessment, available medical history
- Process:
  1. Review patient history and presenting complaint
  2. Perform standard physical examination
  3. Order basic diagnostic tests if needed
  4. Document findings
  5. Determine if Thorough Examination is required
- Duration Target: 20 minutes (1,200 seconds)
- Outcomes:
  * Proceed to Thorough Examination (if findings warrant comprehensive assessment)
  * Proceed directly to Diagnosis (if clinical assessment complete)
- Quality Gate: Physician must document reasoning for thorough examination or skip

**Option 2C: Direct to Diagnosis Path (5-10% of cases - maintained)**
- Activity: Skip examination, proceed to Diagnosis
- Trigger:
  * Follow-up visit for known diagnosis
  * Medication adjustment visit
  * Test result review appointment
  * Administrative visit (clearance, documentation)
- Responsible: Clinical Intake Coordinator
- Inputs: Appointment reason, previous visit records
- Process: Verify appropriateness of skip path against protocol checklist
- Duration Target: 5 minutes (300 seconds)
- Outcomes: Patient routed directly to Diagnosis step
- Quality Gate: Must have documented justification (recent examination within 7 days, known stable condition, etc.)

STEP 3: THOROUGH EXAMINATION (CONDITIONAL)
For patients whose initial examination indicates need for comprehensive assessment:

**Decision Point: Thorough Examination Required**
- Responsible: Examining Physician (Expert or General)
- Decision Criteria (Must meet at least one):
  * Abnormal findings requiring comprehensive work-up
  * Inconclusive initial examination results
  * High-risk patient requiring detailed assessment
  * Protocol-mandated comprehensive examination (e.g., pre-surgical)

**Activity: Thorough Examination**
- Trigger: Decision criteria met and documented by examining physician
- Responsible: Senior Physician or Specialist (may be same as initial examiner)
- Inputs: Initial examination findings, preliminary test results, documented justification
- Process:
  1. Review all previous examination findings
  2. Perform comprehensive system-by-system physical examination
  3. Review and interpret all diagnostic test results
  4. Conduct detailed patient interview
  5. Document complete clinical picture
- Duration Target: 45 minutes (2,700 seconds)
- Expected Volume: 50-55% of examined patients (vs 62% in AS-IS)
- Outcomes: Complete examination findings ready for Diagnosis
- Quality Improvement: Reduced unnecessary thorough examinations through better initial assessment

STEP 4: COMPREHENSIVE DIAGNOSIS
All examination paths converge at standardized diagnosis step:

**Activity: Diagnosis**
- Responsible: Senior Diagnostician or Attending Physician
- Inputs: Complete examination findings, test results, patient history
- Process:
  1. Review all examination findings and test results
  2. Formulate primary diagnosis and differential diagnoses
  3. Assess treatment complexity and patient risk factors
  4. Select evidence-based treatment protocol
  5. Document diagnosis with ICD codes and treatment rationale
  6. Flag high-risk cases for treatment team review
- Duration Target: 30 minutes (1,800 seconds)
- Quality Gates:
  * Mandatory documentation of clinical reasoning
  * Evidence-based treatment protocol selection
  * High-risk cases reviewed by senior clinician
- Outcomes: 
  * Diagnosis documented with treatment plan
  * Patient proceeds to Treatment
- KPI: Diagnosis accuracy rate >95% (measured by treatment success rate)

IMPROVEMENT: Enhanced diagnosis quality through:
- Mandatory use of clinical decision support systems
- Evidence-based protocol libraries
- Peer review for complex cases
- Reduced diagnostic burden (no longer handling rework from 11,746 cases down to ~11,000)

STEP 5: TREATMENT ADMINISTRATION
All diagnosed patients proceed to treatment:

**Activity: Treatment**
- Responsible: Treatment Physicians and Medical Staff
- Inputs: Diagnosis, evidence-based treatment protocol, patient consent
- Process:
  1. Review diagnosis and selected treatment protocol
  2. Verify patient allergies and contraindications
  3. Obtain informed consent
  4. Administer treatment according to protocol
  5. Monitor patient response during treatment
  6. Document treatment administration and immediate response
- Duration Target: 
  * Simple treatments: 30 minutes (1,800 seconds)
  * Standard treatments: 60 minutes (3,600 seconds)
  * Complex treatments: 90 minutes (5,400 seconds)
- Quality Gates:
  * Protocol compliance verification
  * Allergy/interaction screening completed
  * Informed consent documented
- Outcomes: Treatment administered, patient monitored for response
- KPI: Protocol adherence rate >98%

IMPROVEMENT: Treatment success rate target >85% (vs 77% in AS-IS) through:
- Improved diagnosis quality
- Evidence-based protocol adherence
- Pre-treatment risk screening
- Clinical decision support integration

STEP 6: MANDATORY TREATMENT OUTCOME EVALUATION (EXCLUSIVE GATEWAY)
All treated patients undergo formal outcome assessment:

**Activity: Treatment Outcome Evaluation**
- Responsible: Treating Physician
- Inputs: Treatment administered, patient response, clinical observations
- Timing: 
  * Immediate response: Evaluated before patient leaves treatment area
  * Clinical response: Evaluated 2-4 hours post-treatment (or per protocol)
- Process:
  1. Assess patient clinical response to treatment
  2. Review objective measures (vital signs, test results, symptom scores)
  3. Evaluate against expected treatment outcomes
  4. Document outcome with supporting evidence
  5. Make disposition decision
- Duration Target: 15 minutes (900 seconds)
- Quality Gate: MANDATORY outcome documentation - no bypass allowed
- Outcomes: One of three mutually exclusive paths

**Outcome 6A: Treatment Successful (TARGET: >85% of cases)**
- Criteria:
  * Patient condition improved to expected level
  * Symptoms resolved or significantly reduced
  * Clinical parameters within acceptable range
  * Patient stable for discharge
- Responsible: Treating Physician confirms success
- Process:
  1. Document successful outcome with evidence
  2. Prepare discharge instructions and follow-up plan
  3. Route patient to Discharge
- Outcomes: Patient proceeds to Discharge step
- Duration: Immediate routing to discharge (no delay)

**Outcome 6B: Treatment Partially Successful - Minor Adjustment Needed (TARGET: 10-12% of cases)**
- Criteria:
  * Patient showing improvement but outcome suboptimal
  * Minor protocol adjustment likely to achieve success
  * First treatment attempt (no prior failures)
  * Clinical judgment supports one additional attempt
- Responsible: Treating Physician with Senior Clinician consultation
- Process:
  1. Document partial success and rationale for adjustment
  2. Senior Clinician reviews and approves re-treatment
  3. Adjust treatment protocol (dose, medication, approach)
  4. Route patient back to Treatment (Modified Protocol)
- Outcomes: Patient returns to Treatment with adjusted protocol
- Quality Gate: Senior approval required for re-treatment
- Maximum Iterations: ONE additional treatment attempt via this path

**Outcome 6C: Treatment Unsuccessful - Escalation Required (TARGET: <5% of cases)**
- Criteria:
  * No significant patient improvement
  * Treatment protocol ineffective
  * Second treatment attempt failure, OR
  * First attempt failure with complex presentation
- Responsible: Treatment Team with Senior Clinician review
- Process:
  1. Document treatment failure with detailed assessment
  2. Trigger mandatory case review by Quality Review Board
  3. Escalate to Specialist Treatment Team
  4. Comprehensive case re-evaluation
  5. Alternative diagnosis considered
  6. Advanced treatment protocol selected or specialist referral
- Outcomes: Patient routed to Escalated Care Pathway
- Quality Gate: Mandatory review and escalation (no repeated standard rework)

STEP 7: CONTROLLED REWORK WITH ESCALATION (EXCEPTION HANDLING)
Treatment failures are handled through structured escalation:

**Activity: Case Review and Escalation**
- Trigger: Treatment unsuccessful outcome
- Responsible: Quality Review Board (Senior Clinicians, Specialists)
- Inputs: Complete case history, examination findings, diagnosis, treatment attempts
- Process:
  1. Rapid case review (target: 30 minutes)
  2. Evaluate diagnostic accuracy
  3. Review treatment protocol appropriateness
  4. Identify missed factors or alternative diagnoses
  5. Determine escalation pathway
- Duration Target: 30 minutes (1,800 seconds)
- Outcomes: Escalation decision

**Escalation Pathway 7A: Re-Diagnosis Required**
- Trigger: Diagnostic accuracy questioned or new information available
- Responsible: Senior Diagnostician or Specialist
- Process:
  1. Comprehensive diagnostic re-evaluation
  2. Additional tests ordered if needed
  3. Alternative diagnoses considered
  4. New evidence-based treatment protocol selected
- Outcomes: Return to Treatment with escalated oversight
- Maximum Iterations: ONE re-diagnosis cycle

**Escalation Pathway 7B: Specialist Treatment Protocol**
- Trigger: Complex case requiring specialized approach
- Responsible: Specialist Treatment Team
- Process:
  1. Specialist takes over case management
  2. Advanced treatment protocols applied
  3. Intensive monitoring implemented
- Outcomes: Specialized treatment administered
- Quality Gate: Specialist sign-off required

**Escalation Pathway 7C: External Referral**
- Trigger: Facility capabilities insufficient for patient needs
- Responsible: Case Manager and Senior Clinician
- Process:
  1. Identify appropriate referral facility
  2. Coordinate transfer of care
  3. Prepare comprehensive case documentation
- Outcomes: Patient transferred to specialized facility
- Process End: Patient discharged to external facility

**Maximum Rework Rule:**
- No patient cycles through standard Treatment → Re-diagnosis → Treatment more than ONCE
- Second failure triggers mandatory escalation (Pathway 7B or 7C)
- Eliminates 3+ cycle rework loops observed in AS-IS (100+ cases)

IMPROVEMENT: Reduced rework from ~1,746 uncontrolled cycles to <500 controlled escalations
- Faster resolution for patients with complex conditions
- Better resource utilization
- Improved patient satisfaction
- Higher eventual treatment success rate

STEP 8: STANDARDIZED DISCHARGE (MANDATORY END STATE)
All successful treatment paths converge at discharge:

**Activity: Discharge**
- Trigger: Treatment successful evaluation documented
- Responsible: Discharge Coordinator with Physician approval
- Inputs: Treatment success documentation, clinical status, discharge readiness criteria
- Mandatory Prerequisites:
  * Treatment outcome formally evaluated and documented as successful
  * Patient clinically stable per discharge criteria
  * Discharge instructions prepared
  * Follow-up appointments scheduled if required
  * Patient/family education completed
- Process:
  1. Verify treatment success documentation (MANDATORY)
  2. Assess discharge readiness using standardized criteria
  3. Prepare discharge summary and instructions
  4. Educate patient on home care and warning signs
  5. Schedule follow-up appointments
  6. Provide prescriptions and medical documentation
  7. Coordinate transportation if needed
  8. Complete discharge documentation
- Duration Target: 30 minutes (1,800 seconds)
- Quality Gates:
  * Treatment outcome documentation verified (NO exceptions)
  * Discharge criteria checklist completed
  * Patient education documented
  * Follow-up plan documented
- Outcomes: Patient discharged, process ends
- KPI: 
  * 0% discharge without outcome documentation (vs 8% bypass in AS-IS)
  * <5% 30-day readmission rate

IMPROVEMENT: Eliminated direct discharge bypass (995 cases in AS-IS)
- Ensures patient safety through complete outcome evaluation
- Provides complete medical record
- Reduces readmission risk
- Enables quality measurement and improvement

END CONDITIONS
The process terminates when the patient is formally discharged from the healthcare facility after documented successful treatment. Alternative end conditions:
- Patient transferred to external specialized facility (with complete documentation)
- Patient transferred to inpatient admission (separate process)

All end states require complete documentation and formal disposition decision.

PROCESS METRICS AND PERFORMANCE CHARACTERISTICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Volume Targets (Based on ~10,750 annual patient volume):**
- ER registrations: 50-55% of total intake (optimized from 53.5%)
- FD registrations: 45-50% of total intake (optimized from 46.5%)
- Expert Examination route: 40-45% (reduced from 53% through better triage)
- Standard Examination route: 45-50% (increased from 33% with better protocols)
- Direct to Diagnosis: 5-10% (maintained, but with strict criteria)
- Thorough Examination: 50-55% (reduced from 62% by eliminating redundancy)

**Success Targets:**
- First-time treatment success rate: >85% (improved from 77%)
- Treatment requiring one adjustment: 10-12% (controlled rework)
- Treatment requiring escalation: <5% (vs 5%+ multi-cycle rework in AS-IS)
- Cases requiring external referral: <2%
- Overall treatment success rate: >97%

**Performance Targets:**
- Registration: 10-15 minutes (vs 40,000 seconds in AS-IS)
- Initial Examination: 20-30 minutes (vs 40,000 seconds in AS-IS)
- Thorough Examination: 45 minutes (vs 60,000 seconds in AS-IS)
- Diagnosis: 30 minutes (vs 60,000 seconds in AS-IS)
- Treatment: 30-90 minutes depending on complexity (vs 60,000 seconds in AS-IS)
- Outcome Evaluation: 15 minutes (mandatory, vs optional in AS-IS)
- Discharge: 30 minutes (vs 40,000 seconds in AS-IS)

**Target Cycle Times:**
- Fastest path (Direct Diagnosis): 2-3 hours (vs 240,000 seconds = 67 hours in AS-IS)
- Standard path (Examination + Successful Treatment): 4-6 hours (vs 260,000-320,000 seconds = 72-89 hours in AS-IS)
- Complex path (Expert + Thorough + Successful): 6-8 hours (vs 320,000 seconds = 89 hours in AS-IS)
- One adjustment cycle: 8-10 hours (vs 400,000-480,000 seconds = 111-133 hours in AS-IS)
- Escalated cases: 10-14 hours (vs 580,000-800,000+ seconds = 161-222+ hours in AS-IS)

**Throughput Improvements:**
- 90% reduction in process cycle time through elimination of waiting time
- 70% reduction in rework volume through improved diagnosis
- 85% elimination of multi-cycle rework through escalation
- 100% elimination of process bypasses

**Resource Optimization:**
- Diagnosis workload reduced from 11,746 to ~11,050 annual cases (6% reduction)
- Treatment workload reduced from 11,746 to ~11,300 cases (4% reduction)
- Specialist resources better allocated through triage optimization
- Senior clinician involvement for complex cases only
