### IDEAL Process Model

#### Process Overview and Purpose
The IDEAL process model is an improved, streamlined version of the patient admission, examination, diagnosis, treatment, and discharge workflow. It rationalizes the AS-IS complexities by standardizing examination paths, enforcing mandatory outcome checks before discharge, limiting rework loops to a single retry (with escalation), collapsing redundant skips, and ensuring sequential progression. This promotes efficiency, compliance, patient safety, and consistent performance, targeting average cycle times of 240,000-280,000 ms. Retains all core activities from the source without inventing new ones.

#### Key Stakeholders and Roles Involved
Roles not specified in sources; assumes undifferentiated medical staff.

#### Detailed Step-by-Step Process Flow
- **Start Conditions and Triggers**: Patient arrival at ER or FD.

- **Registration (XOR Gateway)**:
  1. 'Register at ER' → post-registration.
  2. OR 'Register at FD' → post-registration.

- **Initial Examination (XOR Gateway, standardized)**:
  - Mandatory choice:
    1. 'Examination' (routine cases) → thorough phase.
    2. OR 'Expert Examination' (high severity, based on registration triage) → thorough phase.
  - Removes AS-IS skips for consistency.

- **Thorough Examination (Mandatory)**:
  - 'Thorough Examination' → diagnosis entry.
  - Removes optional skips; always performed post-initial exam for completeness.

- **Diagnosis** → Treatment phase.

- **Treatment → Outcome Check (Loop limited to 1 retry)**:
  1. 'Treatment'.
  2. **XOR Outcome Gateway**:
     - 'Treatment successful' → Discharge path.
     - OR 'Treatment unsuccessful' → Single rework: back to 'Diagnosis' → 'Treatment' (second attempt).
  3. After second 'Treatment':
     - MUST 'Treatment successful' → Discharge (no further loops).
     - If second unsuccessful: Escalation path (implicit merge to Discharge with note, but enforce success or external handling).

- **Discharge (Final Synchronization)**:
  - 'Discharge' → process end.
  - Removes direct Treatment → Discharge skips; outcome mandatory.

- **Decision Points**:
  | Gateway | Type | Criteria/Outcomes |
  |---------|------|-------------------|
  | Registration | XOR | ER (urgent) vs FD (transfer?). |
  | Initial Exam | XOR | Routine ('Examination') vs Severe ('Expert Examination'). |
  | Outcome | XOR | Success → End; Unsuccessful → 1x rework only. |

- **Parallel Activities and Synchronization**: None; fully sequential.
- **Exception Handling and Error Paths**: After 1 unsuccessful treatment, second failure triggers escalation (e.g., specialist consult implied by loop limit, not new activity).
- **Rework Loops**: Strictly limited to 1 Diagnosis-Treatment cycle retry.
- **End Conditions and Outcomes**:
  - Discharge only after confirmed 'Treatment successful'.
  - No premature ends.

#### Process Metrics and Performance Characteristics (Targeted)
- Expected avg time: 240k ms straight success (90% cases); 360k ms with 1 loop (10%).
- Bottlenecks addressed: Fixed exam sequence (~120k ms total exams); limited loops.
- Compliance: 100% outcome-checked discharges; standardized paths.

#### Key Improvements Made
- **Streamlined Exams**: Mandatory sequence (Initial → Thorough) eliminates skips/variability; Expert for severe cases only.
- **Rework Limits**: Max 1 loop prevents long cycles (eliminates 640k+ ms traces).
- **Enforced Compliance**: No Discharge without 'Treatment successful'; removes risky skips.
- **Reduced Variability**: Fewer gateways (XOR only); consistent ~80% reduction in variants.
- **Efficiency Gains**: Targets 20-30% faster throughput by cutting rework (from ~15% 1-loop to 10%, no multi-loops).
- **Safety Enhancements**: Mandatory thorough checks and outcome validation reduce errors.
- **Bottleneck Fixes**: Clear sequencing avoids optional paths; escalation after 2 failures.
