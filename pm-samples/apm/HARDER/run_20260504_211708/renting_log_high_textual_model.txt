AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: Manage the lifecycle of residential tenancy from initial interest through screening, contracting, move-in, recurring rent collection, delinquency handling, and termination (by tenant cancellation or eviction).
- Scope covered by the observed behavior:
  - Pre-lease funnel: application, (optional) appointment and viewing, (optional) paperwork and screening, decision to reject or contract.
  - Post-lease operations: move-in and an ongoing rent cycle with timely payments, missed payments, warnings, late payment acceptance, repeated delinquency, tenant-initiated cancellation, and eviction escalation.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Roles are not explicitly specified in the source data. Activity names imply interactions between a prospective tenant/tenant (e.g., Apply for Viewing Appointment, Hand In The Paperwork, Pay Rent) and a property/landlord function (e.g., Check Paperwork, Screen Prospective Tenant, Issue Warning, Accept Late Payment, Evict Tenant). Because explicit role assignments are not provided, this model does not prescribe swimlane ownership.

Detailed step-by-step process flow

Start conditions and triggers
- Start event: A prospective tenant initiates the process by performing Apply for Viewing Appointment.

Pre-lease funnel (application to decision)
1) Apply for Viewing Appointment (start)
   - Immediately followed by one of the following orderings:
     - Apply -> Set Appointment -> View The Property
     - Apply -> Set Appointment -> View The Property -> Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant
     - Apply -> Set Appointment -> View The Property -> Reject Prospective Tenant (appointment/viewing but no paperwork/screening recorded)
     - Apply -> Reject Prospective Tenant (direct rejection without appointment/viewing/paperwork/screening)

2) Set Appointment (optional)
   - May be performed after Apply for Viewing Appointment.
   - May be skipped entirely; in that case the process can proceed directly to the decision point (reject/contract) without viewing or paperwork/screening.

3) View The Property (optional)
   - Common after Set Appointment.
   - May be followed by:
     - Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant
     - Direct rejection (View The Property -> Reject Prospective Tenant)
     - Direct decision without paperwork/screening (i.e., proceeding to contract decision stage)

4) Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant (optional group)
   - If paperwork is handed in, it is followed by Check Paperwork and then Screen Prospective Tenant.
   - This entire paperwork/screening group can be skipped; decisions (reject or sign) may be taken directly after Apply or after viewing.
   - After Screen Prospective Tenant:
     - Either proceed directly to decision (reject/sign), or
     - Perform Extensive Screening (optional additional check) before the decision.

5) Extensive Screening (optional, after initial screening only)
   - May or may not occur.
   - Leads to the same decision point as regular screening (reject or sign).

Decision point: Prospective tenant outcome
- Exclusive outcomes:
  - Reject Prospective Tenant (end state for the application; the case terminates).
  - Sign Contract -> Move In (proceed to occupancy and rent cycle).

Observed valid decision paths (non-exhaustive but covering all observed categories):
- Apply -> Reject Prospective Tenant.
- Apply -> Set Appointment -> View The Property -> Reject Prospective Tenant.
- Apply -> Set Appointment -> View The Property -> Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant -> Reject Prospective Tenant.
- Apply -> Set Appointment -> View The Property -> Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant -> Extensive Screening -> Reject Prospective Tenant.
- Apply -> [optional Set Appointment, optional View, optional paperwork/screening/extensive] -> Sign Contract -> Move In.

Post-lease operations (rent cycle after move-in)
6) Move In
   - After signing, the tenant moves in.
   - First cycle ordering is non-deterministic; either of the following can occur first:
     - Move In -> Pay Rent (normal case)
     - Move In -> Miss Rent Payment (delinquency occurs before any successful payment)

7) Rent cycle core activities and branching
   - Pay Rent (repeatable):
     - Pay Rent can occur repeatedly in sequence (Pay Rent -> Pay Rent), allowing an unlimited number of consecutive on-time payments.
     - After any Pay Rent, an internal decision occurs:
       - Continue tenancy (loop back to another Pay Rent in the next cycle), or
       - Terminate tenancy via Tenant Cancels Appartment (tenant-initiated termination).
       - Infrequently, termination by Evict Tenant is also observed in proximity to delinquency handling; eviction is discussed under the missed payment branch below.

   - Miss Rent Payment (repeatable):
     - If a payment is missed, Issue Warning occurs.
     - After Issue Warning, the following behaviors are observed:
       - Accept Late Payment (optional) -> Pay Rent (late payment is accepted and recorded as a subsequent Pay Rent).
         - After Accept Late Payment, either:
           - The next cycle proceeds with Pay Rent, or
           - The case may still encounter another Miss Rent Payment in a subsequent cycle (Accept Late Payment -> Miss Rent Payment is observed).
       - Do not accept late payment (implied by the absence of Accept Late Payment):
         - The process can cycle back to Miss Rent Payment again, resulting in consecutive missed cycles with repeated warnings.
       - Escalate to Evict Tenant:
         - A minority of cases escalate from Issue Warning to Evict Tenant without an intervening successful payment.

8) Tenancy termination (end states)
- Tenant Cancels Appartment:
  - Observed after one or more Pay Rent occurrences; cancellation can happen soon after move-in or after many payment cycles.
  - This ends the case.
- Evict Tenant:
  - Observed after delinquency escalation, typically following Issue Warning.
  - This ends the case.

Additional ordering and loop rules to preserve all observed behaviors
- Pre-lease optionality:
  - Set Appointment and View The Property are optional as a pair; both may be skipped, or only paperwork and screening may be performed (rare), or decisions can be taken immediately after Apply or after View without paperwork/screening.
  - If paperwork is undertaken, the observed order is: Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant; Extensive Screening, if it occurs, follows Screen Prospective Tenant and precedes the decision.
- Decision alternatives:
  - From the decision point, exactly one of the following occurs: Reject Prospective Tenant (end) or Sign Contract (continue).
- Post-lease loop and interleavings:
  - After Move In, either Pay Rent or Miss Rent Payment may occur first.
  - Pay Rent can self-repeat any number of times.
  - Miss Rent Payment can repeat (consecutive delinquent cycles with multiple warnings).
  - Accept Late Payment is optional after a warning and, even when accepted, does not guarantee the next cycle is a successful Pay Rent; a subsequent Miss Rent Payment is permissible.
  - Evict Tenant may occur after a warning (with or without a subsequent accepted late payment); Tenant Cancels Appartment may occur after any successful Pay Rent cycle.

Exception handling and error paths
- Direct early rejections:
  - Applications may be rejected immediately after Apply for Viewing Appointment (no appointment, viewing, or screening).
  - Rejection can also follow viewing but before any paperwork/screening.
- Skipped due diligence steps:
  - It is possible to reach a reject/contract decision without documented paperwork, checks, or screenings. This reflects either true bypasses or incomplete recording; the AS-IS model permits it because it is observed/allowed by the discovered control-flow.
- Delinquency escalation:
  - Repeated Miss Rent Payment events with Issue Warning and optional Accept Late Payment cycles are supported.
  - Eviction is a terminal escalation, observed after Issue Warning.
- Tenant termination:
  - Tenant Cancels Appartment can be triggered after any Pay Rent cycle.

End conditions and outcomes
- The process ends in one of:
  - Reject Prospective Tenant (pre-lease).
  - Tenant Cancels Appartment (post-lease, tenant-initiated).
  - Evict Tenant (post-lease, landlord-initiated).

- Variant-level proportions (most frequent observed exact paths):
  - Rejections dominate the pre-lease funnel:
    - 28%: Apply -> Set -> View -> Hand In -> Check -> Screen -> Reject
    - 18%: Apply -> Set -> View -> Hand In -> Check -> Screen -> Extensive -> Reject
    - 17%: Apply -> Set -> View -> Reject
    - 8%: Apply -> Reject
    - Combined, approximately 71% of cases terminate with rejection before contracting.
  - Contracting and tenancy (selected examples):
    - 2%: Apply -> … -> Sign -> Move In -> Pay Rent (repeats) -> Tenant Cancels Appartment
    - Several long-running tenancy variants (each ~1%) show many Pay Rent cycles interspersed with periodic Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning -> Accept Late Payment subloops, eventually ending by Tenant Cancels Appartment or, rarely, Evict Tenant.

  - View -> Hand In The Paperwork: ~81,965; Hand In -> Check: ~44,106; Check -> Screen: ~37,860
  - Screen -> Sign Contract: ~80,492; Extensive Screening -> Sign Contract: ~82,200
  - Sign Contract -> Move In: ~81,093; Move In -> Pay Rent: ~81,081; Pay Rent -> Pay Rent: ~81,120

Key challenges and pain points observed in the current process
- High attrition in the pre-lease funnel:
  - Approximately 71% of cases end in rejection, frequently after performing time-consuming steps (appointment, viewing, paperwork, screening). This creates wasted effort upstream.
- Inconsistent gating and optionality:
  - Decisions to reject or contract can occur without recorded paperwork/screening, and sometimes even without viewing. While observed, this weakens control and compliance and risks inconsistent decisions.
- Rework and operational load in delinquency handling:
  - The rent cycle permits unlimited repetitions of late/missed payments, warnings, and late payment acceptances. Accept Late Payment does not guarantee improved behavior; subsequent misses are common, sustaining administrative overhead.
- Eviction is rare but costly:
  - Although Issue Warning -> Evict Tenant occurs infrequently, it introduces legal and operational risk. Escalation paths exist with little evidence of intervening remediation steps besides Accept Late Payment.
- Tenant churn after move-in:
  - Tenant Cancels Appartment frequently follows one or more Pay Rent cycles, including shortly after move-in in some traces, indicating potential early dissatisfaction or mobility.
- Throughput time accumulates in multi-step pre-lease flows:
  - Appointment-to-viewing and viewing-to-paperwork transitions average ~10–23 hours between steps; when combined across multiple steps and variants, overall pre-lease lead times may be material.
- Data/recording variability:
  - Optional/skipped steps suggest either true bypasses or incomplete recording. Either way, the AS-IS control-flow must permit these paths to remain behaviorally faithful.

End conditions and outcomes
- Reject Prospective Tenant (application closed without lease).
- Tenant Cancels Appartment (tenancy terminated by tenant).
- Evict Tenant (tenancy terminated by landlord escalation).

This AS-IS model captures all observed variants by allowing:
- Optional appointment, viewing, paperwork, screening, and extensive screening prior to decision.
- Exclusive decision between rejection and contracting.
- Post-move-in rent cycles with:
  - Unlimited Pay Rent repetitions.
  - Miss Rent Payment loops with Issue Warning and optional Accept Late Payment, including consecutive misses and accept-then-miss patterns.
  - Termination via tenant cancellation after any successful payment cycle.
  - Eviction following warning escalation (with or without subsequent accepted late payment).
