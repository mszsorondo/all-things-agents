Business Process Description:
AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: Manage patient episodes from registration through clinical assessment, diagnosis, treatment, and discharge, allowing for re-diagnosis and re-treatment when initial treatment is unsuccessful.
- Scope: Single patient episode from first registration (at ER or FD) to discharge. The process supports multiple treatment cycles until a stable outcome is reached.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Recorded resources (from the event log): Doc. Low, Doc. Pro, Nurse 1, Nurse 2, Nurse 3, Nurse 4, Nurse 5.
- Role allocation: The log confirms doctors and nurses are involved; specific role-to-activity assignments are not explicitly provided by the source data and are therefore not fixed in this description.

Detailed step-by-step process flow

Start conditions and triggers
- Start event: Patient registration occurs via exactly one of:
  1) Register at ER
  2) Register at FD
- Both ER and FD are equally used entry points (≈50%/50% split: Register at ER freq. ≈5008; Register at FD freq. ≈4992).

End conditions and outcomes
- Single end event: Discharge
  - Discharge occurs either after an explicit Treatment successful or directly after Treatment without success being explicitly recorded.

Core flow with all observed variants
1) Registration (exclusive start)
   - Exactly one:
     - Register at ER
     - Register at FD

2) Initial assessment before diagnosis (exclusive and optional)
   - Exactly one of the following occurs; “skip” is allowed:
     - Skip directly to the next step (no initial assessment recorded)
     - Expert Examination
     - Examination
   - Notes:
     - Expert Examination and Examination are mutually exclusive within the same episode (never both).
     - Observed orders:
       - Register → Expert Examination
       - Register → Examination
       - Register → (skip)

3) Additional assessment (optional, may follow any of the above)
   - Thorough Examination may occur once, after step 2, or be skipped:
     - If step 2 was Expert Examination: Expert Examination → Thorough Examination
     - If step 2 was Examination: Examination → Thorough Examination
     - If step 2 was skipped: Register → Thorough Examination (rare)
     - It is also valid to proceed directly to Diagnosis without Thorough Examination.
   - Observed rare paths:
     - Register → Diagnosis (skip both initial assessments and Thorough Examination)
     - Register → Thorough Examination (then Diagnosis), without an initial Expert/standard Examination

4) Diagnostic and treatment cycle (loop, one or more iterations)
   - Each cycle consists of:
     a) Diagnosis
     b) Treatment
   - Mandatory order within each cycle: Diagnosis → Treatment (always in this order).
   - Post-treatment outcome routing:
     - If Treatment unsuccessful: loop back to Diagnosis for a new cycle
     - If Treatment successful: exit the loop towards discharge steps
     - If no explicit success is recorded: Treatment may lead directly to Discharge
   - Number of cycles: One or more (unbounded). Multiple consecutive Treatment unsuccessful outcomes are observed before a final successful treatment or a direct discharge after a treatment.
   - Valid end-of-loop patterns observed:
     - … → Diagnosis → Treatment → Treatment successful → Discharge
     - … → Diagnosis → Treatment → Discharge (no “Treatment successful” recorded)
     - … → Diagnosis → Treatment → Treatment unsuccessful → Diagnosis → Treatment → … (repeats) → [Treatment successful or Discharge]
   - Early-assessment activities (Expert Examination, Examination, Thorough Examination) occur before the first Diagnosis only; they do not reappear during rework loops.

5) Discharge (end)
   - Exactly one Discharge per case (end of episode).
   - Predecessors to Discharge:
     - Treatment successful → Discharge
     - Treatment → Discharge (no explicit success event)

Decision points with criteria and possible outcomes
- After Registration (exclusive choice, exactly one):
  - Proceed with Expert Examination, or
  - Proceed with Examination, or
  - Skip initial assessment
  - Criteria: Not encoded in the models; likely clinical triage/judgment.
- After initial assessment (optional inclusive behavior over time, but realized as a sequenced option):
  - Option to perform Thorough Examination, else skip
  - Criteria: Suggested by clinical need or findings from initial assessment; not encoded.
- After each Treatment (exclusive choice, exactly one outcome observed per cycle):
  - Treatment successful: proceed to Discharge (end)
  - Treatment unsuccessful: return to Diagnosis (rework)
  - No explicit outcome recorded: proceed directly to Discharge
  - Criteria: Clinical outcome/recording practice; not encoded.

Parallel activities and synchronization points
- None observed. All activities occur in sequence; no parallel branches are modeled or required to synchronize.

Exception handling and error paths
- Rework/unsuccessful treatment:
  - Treatment unsuccessful triggers immediate return to Diagnosis and a new Treatment cycle.
  - This can repeat multiple times (unbounded) before stabilizing.
- Missing explicit success recording:
  - Some episodes record Treatment → Discharge without an explicit Treatment successful step. This is accepted behavior in the observed data and should be treated as a valid completion path.
- Skipped assessments:
  - It is valid (though less frequent) to go directly from Registration to Diagnosis, or to perform Thorough Examination without a preceding Expert/standard Examination.

Rework loops and escalation procedures
- Rework loop:
  - Pattern: Diagnosis → Treatment → Treatment unsuccessful → Diagnosis → Treatment → …
  - The loop ends when either:
    - A Treatment successful is recorded (then Discharge), or
    - The episode proceeds directly from Treatment to Discharge without recording success.

All valid orderings and optionalities (explicitly enumerated)
- Pre-diagnosis phase (from Registration to the first Diagnosis):
  - Register at ER/FD →
    - Expert Examination → (optional) Thorough Examination → Diagnosis
    - Examination → (optional) Thorough Examination → Diagnosis
    - (Skip) → (optional) Thorough Examination → Diagnosis
    - Thorough Examination only → Diagnosis (rare)
    - Directly Diagnosis (skip all assessments; rare)
- Diagnostic-treatment cycles:
  - [Diagnosis → Treatment] × N, N ≥ 1, where after each Treatment:
    - Either Treatment unsuccessful → next cycle, or
    - Final cycle ends with either:
      - Treatment successful → Discharge
      - Treatment → Discharge (no success recorded)

Key challenges and pain points observed in the current process
- Rework and prolonged cycles:
  - A non-trivial share of treatments end as unsuccessful, causing repeated Diagnosis → Treatment cycles and extending throughput times.
- Inconsistent outcome documentation:
  - A significant minority of episodes end with Treatment → Discharge without an explicit Treatment successful record, complicating outcome analytics and performance tracking.
- Variability in pre-diagnosis pathways:
  - Multiple valid paths (Expert Examination vs Examination vs skips, optional Thorough Examination) create variability in time-to-diagnosis and overall cycle time.
- Waiting times between clinical steps:
  - Notable average delays (~11–17 hours) between successive clinical steps; the longest average delay is Treatment → Discharge without explicit success (~27.8 hours), suggesting potential discharge planning or bed/administrative constraints.
- Resource contention (implied):
