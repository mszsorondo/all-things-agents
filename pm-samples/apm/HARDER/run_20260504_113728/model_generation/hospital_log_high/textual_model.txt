AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: Manage a patient episode from arrival/registration through clinical assessment, diagnosis, treatment, and discharge.
- Scope: Starts when a patient registers either at the Emergency Room (ER) or Front Desk (FD) and ends at Discharge.
- Observed behavior: The process contains alternative pre-diagnosis assessment routes, optional steps, and an iterative treatment loop that can repeat multiple times before final discharge. Some cases discharge without an explicit “Treatment successful” record.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Roles/actors are not specified in the source data. Activities are listed as observed steps without role assignments.

Detailed step-by-step process flow

Start conditions and triggers
- Trigger: A patient episode begins when the patient registers at either:
  - Register at ER, or
  - Register at FD
- The start is exclusive (a case begins with one of these, not both).

Activities (as observed)
- Register at ER
- Register at FD
- Examination (standard examination)
- Expert Examination (specialist-level assessment)
- Thorough Examination (in-depth assessment)
- Diagnosis
- Treatment
- Treatment unsuccessful (explicit outcome marker)
- Treatment successful (explicit outcome marker)
- Discharge (process completion)

Core control flow and valid orderings

1) Entry and pre-diagnosis assessment (exclusive alternatives followed by an optional step)
- Immediately after Register at ER or Register at FD, one of the following mutually exclusive paths occurs:
  a) Examination
  b) Expert Examination
  c) Skip directly to the next stage without either examination (implicit in the model)
- Then, from the next stage, Thorough Examination is optional:
  - If performed: … -> Thorough Examination -> …
  - If skipped: … -> (no thorough exam) -> …
- All pre-diagnosis assessment paths converge at Diagnosis.

Explicitly enumerated valid pre-diagnosis sequences (all occur with both ER and FD as the starting registration):
- Register at ER/FD -> Examination -> Thorough Examination -> Diagnosis
- Register at ER/FD -> Examination -> Diagnosis
- Register at ER/FD -> Expert Examination -> Thorough Examination -> Diagnosis
- Register at ER/FD -> Expert Examination -> Diagnosis
- Register at ER/FD -> Thorough Examination -> Diagnosis (skipping the initial Examination/Expert Examination)
- Register at ER/FD -> Diagnosis (skipping both the initial exam and the thorough exam)

Notes:
- Examination and Expert Examination are exclusive; they do not co-occur in the same case.
- Thorough Examination, when it occurs, follows whichever of the two examinations (if any) was done, or it can follow registration directly if the initial exam was skipped.
- The Petri net’s silent transitions account for the observed ability to skip examinations and go straight to Diagnosis.

2) Diagnosis to Treatment
- After Diagnosis, the process always proceeds to Treatment (no observed alternative end before Treatment).

3) Post-treatment outcomes and the rework loop
- After Treatment, there is an exclusive choice among three outcomes:
  a) Treatment unsuccessful -> loop back to Diagnosis
     - Pattern: … -> Treatment -> Treatment unsuccessful -> Diagnosis -> Treatment -> …
     - This rework loop can repeat multiple times (observed up to at least five cycles in rare variants).
  b) Treatment successful -> Discharge
     - Pattern: … -> Treatment -> Treatment successful -> Discharge
  c) Direct Discharge without an explicit success record
     - Pattern: … -> Treatment -> Discharge
     - This is a valid and observed path (the model allows a silent step that bypasses the “Treatment successful” record before discharge).

Synchronization and merging points
- All pre-diagnosis alternatives merge at Diagnosis.
- The treatment loop (if any) ultimately resolves to Discharge, either after an explicit Treatment successful or by discharging directly after a Treatment without that success record.
- No true parallelism is observed; all branching is by exclusive choice, followed by merges.

Exception handling and error paths (as actually observed)
- Skipped assessments: Some cases bypass all examinations (no Examination, no Expert Examination, no Thorough Examination) and move straight from registration to Diagnosis.
- Missing success marker before discharge: A non-trivial portion of cases go from Treatment directly to Discharge without the “Treatment successful” activity being recorded.
- Rework: “Treatment unsuccessful” explicitly triggers a return to Diagnosis and another Treatment cycle; this may repeat multiple times before eventual discharge.

Rework loops and escalation
- Rework loop structure:
  [Treatment -> Treatment unsuccessful -> Diagnosis -> Treatment]n, n ≥ 1
  Eventually followed by either:
  - Treatment -> Treatment successful -> Discharge, or
  - Treatment -> Discharge (no explicit success recorded)
- Observed depth: Up to at least five unsuccessful cycles in rare cases.
- No alternate escalation or termination endpoints were observed; all cases end in Discharge.

End conditions and outcomes
- Single end event: Discharge.
- Two discharge patterns exist:
  - With explicit success: … -> Treatment successful -> Discharge
  - Without explicit success: … -> Treatment -> Discharge

Process metrics and performance characteristics

Frequencies (selected, from directly-follows counts and top variants)
- Start alternatives are both common:
  - Register at FD -> next step and Register at ER -> next step occur in comparable volumes (e.g., FD->Expert Examination: 2,888; ER->Expert Examination: 2,866).
- Dominant “happy-path”-type variants (selected):
  - FD -> Expert Examination -> Thorough Examination -> Diagnosis -> Treatment -> Treatment successful -> Discharge: ~15%
  - ER -> Expert Examination -> Thorough Examination -> Diagnosis -> Treatment -> Treatment successful -> Discharge: ~14%
  - FD -> Expert Examination -> Diagnosis -> Treatment -> Treatment successful -> Discharge: ~8%
  - ER/FD -> Examination -> (optional Thorough Examination) -> Diagnosis -> Treatment -> Treatment successful -> Discharge: each ~5–7%
- Rework prevalence (transition-level view):
  - After Treatment, direct successors observed:
    - Treatment -> Treatment successful: 9,005
    - Treatment -> Treatment unsuccessful: 1,746
    - Treatment -> Discharge: 995
  - Approximate split of outcomes from Treatment steps: ~77% successful, ~15% unsuccessful (rework), ~8% direct discharge (no explicit success record).
- Looping: Rare but present; a small share of cases iterate the unsuccessful loop multiple times before completing.

Performance (average time differences, indicative order of magnitude)
- Diagnosis -> Treatment: ~60,000 seconds (~16.7 hours)
- Treatment -> Treatment successful: ~60,001 seconds (~16.7 hours)
- Treatment successful -> Discharge: ~40,001 seconds (~11.1 hours)
- Treatment -> Discharge (without success record): ~100,003 seconds (~27.8 hours) — notably slower than when a success record exists
- Pre-diagnosis steps (typical):
  - Register -> Examination/Expert Examination: ~40,000 seconds (~11.1 hours)
  - Examination/Expert Examination -> Thorough Examination: ~60,000 seconds (~16.7 hours)
  - Examination/Expert Examination -> Diagnosis (when thorough is skipped): ~60,000 seconds (~16.7 hours)
- Whole-case durations (selected variants; average throughput):
  - Typical non-rework paths with success: ~260,000–320,000 seconds (~3.0–3.7 days)
  - Paths with multiple rework cycles: ~580,000–1,120,000 seconds (~6.7–13.0 days)

Key challenges and pain points observed in the current process
- High variability before Diagnosis:
  - Multiple exclusive paths (Examination, Expert Examination, or skipping them), plus optional Thorough Examination, lead to inconsistent assessment depth across cases.
  - Some cases bypass all examinations and go straight to Diagnosis, which may contribute to later rework.
- Rework after Treatment:
  - Approximately 15% of observed post-treatment outcomes are explicitly labeled “Treatment unsuccessful,” forcing a return to Diagnosis and additional treatment cycles.
  - Rare cases repeat this loop many times, driving very long case durations (up to ~13 days on average in the longest observed variants).
- Data/completion quality:
  - About 8–10% of post-treatment transitions go directly to Discharge without an explicit “Treatment successful” record. This may indicate documentation gaps or an alternate clinical discharge decision-making path not captured by a success marker.
- Waiting times/bottlenecks:
  - Many handoffs show day-scale delays (~11–17 hours) between steps.
  - The “Treatment -> Discharge” path without a success record is significantly slower than the “Treatment successful -> Discharge” path, suggesting post-treatment administrative or clinical confirmation delays when success is not explicitly recorded.
- Predictability and throughput:
  - The combination of optional assessments and the treatment rework loop yields highly variable overall durations (ranging from ~3 days to ~13 days on average, depending on loops), complicating resource planning and patient flow predictability.

This AS-IS description permits every observed variant:
- All start alternatives (Register at ER or FD).
- All pre-diagnosis orderings (Examination vs Expert Examination vs skip; Thorough Examination optional; both exams do not co-occur).
- Mandatory Diagnosis before Treatment.
- Post-treatment exclusive outcomes:
  - Treatment unsuccessful -> loop to Diagnosis (repeatable, any number of times, including multiple).
  - Treatment successful -> Discharge.
  - Direct Discharge after Treatment without an explicit success record.
- Single terminal event: Discharge.
