AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: Manage residential leasing from the first tenant contact through viewing, screening, contracting, occupancy, rent collection, and case closure (by tenant cancellation or eviction).
- Scope covered by the event log: 
  1) Pre-lease funnel (application → appointment → viewing → optional paperwork/screening → decision).
  2) Lease execution and occupancy (sign contract → move in → recurring rent cycles with on-time and late-payment behaviors → termination by tenant cancellation or eviction).
- Observed timeframe: 2015-01-05 to 2015-03-17 (UTC).

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Tenant
- Real Estate Agents (1–5)
- Hotline
- Landlords (1–5)
- Lawyers (1–3)
Notes:
- The log lists the above resources. Based on standard responsibilities and activity names, tenants typically initiate applications, hand in paperwork, sign contracts, move in, pay rent, and can cancel; agents typically manage appointments, viewings, and screenings; landlords/lawyers appear in warning and eviction actions. The log does not explicitly bind each activity to a unique role, but these are the primary actors observed.

Detailed step-by-step process flow

Start conditions and triggers
- Start event: Apply for Viewing Appointment (always the first event; exactly once per case when present in the pre-lease sub-process).

Stage 1: Appointment and viewing (optional, may be skipped altogether)
- Exclusive choice after “Apply for Viewing Appointment”:
  - Path A (skip): Proceed directly to a lease decision without scheduling/viewing.
  - Path B (execute once each, in sequence):
    1) Set Appointment
    2) View The Property
- Behavioral constraints (observed):
  - If appointment is set, it is followed by the viewing.
  - Set Appointment and View The Property occur at most once each per case when they occur.
  - Variants also show that rejection can happen even if these steps are skipped.

Stage 2: Paperwork and screening (optional chain)
- After viewing (when viewing occurred), there is an exclusive choice to skip or execute the paperwork-and-screening sequence:
  - Skip: Move directly to a decision without paperwork/screening.
  - Execute (in sequence):
    1) Hand In The Paperwork
    2) Check Paperwork
    3) Screen Prospective Tenant
    4) Optional: Extensive Screening (may or may not occur)
- Behavioral constraints (observed):
  - Hand In The Paperwork → Check Paperwork → Screen Prospective Tenant are executed in that order when taken.
  - Extensive Screening, if it occurs, follows after Screen Prospective Tenant and can lead either to rejection or to signature (both observed).

Stage 3: Lease decision (exclusive)
- From any of the above contexts (including direct after application, after viewing, after screening, or after extensive screening), the process makes an exclusive decision:
  - Reject Prospective Tenant
    - This ends the case immediately.
    - Observed points of rejection:
      - Directly after Apply for Viewing Appointment (no appointment/viewing).
      - After View The Property (without paperwork/screening).
      - After Screen Prospective Tenant.
      - After Extensive Screening.
  - Proceed to contract
    - Sign Contract
    - Move In
    - Then continue into recurring rent management.

Stage 4: Occupancy and recurring rent management (loop with multiple valid interleavings)
- After Move In, the process enters a repeating rent cycle with two kinds of behaviors that can appear repeatedly, in sequence or alternating:
  A) On-time payments:
     - Pay Rent can occur any number of times, including multiple times consecutively.
  B) Late-payment cycles (repeatable and interleavable with on-time payments):
     - Miss Rent Payment
     - Issue Warning
     - Optional: Accept Late Payment
     - This three-step pattern (with Accept Late Payment optional) may repeat back-to-back, and can also occur between on-time payments.
- All valid local orderings observed (examples; these can repeat and interleave):
  - Pay Rent → Pay Rent → Pay Rent (any length)
  - Pay Rent → Miss Rent Payment → Issue Warning → Accept Late Payment → Pay Rent
  - Pay Rent → Miss Rent Payment → Issue Warning → Accept Late Payment → Miss Rent Payment → Issue Warning → Accept Late Payment → Pay Rent
  - Miss Rent Payment → Issue Warning → Accept Late Payment → Pay Rent
  - Miss Rent Payment → Issue Warning → Evict Tenant (rare, ends case)
  - Accept Late Payment can be followed by either Pay Rent or (less commonly) another Miss Rent Payment.
  - A Miss Rent Payment can occur right after Move In or after any Pay Rent or after Accept Late Payment.
- Important constraints (from log evidence):
  - Miss Rent Payment is always followed by Issue Warning.
  - Evict Tenant is observed after Issue Warning (not directly after Move In or after a regular Pay Rent).
  - Tenant Cancels Appartment frequently follows a Pay Rent event, but conceptually may occur after periods of on-time or late payments.

End conditions and outcomes
- If the application is rejected: case ends at Reject Prospective Tenant.
- If the tenant was contracted and moved in: the case ends when one of the following occurs:
  - Tenant Cancels Appartment (common)
  - Evict Tenant (rare, typically after one or more warnings due to missed payments)

Decision points and gateway logic
- After Apply for Viewing Appointment:
  - Optional path (skip) vs. appointment path:
    - Skip: Go directly to decision.
    - Take: Set Appointment → View The Property.
- After View The Property:
  - Optional path (skip) vs. paperwork-and-screening path:
    - Skip: Go directly to decision.
    - Take: Hand In The Paperwork → Check Paperwork → Screen Prospective Tenant → optionally Extensive Screening.
- Decision on outcome of application (exclusive):
  - Reject Prospective Tenant OR Sign Contract (not both).
- Post Move In:
  - Iterative rent cycle:
    - In each cycle, the process may include any number of Miss Rent Payment → Issue Warning → optional Accept Late Payment sequences and optionally a Pay Rent; cycles may repeat indefinitely until termination.
  - Termination (exclusive): Tenant Cancels Appartment OR Evict Tenant.

Parallel activities and synchronization points
- No true parallel execution observed in the discovered models; activities occur in sequence with exclusive choices and loops.
- Synchronization points are implicit at decision boundaries (e.g., before the reject vs. sign split) and at the end of the occupancy loop (before termination choice).

Exception handling and error paths
- Early rejection at multiple points (directly after application, after viewing, after screening, after extensive screening) represents exception/denial paths in the funnel.
- Late payments trigger Issue Warning; Accept Late Payment may resolve the delinquency for a period.
- Repeated delinquencies can escalate to Evict Tenant (rare).
- Tenant Cancels Appartment represents a customer-initiated termination, frequently observed after one or more rent payments.

Rework loops and escalation procedures
- Rework/recurrence:
  - Pay Rent repeats any number of times.
  - Late-payment mini-loop (Miss Rent Payment → Issue Warning → optional Accept Late Payment) can repeat multiple times and can interleave with periods of on-time payment.
- Escalation:
  - From Issue Warning, if payment issues persist or are not cured, the case may escalate to Evict Tenant (observed but rare).

Roles and responsibilities by activity (typical based on resource names and domain norms)
- Tenant: Apply for Viewing Appointment; Hand In The Paperwork; Sign Contract; Move In; Pay Rent; Accept Late Payment; Tenant Cancels Appartment.
- Real Estate Agents/Hotline: Set Appointment; View The Property (organize/conduct); Check Paperwork; Screen Prospective Tenant; Extensive Screening.
- Landlords/Lawyers: Issue Warning; Evict Tenant.
Note: These assignments reflect typical responsibilities suggested by resource names in the log; the log does not explicitly fix a single role per activity across all cases.

Process metrics and performance characteristics
- Funnel volume (from activity counts; one terminal event per case suggests counts approximate case-level outcomes):
  - Apply for Viewing Appointment: 10,000 cases (baseline).
  - Sign Contract and Move In: 2,770 cases (~27.7% conversion from application to contract).
  - Reject Prospective Tenant: 7,230 cases (~72.3% overall rejection rate across all denial points).
- Screening intensity:
  - Paperwork-and-screening path taken in 7,441 cases.
  - Extensive Screening occurs in 2,793 cases (~28% of those screened).
- Post-move-in outcomes (among contracted cases, 2,770):
  - Tenant Cancels Appartment: 2,680 cases (very common closure).
  - Evict Tenant: 90 cases (rare; ~3.2% of closures after move-in).
- Late-payment prevalence (among contracted cases):
  - Miss Rent Payment: 945 occurrences,
  - Issue Warning: 945 occurrences (always follows a miss),
  - Accept Late Payment: 855 occurrences (optional resolution).
  - Indicative that a substantial minority of occupants experience at least one late-payment cycle.
- Recurrence:
  - Pay Rent total events: 18,269; average ≈ 6.6 payments per contracted case (18,269 / 2,770).
  - Pay Rent frequently repeats back-to-back (strong self-loop observed).
- Performance (average time between directly-following activities; units per log are in seconds):
  - Apply for Viewing Appointment → Set Appointment: ~82,731 s
  - Set Appointment → View The Property: ~82,730 s
  - View The Property → Hand In The Paperwork: ~81,965 s
  - Check Paperwork → Screen Prospective Tenant: ~37,860 s
  - Screen Prospective Tenant → Reject Prospective Tenant: ~81,283 s
  - View The Property → Reject Prospective Tenant: ~86,058 s
  - Sign Contract → Move In: ~81,093 s
  - Pay Rent → Pay Rent: ~81,120 s (typical spacing between consecutive payments in the log)
  - Miss Rent Payment → Issue Warning: ~81,027 s
  - Issue Warning → Accept Late Payment: ~80,664 s
- Variant-level durations (whole-case durations, representative):
  - Application → Screening → Reject: ~406k s (no extensive screening) vs. ~506k s (with extensive screening).
  - Application → View → Reject: ~258k s.
  - Application → immediate Reject: ~85.7k s.
  - Long occupancy paths with many payments and occasional late cycles: 4.2M–6.2M s.

Key challenges and pain points observed in the current process
- High attrition in the pre-lease funnel:
  - Approximately 72% of applicants are rejected, often after viewing or after standard/extensive screening, inflating throughput time for non-converting cases.
- Optionality and variability:
  - Multiple valid denial points (post-application, post-viewing, post-screening) create wide behavioral variance and complicate forecasting.
- Recurring late-payment behavior:
  - A notable minority of occupants exhibit repeated late-payment cycles, requiring repeated warnings and occasional acceptance of late payments.
- Escalations and legal actions:
  - Though rare, evictions occur after warnings, introducing legal/administrative workload (landlord/lawyer involvement).
- Customer-driven termination:
  - Tenant cancellations are common end-states after move-in, often following one or more on-time payments, reducing long-term occupancy yield.
- Waiting times between key steps:
  - Consistently high inter-step times (~80–86k s) suggest handoff delays (e.g., from application to appointment, appointment to viewing, viewing to paperwork/screening).
- Operational load on agents/landlords:
  - Agents handle a large volume of screenings and viewings; landlords/lawyers handle warning/eviction tasks, which, while less frequent, are intensive when they occur.

Summary of all valid paths and orderings (coverage of observed variants)
- Mandatory start:
  - Apply for Viewing Appointment (first, exactly once).
- Optional appointment/viewing:
  - Either skip OR Set Appointment → View The Property (each at most once).
- Optional paperwork/screening (only if viewing occurred):
  - Either skip OR Hand In The Paperwork → Check Paperwork → Screen Prospective Tenant → optionally Extensive Screening.
- Decision (exclusive, can occur from any above context):
  - Reject Prospective Tenant (ends case), OR
  - Sign Contract → Move In (continue).
- Occupancy loop (any number of cycles in any interleaving):
  - Pay Rent may occur any number of times, including consecutively.
  - Late cycle may occur at any time: Miss Rent Payment → Issue Warning → optional Accept Late Payment; can repeat and interleave with Pay Rent.
  - From Issue Warning, Evict Tenant may occur (rare; ends case).
  - From states after payments or resolved late cycles, Tenant Cancels Appartment may occur (ends case).
- End states:
  - Reject Prospective Tenant (pre-contract),
  - Tenant Cancels Appartment (post-move-in),
  - Evict Tenant (post-move-in).
