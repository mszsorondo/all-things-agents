AS-IS Process Model

1) Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: This is the observed hiring / candidate selection process used to evaluate job applicants, screen them, check backgrounds, conduct technical and behavioral interviews, and reach a final disposition (Make Job Offer or Application Rejected).
- Scope: From applicant submission ("Hand In Job Application") to final disposition(s). The discovered logs and models show a complex, permissive workflow with many optional and repeatable steps, parallel branches, rework (rejected cases later re-activated), and non-linear ordering of technical interviews.

2) Key stakeholders and roles (observed in the event log)
- Applicant (submits application).
- Online System / Front Desk (channels used to submit application).
- HR group (HR-dummy, HR-pro) – performs screening, coordination and some interview steps.
- Telephonic / Screening Interviewers (role inferred from activity names).
- Coding Interviewer(s) – conducts coding interviews.
- Frontend / Backend / ML Interviewers – conduct technical interviews.
- Senior Partner – participates in higher-level interview steps and decisions.
- External provider(s) (deepResearch Inc.) – used for background checks / extensive background checks.
Note: The log lists resources such as Online System, Front Desk, HR-dummy, HR-pro, Coding Interviewer, Senior Partner and deepResearch Inc. The roles above are derived from those resource labels; the input models do not assign a single role to each activity for every case.

3) Start and end events (inferred)
- Start: Hand In Job Application (every case begins with this activity).
- End(s): Two terminal dispositions are observed as final outcomes: Make Job Offer and Application Rejected. However, both of these activities can appear multiple times and are not always strictly terminal in observed traces (see rework/exceptions below).

5) High-level summary of the observed control flow (short)
- The process starts with Hand In Job Application.
- Frequently (but not always) a Telephonic Screening follows the application.
- After telephonic screening, the process may proceed to Background Check and/or Screening Interview (both may occur — they are not strictly mutually exclusive).
- An Extensive Background Check is an optional follow-up to Screening Interview and/or Background Check in some traces.
- A Coding Interview commonly follows the screening/background steps.
- After the Coding Interview, candidates frequently undergo one or more technical interviews (Frontend, ML, Backend) — these can occur in varying orders, can be interleaved, and some technical interviews may be skipped.
- Behavioral Interview(s) occur (often near decision time) and may repeat.
- Final outcomes observed: Make Job Offer or Application Rejected. Rejections can occur early (immediately after application or screening) or late; in many traces rejections are later reversed or followed by further interviews/offers (rework).
- Many optional loops and repeated occurrences exist (Behavioral Interview, Application Rejected, Make Job Offer can repeat).

6) Detailed step-by-step process flow, with all variants, ordering possibilities and conditions
- Start condition / trigger:
  - A case ALWAYS starts with "Hand In Job Application" (initial event in traces).

- Step 1: Decide whether a Telephonic Screening is performed
  - Observed behaviors:
    - Common path: Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening (very high frequency).
    - Alternative: Hand In Job Application -> Application Rejected (early rejection) without Telephonic Screening.
    - Telephonic Screening is common but optional — some traces skip it entirely.
  - Criteria (observed): not explicitly provided in logs; selection appears to be a business rule or triage decision.

- Step 2: Post-telephonic branching (three possible activities; any can occur singly or in combination)
  - From Telephonic Screening observed transitions:
    - Telephonic Screening -> Background Check
    - Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview
    - Telephonic Screening -> Application Rejected (early stop)
  - Valid observed patterns:
    - Only Background Check.
    - Only Screening Interview.
    - Both Background Check AND Screening Interview (both may be performed; they can run in parallel or in any order).
    - Neither (telephonic may be followed straight to later steps or rejection).
  - Important: The models show Telephonic -> Background Check and Telephonic -> Screening Interview as strong causal relations; the inductive and process-tree representations allow both to happen (parallel or inclusive choice).

- Step 3: Extensive Background Check (optional follow-up)
  - Observed behaviors:
    - Extensive Background Check can follow Screening Interview and/or Background Check.
    - This activity is optional (many cases skip it).
    - If performed, it may lead to either Coding Interview or Application Rejected.
  - Valid variants:
    - Background Check -> Extensive Background Check -> Coding Interview
    - Screening Interview -> Extensive Background Check -> Application Rejected
    - Background Check -> Application Rejected (skip Extensive)
    - Extensive Background Check -> Coding Interview

- Step 4: Coding Interview (common gateway to technical interviews)
  - Observed behaviors:
    - Coding Interview often follows Screening Interview and/or Background Check (both are frequent predecessors).
    - Coding Interview may be skipped in some traces (some candidates proceed to technical/behavioral interviews by other routes), but it is a major pathway.
    - After Coding Interview, multiple outcomes are observed:
      - Proceed to one or more technical interviews: Frontend Interview, ML Interview, Backend Interview.
      - Direct rejection (Coding Interview -> Application Rejected).
  - Valid variants:
    - Screening/Background/Extensive -> Coding Interview -> (then technical interviews).
    - Coding Interview can lead directly to Application Rejected (case ends or rework happens).

- Step 5: Technical interviews (Frontend, ML, Backend) — ordering, concurrency and rework
  - Observed behaviors:
    - The technical interviews (Frontend, ML, Backend) are not strictly ordered by the logs — frequent bidirectional direct-follows edges exist between these activities (e.g., Frontend <-> ML, Frontend <-> Backend, Backend <-> Frontend), implying:
      - Any permutation/interleaving of these technical interviews is possible.
      - Candidates may take one technical interview, then another, then return to a previous one: reordering and repeated interactions occur.
    - Some candidates do only one technical interview, others do two or all three.
    - A technical interview can directly lead to Application Rejected or to Behavioral Interview or to further technical interviews.
  - All valid observed orderings include (but are not limited to):
    - Coding -> Frontend
    - Coding -> ML
    - Coding -> Backend
    - Coding -> Frontend -> ML -> Backend
    - Coding -> Backend -> Frontend
    - Coding -> Frontend -> ML -> Frontend (repetition/interleaving)
    - Any interleaving/permutation of the performed technical interviews is allowed by the observed traces.
  - Note: Frequencies show strong mutual transitions between Frontend and ML and between Frontend and Backend, implying pairs of technical interviews often alternate.

- Step 6: Behavioral Interview(s)
  - Observed behaviors:
    - Behavioral Interview is frequently executed (often multiple times per case).
    - Behavioral Interview often occurs after technical interviews and is strongly associated with Make Job Offer.
    - It can also occur before some technical interviews in a subset of traces.
    - Behavioral Interview may repeat (loop) — the model shows a behavioral->behavioral self-loop.
  - Valid variants:
    - Technical(s) -> Behavioral Interview -> Make Job Offer
    - Behavioral Interview may be repeated multiple times prior to decision.
    - Behavioral Interview can be reached from Application Rejected in some traces (see rework below).

- Step 7: Final decision(s)
  - Observed terminal outcomes:
    - Make Job Offer
    - Application Rejected
  - Observed variants and behaviors:
    - Cases frequently end in Application Rejected (high frequency).
    - Cases end in Make Job Offer (substantial frequency).
    - Rejection may happen at any stage:
      - Immediately after Hand In Job Application (early screening rejection).
      - After Telephonic Screening.
      - After Background Check / Extensive Background Check.
      - After Coding or technical interviews.
      - After Behavioral Interview(s).
    - Rework / reversal:
      - The models show Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer (i.e., a previously rejected candidate later receives an offer in observed traces).
      - Application Rejected -> Behavioral Interview is observed (rejection reversed / appeal / re-evaluation).
      - Application Rejected can repeat (Application Rejected -> Application Rejected).
      - Make Job Offer also appears with self-looping (Make Job Offer -> Make Job Offer), indicating adjustments to the offer or repeated offer events in traces.
  - Important behavioral rule: While Make Job Offer and Application Rejected are "end-state" activities, the logs show both can appear multiple times and may not be strictly final in every trace — the process allows observed re-activation after a rejection.

7) Synchronization, parallelism and joins
- Background Check and Screening Interview:
  - Both can occur after Telephonic Screening and the inductive/process-tree models show these may execute in parallel (inclusive/parallel split) or one may be skipped. All interleavings are valid.
- Technical interviews:
  - The set of technical interviews (Frontend, ML, Backend) behaves like a flexible group: the process permits executing any subset in any order, including interleavings and repetitions between Frontend and ML / Backend.
- No strict synchronization point requiring all technical interviews to complete before decision is evident in the logs; decisions may be made after any subset of technical and behavioral interviews.

8) Loops, rework, exceptions and escalation procedures (observed)
- Rework loops:
  - Behavioral Interview repeats (Behavioral Interview -> Behavioral Interview).
  - Application Rejected repeats (Application Rejected -> Application Rejected).
  - Make Job Offer repeats (Make Job Offer -> Make Job Offer).
  - Technical interviews can alternate and repeat (Frontend <-> ML, Frontend <-> Backend).
- Reversal of decisions (exceptional but observed):
  - Application Rejected -> Behavioral Interview (candidate re-evaluated after rejection).
  - Application Rejected -> Make Job Offer (a rejected case later receives an offer).
  - These behaviors indicate appeal/re-evaluation or data artifacts where a rejection event is followed by active evaluation and eventual offer.
- Early exceptions / short-circuit:
  - Hand In Job Application -> Application Rejected occurs frequently: immediate rejection without interviews.
  - Telephonic Screening -> Application Rejected: early stop after phone screen.
- Escalation:
  - No explicit "escalation" activity is named in the logs; escalation is indirectly observed when Application Rejected is followed by behavioral interviews or offers (manual override/escalation by HR/Senior Partner).

9) All observed valid end conditions and their business meaning
- Make Job Offer (observed as final outcome in many traces)
  - Business meaning: a candidate receives an offer; may be followed by additional offer-related events in the trace (amendments).
- Application Rejected (observed as final outcome in many traces)
  - Business meaning: candidate not selected. However, this state can be later changed in observed traces (re-evaluation/reversal).

10) Explicit listing of all major observed process variants (non-exhaustive but covering all behaviors seen in models)
- Variant group A (common / main):
  - Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening -> Background Check AND/OR Screening Interview (inclusive / parallel) -> [optional Extensive Background Check] -> Coding Interview -> one or more technical interviews in any order/interleaving (Frontend, ML, Backend) -> Behavioral Interview(s) -> Make Job Offer OR Application Rejected.
- Variant group B (short / rejected early):
  - Hand In Job Application -> Application Rejected (no telephonic or interviews).
- Variant group C (short / rejected after téléphonic):
  - Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening -> Application Rejected.
- Variant group D (extensive check path):
  - Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening -> Background Check -> Extensive Background Check -> Coding Interview -> technical interviews -> Behavioral Interview(s) -> Offer/Reject.
- Variant group E (rework / reversal):
  - Hand In Job Application -> ... -> Application Rejected -> Behavioral Interview -> Make Job Offer (or further interviews) — observed re-activation and reversal.
- Variant group F (technical-only / alternate orders):
  - Hand In Job Application -> Telephonic Screening -> Screening Interview -> Coding Interview -> Frontend -> ML -> Backend -> Behavioral -> Make Job Offer (any permutation of technical interviews is valid).
- Note: Within these variant groups any subset of optional activities may be skipped (e.g., some candidates never receive Telephonic Screening; some skip Coding Interview but receive technical interviews).

12) Key challenges and pain points observed in the current process (AS-IS)
- High variability and non-determinism:
  - Many optional branches and permutations of technical interviews lead to unpredictable candidate journeys, complicating planning and candidate experience.
- Frequent rework and reversed decisions:
  - Application Rejected is both frequent and appears in re-activation paths to interviews and offers; this indicates inconsistent decision-making, appeals, or data quality issues (rejections later reversed).
- Long waiting times / bottlenecks:
  - Average times between stages are often >20 hours and frequently 30–50 hours for some decision points, pointing to multi-day delays and potential throughput problems.
- Complex parallelism and lack of enforced synchronization:
  - Background Check and Screening Interview may both occur, but there is no guaranteed gating: decisions occur despite missing checks or interviews, increasing risk of inconsistent candidate evaluation.
- Repeated/duplicated events:
  - Loops in Behavioral Interview and repeated Make Job Offer events indicate possible rework, offer re-negotiations, or duplicated logging.
- Ambiguity in responsibilities:
  - Although resource names exist in logs, the discovered models do not consistently map specific activities to unique roles; this may create coordination issues between HR, technical interviewers and external providers.
- Data quality and noise:
  - Evident contradictions across discovered models (e.g., some declarative constraints claiming exact occurrences while other models show optionality) suggest noise, missing attributes, or artifacts in logging.
- Hard to define a canonical "happy path":
  - Because multiple paths are common (e.g., screening/background both present or only one present) stakeholders cannot rely on a single standardized candidate path.

13) Explicit guidance on behavioral fidelity and what “valid” traces are
- Any trace that:
  - Starts with Hand In Job Application, and
  - Proceeds with zero or one Telephonic Screening (often), and
  - Optionally includes Background Check and/or Screening Interview (both may occur), and
  - Optionally includes Extensive Background Check, and
  - Optionally includes Coding Interview and any subset of technical interviews (Frontend, ML, Backend) in any order (including interleavings and repetitions), and
  - Optionally includes one or more Behavioral Interviews, and
  - Ends in either Make Job Offer or Application Rejected,
is considered valid with respect to the observed AS-IS behavior. Additionally, traces where Application Rejected appears and is later followed by additional interviews or a Make Job Offer were observed and therefore must be allowed as valid behavior in the AS-IS description.

15) Actionable insights and immediate opportunities (based on AS-IS)
- Standardize gating rules: define explicit prerequisites for decision points (e.g., require completion of Background Check and a set of technical interviews prior to final decision) to reduce inconsistent rework and reversals.
- Reduce latency between stages: identify stages with the longest average inter-stage times (e.g., between screening and final decision) and assign SLAs or automation to reduce waits.
- Clarify rejection/re-activation policy: prevent or control re-activations of rejected cases (or create explicit "appeal" activity) to avoid inconsistent traces and improve auditability.
- Reduce variability in technical interview ordering: define a standard minimum set of technical interviews required depending on role to simplify scheduling and evaluation.
- Clean up logging: remove duplicate events and ensure each important event has consistent resource mapping to reduce data quality noise that impairs process analysis.

16) Final remarks (AS-IS fidelity)
- This AS-IS description is intentionally permissive: it captures all observed variants across multiple discovery techniques. Any trace that follows any of the observed sequences and behaviors described above should be considered valid according to the current AS-IS process.
- The process is highly flexible but also highly variable, which both enables adaptability and causes the operational issues and inefficiencies outlined in the pain points.
