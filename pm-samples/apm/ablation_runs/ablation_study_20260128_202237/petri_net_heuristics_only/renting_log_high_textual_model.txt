AS-IS Process Model
write_AS_IS_comprehensive_textual_description(content:
"Process overview and purpose
- Scope: End-to-end residential letting lifecycle from initial request to view a property through tenant onboarding and ongoing rent collection, including exception handling (rejections, missed rent, late payments, cancellations, and evictions).
- Source model: A Heuristics Miner Petri net. All named transitions are treated as visible business activities; unnamed hid_* transitions are silent routing steps (merges/joins/loop connectors).
- Purpose: Describe the currently observed behavior, including all decision points, loops, and non-ideal paths.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Prospective Tenant/Tenant: Applies to view, attends viewing, submits paperwork, signs contract, pays rent, may cancel.
- Property Management/Landlord: Schedules appointments, checks paperwork, screens applicants, conducts extensive screening if needed, issues warnings, accepts late payments, and may evict.
(Note: Roles are inferred from activity names; no additional actors are introduced.)

Detailed step-by-step process flow
Start conditions and triggers
- Trigger: A prospective tenant initiates the process by submitting a request to view the property (Apply for Viewing Appointment).

Core activities and control flow (sequential with exclusive choices; no structured parallelism was discovered)
1) Apply for Viewing Appointment
   - Observed outcomes (exclusive):
     a) Set Appointment (proceed to viewing), or
     b) Reject Prospective Tenant (early rejection path directly from the application stage).
2) Set Appointment
3) View The Property
   - Observed outcomes (exclusive):
     a) Hand In The Paperwork (continue), or
     b) Reject Prospective Tenant (rejection directly after viewing).
4) Hand In The Paperwork
5) Check Paperwork
6) Screen Prospective Tenant
   - Observed outcomes (exclusive):
     a) Sign Contract (direct approval after screening), or
     b) Extensive Screening (escalation), or
     c) Reject Prospective Tenant (rejection after screening).
7) Extensive Screening (if chosen)
   - Observed outcomes (exclusive):
     a) Sign Contract, or
     b) Reject Prospective Tenant.
8) Sign Contract
9) Move In

Recurring rent-cycle after Move In (rework/loop)
- Immediately after Move In, each rent period repeats and the process follows one of the observed alternatives:
  A) Pay Rent
     - After Pay Rent, observed model permits:
       • Loop to the next rent cycle (repeat Pay Rent in future periods).
       • Transition into a path that leads to the next cycle’s Miss Rent Payment (reflecting that a subsequent period may be missed).
       • Tenant Cancels Appartment (modeled endpoint that terminates the tenancy after a payment event).
  B) Miss Rent Payment
     - Issue Warning is triggered.
     - From Issue Warning:
       • Accept Late Payment (late payment is recorded/accepted and the cycle continues; the model explicitly routes back towards Pay Rent and future cycles), or
       • Evict Tenant (termination).

Decision points and criteria (as implied by the model)
- Early rejection decisions exist at multiple points:
  • After Apply for Viewing Appointment.
  • After View The Property.
  • After Screen Prospective Tenant and after Extensive Screening.
- Screening decisions:
  • From Screen Prospective Tenant: either directly approve (Sign Contract), escalate (Extensive Screening), or reject.
  • From Extensive Screening: either Sign Contract or Reject Prospective Tenant.
- Post–Move In rent-cycle decision:
  • Either Pay Rent or Miss Rent Payment.
  • If missed: Issue Warning, then either Accept Late Payment (continue) or Evict Tenant (end).
- Tenant-initiated termination:
  • Tenant Cancels Appartment is reachable after Pay Rent.

Parallel activities and synchronization points
- No true parallel splits/joins are present. Silent (hid_*) transitions act as internal merges/loop connectors but do not represent concurrency.

Exception handling and error paths
- Early rejections: The process can reject an applicant immediately after the application or after the viewing, without necessarily completing paperwork or screening.
- Missed rent: Triggers Issue Warning; may lead to Accept Late Payment (continuation) or Evict Tenant (termination).
- Eviction: Final termination in response to unresolved non-payment.
- Tenant cancellation: Tenant Cancels Appartment ends the tenancy; in the discovered model it is observed following a Pay Rent event.

Rework loops and escalation procedures
- Recurring rent cycle: After Move In, Pay Rent may repeat indefinitely period-over-period. The model also allows transitions from a paid period to a subsequent period that may be missed.
- Late payment loop: Miss Rent Payment → Issue Warning → Accept Late Payment → Pay Rent → next cycle. No explicit cap on the number of late payment acceptances is modeled, so repeated late cycles are possible.

End conditions and outcomes
- Reject Prospective Tenant: Application/tenancy does not proceed (pre–Move In termination).
- Tenant Cancels Appartment: Tenancy ends voluntarily (post–Move In termination).
- Evict Tenant: Tenancy ends involuntarily (post–Move In termination).

Process metrics and performance characteristics (as observed/derivable from the model structure)
- Lead time segments:
  • Application-to-viewing scheduling time (Apply → Set Appointment).
  • Viewing-to-paperwork submission time (View → Hand In The Paperwork).
  • Paperwork-to-decision time (Check Paperwork/Screen Prospective Tenant/Extensive Screening → Sign or Reject).
- Conversion rates:
  • Application-to-viewing conversion (early rejections present).
  • Viewing-to-paperwork conversion (rejections present).
  • Screening approval rate (direct vs extensive screening vs reject).
- Tenancy health metrics:
  • On-time payment rate vs Miss Rent Payment frequency.
  • Late payment acceptance rate.
  • Eviction rate.
  • Tenant cancellation rate.
- Structural characteristics:
  • No parallelism; multiple exclusive choice points.
  • Unbounded rent-cycle loop without an explicit “lease complete” end.
  • Multiple early rejection points increase variance in paths and outcomes.

Key challenges and pain points
- Fragmented decision governance:
  • Rejection may occur at several early points (after Apply, after View), creating perceived inconsistency and potentially poor applicant experience.
- Unbounded late payment loop:
  • Accept Late Payment has no modeled limit or escalation thresholds, enabling recurrent delinquency without a formal cap.
- Ambiguous termination access:
  • Tenant Cancels Appartment is connected only after Pay Rent in the discovered model, which may under-represent legitimate cancellation timing in real life (e.g., mid-cycle notice).
- Lack of an explicit “lease conclusion” path:
  • The process continues indefinitely via rent cycles, with termination only via cancellation or eviction.
- Potential rework and inefficiency:
  • Multiple rejection points may duplicate effort (e.g., viewing conducted for applicants who might have been rejected earlier or later with incomplete information).
  • Hidden routing (tau transitions) masks timing/periodicity semantics (e.g., month boundaries), making compliance and SLA management harder."
)

IDEAL Process Model
write_IDEAL_comprehensive_textual_description(content:
"Process overview and purpose
- Scope: Streamlined letting lifecycle from application through onboarding and ongoing rental, with clear, consistently governed decision gates, well-defined rent-cycle behavior, and transparent termination options.
- Purpose: Improve fairness, predictability, and control by consolidating decision points, enforcing sequencing, and introducing clear policies for missed and late payments (without inventing new activities).

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Prospective Tenant/Tenant.
- Property Management/Landlord.
(Note: Only roles implied by the activity names are used.)

Detailed step-by-step process flow (to-be)
Start conditions and triggers
- Trigger: Prospective tenant submits Apply for Viewing Appointment.

Governed, sequenced core flow
1) Apply for Viewing Appointment
   - Target behavior: Proceed to Set Appointment whenever application meets minimal criteria for viewing. Avoid rejection at this point except for clear ineligibility (criteria documented).
2) Set Appointment
3) View The Property
   - Target behavior: Do not reject immediately after viewing unless there is a documented, objective disqualifier. Encourage applicants to proceed to paperwork when interested.
4) Hand In The Paperwork
5) Check Paperwork
6) Screen Prospective Tenant
7) Extensive Screening (only if needed based on screening outcomes)
8) Single consolidated decision gate (exclusive):
   - Sign Contract (preferred path), or
   - Reject Prospective Tenant (if criteria not met).
   Rationale: Consolidates rejections to a single governed gate after adequate evidence (Check Paperwork + Screening/Extensive Screening), improving consistency and fairness.
9) Sign Contract
10) Move In

Standardized rent-cycle (post–Move In)
- Recurring monthly cycle with explicit, governed outcomes:
  A) Pay Rent (by due date)
     - After Pay Rent: continue to next cycle.
     - Tenant Cancels Appartment may occur at any time post–Move In in accordance with notice terms; if it occurs after a payment, proceed to termination; if it occurs on a non-payment period, resolve outstanding balances per policy and terminate. (No new tasks added; this clarifies when the existing termination activity is applicable.)
  B) Miss Rent Payment
     - Issue Warning promptly upon missing the due date.
     - Decision (exclusive) within a defined grace period:
       • Accept Late Payment (if received within grace), then return to normal cycle, or
       • Evict Tenant (if not received or criteria for acceptance are not met).
     - Policy refinement: Limit the number/frequency of Accept Late Payment occurrences within a rolling window to prevent chronic delinquency. Exceeding the threshold proceeds toward Evict Tenant using the existing activity.

Decision points with criteria and outcomes (to-be)
- Consolidated pre–Move In gate:
  • After Check Paperwork/Screen Prospective Tenant/(optionally) Extensive Screening: either Sign Contract or Reject Prospective Tenant.
- Post–Move In rent-cycle gate:
  • On-time vs missed payment decision, followed by Issue Warning and either Accept Late Payment (within grace) or Evict Tenant.
- Tenant-initiated termination:
  • Tenant Cancels Appartment is permitted at any point post–Move In (not artificially bound to occur only after a payment event).

Parallel activities and synchronization points
- None required. All flow remains sequential with governed exclusive choices. Silent technical joins in the discovered model are abstracted into clear gateways.

Exception handling and escalation procedures
- Early-stage exceptions:
  • Only reject early (before the consolidated gate) for well-defined, objective ineligibility (e.g., incomplete application preventing any evaluation). Otherwise, move applicants through to the consolidated screening decision.
- Missed rent:
  • Issue Warning upon due-date breach.
  • Accept Late Payment within a documented grace period and within policy limits; otherwise, Evict Tenant.
- Tenant cancellation:
  • Allow Tenant Cancels Appartment at any point post–Move In; settle balances per policy and close the case.
- Documentation:
  • Record the reason and criteria for every rejection, late acceptance, or eviction to support audit and fairness.

End conditions and outcomes
- Reject Prospective Tenant (single governed gate).
- Tenant Cancels Appartment (tenant-initiated termination at any time post–Move In).
- Evict Tenant (non-payment not cured within policy).

Process metrics and performance characteristics (to-be targets)
- Lead time and conversion:
  • Application-to-Viewing SLA (e.g., schedule within X business days).
  • Viewing-to-Paperwork SLA (encourage submission within Y days).
  • Screening decision SLA (Check Paperwork + Screen/Extensive Screen → decision within Z days).
  • Approval conversion rate at the consolidated gate.
- Tenancy health:
  • On-time payment rate and trend.
  • Late payment acceptance rate within policy thresholds.
  • Eviction rate and time-to-eviction after breach.
  • Tenant cancellation rate and average tenancy duration.
- Control and compliance:
  • Percent of rejections executed at the consolidated gate (target ~100% except for objectively ineligible cases).
  • Violations of grace period/late acceptance cap (target 0).
  • Full auditability of decisions (reasons captured).

Improvements made (rationale)
- Consolidated, criteria-based decision gate before Sign Contract:
  • Eliminates fragmented early rejections; increases consistency, fairness, and transparency.
- Enforced sequencing:
  • Apply → Set Appointment → View The Property → Hand In The Paperwork → Check Paperwork → Screen Prospective Tenant → (Extensive Screening if needed) → Sign Contract → Move In.
- Clarified rent-cycle control:
  • Explicit grace window for Accept Late Payment and a policy cap on repeated late acceptances to reduce chronic delinquency and administrative churn.
- Clear termination semantics:
  • Tenant Cancels Appartment allowed at any time post–Move In; end-of-case handling is consistent and auditable.
- Reduced rework and noise:
  • Removes unnecessary early rejection variability; focuses effort on applicants with complete information.
- Governance and auditability:
  • Decision criteria and reasons documented at key gates (reject, late acceptance, eviction), supporting compliance and continuous improvement without adding new activities."
