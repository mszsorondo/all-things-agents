AS-IS Process Model

# PROCESS OVERVIEW AND PURPOSE

This AS-IS process model describes the actual observed patient care workflow in a healthcare facility that handles both emergency and non-emergency cases. The process encompasses patient registration, examination, diagnosis, treatment, and discharge activities. The facility operates with two registration entry points (Front Desk for regular patients and Emergency Room for urgent cases) and manages various examination pathways, treatment attempts including rework loops for unsuccessful treatments, and eventual patient discharge.

The purpose of this process is to provide medical care from initial patient registration through to successful treatment and discharge. However, the current process exhibits significant variability in care pathways, frequent treatment failures requiring rework, and inconsistent examination protocols.

# KEY STAKEHOLDERS AND ROLES INVOLVED

Based on the process activities, the following roles are involved:

* **Registration Staff**: Responsible for patient registration at either the Front Desk (FD) or Emergency Room (ER)
* **General Practitioners/Nurses**: Conduct standard examinations
* **Medical Specialists/Experts**: Perform expert examinations requiring specialized knowledge
* **Diagnostic Personnel**: Conduct thorough examinations and establish diagnoses
* **Treatment Providers**: Administer medical treatments to patients
* **Discharge Coordinators**: Process patient discharge after successful treatment

# DETAILED STEP-BY-STEP PROCESS FLOW

## Start Conditions and Triggers

The process is triggered when a patient arrives at the healthcare facility requiring medical attention. The patient's condition and urgency determine the initial registration point.

## Initial Registration (Parallel Entry Points)

The process begins with one of two mutually exclusive registration activities:

1. **Register at FD (Front Desk)**: Patients with non-urgent conditions or scheduled appointments register at the front desk (frequency: ~7,000 cases based on aggregate flows)

2. **Register at ER (Emergency Room)**: Patients with urgent or emergency conditions register at the emergency room (frequency: ~6,000 cases based on aggregate flows)

After registration at either point, the patient proceeds to place p_3, which represents a decision/routing point for examination pathways.

### Path 2: Standard Examination

### Outcome 1: Treatment Unsuccessful
* **Activity**: Treatment unsuccessful
* **Frequency**: 1,746 cases
* **Average Duration**: ~60,001 seconds after treatment

## Exception Handling and Error Paths

The process contains several problematic exception patterns:

### Treatment Failure Rework Loop

* **Path**: Treatment unsuccessful → Diagnosis → Treatment (repeats until successful)
* **Duration Impact**: Each rework cycle adds ~160,000 seconds

### Examination Pathway Inconsistency

* **Frequency**: 320 cases (163 from FD, 157 from ER)
* **Duration**: ~80,000 seconds from registration
* **Issue**: Patients bypass examination protocols and proceed directly to diagnosis
* **Risk**: Potential for misdiagnosis due to insufficient examination

# KEY CHALLENGES AND PAIN POINTS

## 1. High Treatment Failure Rate
* **Issue**: 15% of initial treatments fail, requiring complete rework cycles
* **Impact**: 
  - Extended patient stay times (additional 160,000+ seconds per failure)
  - Increased resource consumption
  - Patient dissatisfaction and potential health risks
  - 1,746+ rework cycles consuming diagnostic and treatment capacity
* **Root Causes**: Potentially inadequate diagnosis, inappropriate treatment selection, or examination quality issues

## 2. Inconsistent Examination Protocols
* **Issue**: No standardized approach to determining examination pathway
* **Impact**:
  - Wide variation in care quality and diagnostic accuracy
  - Unpredictable resource utilization
  - Similar patients receiving different examination approaches
  - Six different examination pathway combinations observed
* **Evidence**: 
  - Expert Examination → Thorough Examination: ~3,637 cases
  - Expert Examination → Direct to Diagnosis: ~2,117 cases
  - Examination → Thorough Examination: ~1,968 cases
  - Examination → Direct to Diagnosis: ~1,641 cases
  - Direct to Diagnosis (skip examinations): ~320 cases
  - Direct to Thorough Examination: ~317 cases

## 3. Examination Bypass Shortcuts
* **Issue**: 320 patients bypass examination protocols entirely (direct registration to diagnosis)
* **Impact**:
  - Potential diagnostic errors due to insufficient information gathering
  - Inconsistent care quality
  - Possible correlation with treatment failures
* **Risk**: Patients receiving diagnosis without proper examination may have higher treatment failure rates

## 4. Unclear Decision Criteria
* **Issue**: No visible criteria for routing decisions at multiple gateway points
* **Affected Decisions**:
  - Registration point selection (FD vs ER)
  - Examination type selection (Expert vs Standard vs Skip)
  - Thorough Examination necessity (perform vs skip)
* **Impact**: Inconsistent care pathways, potential for inappropriate routing

## 5. Multiple Rework Loops
* **Issue**: Some patients require 2, 3, or even 4 treatment attempts
* **Extreme Cases**: 
  - 268 patients require 3+ treatment cycles
  - 41 patients require 4+ treatment cycles
* **Impact**: 
  - Severe delays (up to 800,000 seconds / 9.3 days)
  - Extremely poor patient experience
  - Massive resource waste
  - Potential indication of systematic diagnostic or treatment problems

## 6. Process Complexity and Variability
* **Issue**: 55+ distinct process variants observed
* **Impact**:
  - Difficult to standardize and improve
  - Training complexity for staff
  - Challenging to predict resource needs
  - Quality control difficulties

## 7. Silent Transitions and Ambiguous Routing
* **Issue**: Multiple skip transitions (skip_1, skip_2, skip_5, skip_6) represent unclear process logic
* **Impact**:
  - Process behavior not fully visible or controlled
  - Potential compliance issues
  - Difficulty in quality assurance

## 8. Performance Bottlenecks
* **Issue**: Each major activity (Examination, Diagnosis, Treatment) takes approximately 60,000 seconds (~16.7 hours)
* **Impact**:
  - Long total throughput times (3-9 days for most cases)
  - Significant waiting times between activities
  - Potential bed/resource occupancy issues

## 9. No Visible Triage or Urgency Handling
* **Issue**: ER and FD patients follow identical pathways after registration despite different urgency levels
* **Impact**:
  - Emergency patients may not receive appropriately expedited care
  - Resource allocation not optimized for urgency

## 10. Discharge Timing Variability
* **Issue**: Two different discharge timing patterns (~40,000 vs ~100,000 seconds post-treatment)
* **Impact**:
  - Unpredictable bed availability
  - Inconsistent patient experience
  - Potential premature or delayed discharges

## Summary of AS-IS State

The current process successfully treats and discharges patients but exhibits significant inefficiencies, quality issues, and inconsistencies. The 15% treatment failure rate is the most critical issue, generating substantial rework and delays. The lack of standardized examination protocols and the presence of examination bypass shortcuts create quality risks. The process would benefit from standardization, clearer decision criteria, improved diagnostic accuracy, and elimination of unnecessary variation while maintaining appropriate clinical flexibility.
