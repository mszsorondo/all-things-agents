Executive summary
- Purpose and scope: This is the end-to-end residential leasing process from first contact (viewing application) through viewing, paperwork, screening, contracting, move-in, monthly rent cycles, and termination (tenant cancellation or eviction). The model includes the main path and every observed deviation, loop, and exception.
- Conversion and outcomes: Roughly 10,000 applications start the process; 27.7% sign and move in; 72.3% are rejected before contract. Once moved in, 96.7% of leases end via tenant cancellation and 3.3% via eviction.
- Operational hotspots: High attrition after viewing and during screening (particularly after extensive screening) drives wasted capacity. Monthly delinquency loops (late payment acceptance) create recurring rework. Appointment, viewing, and contract-to-move-in handoffs show long wait times.
- Risks: The process technically permits skipping required steps (application → contract) via automatic routing, even if rarely observed, creating governance exposure. Cancellations are allowed after paid cycles without an explicit balance check.

Stakeholders and roles (as seen in the data; titles may vary)
- Prospective Tenant: applies, views, submits paperwork, signs, moves in, pays rent, may cancel.
- Property Management/Leasing Team: sets appointments, checks paperwork, screens, accepts/rejects, issues warnings, accepts late payments, performs evictions.

Start condition
- Trigger: A prospective tenant submits “Apply for Viewing Appointment” (process start).

Phase 1 — Pre-leasing pipeline (from application to decision)
1) Apply for Viewing Appointment (start)
- Outcomes:
  - Proceed to Set Appointment: 9,151 cases (≈91.5% of starts).
  - Early rejection (Reject Prospective Tenant) without appointment: 849 cases (≈8.5% of starts).
  - Note: This early exit reflects pre-appointment eligibility checks at a decision point requiring a choice.

2) Set Appointment → 3) View The Property
- Flow: Set Appointment → View The Property occurs in 9,151 cases (≈100% of those scheduled).

4) Post-viewing decision (decision point requiring a choice)
- Stop path: Reject Prospective Tenant after viewing: 1,710 cases (≈18.7% of viewers; ≈17.1% of all applicants).
- Continue path: proceed to documentation:
  - View → Hand In The Paperwork: 7,441 cases (≈81.4% of viewers; ≈74.4% of all applicants).

5) Hand In The Paperwork → 6) Check Paperwork → 7) Screen Prospective Tenant
- Hand In → Check: 7,441 (≈100% of those who handed in).
- Check → Screen: 7,441 (≈100% of those checked).

8) Screening decision (decision point requiring a choice; base = 7,441 screened)
- Option 1: Reject Prospective Tenant right after screening: 2,856 (≈38.4% of screened; ≈28.6% of all applicants).
- Option 2: Extensive Screening: 2,793 (≈37.5% of screened), then:
  - Extensive Screening → Reject Prospective Tenant: 1,815 (≈65.0% of those extensively screened; ≈24.4% of screened; ≈18.15% of all applicants).
  - Extensive Screening → Sign Contract: 978 (≈35.0% of those extensively screened; ≈13.1% of screened).
- Option 3: Screen Prospective Tenant → Sign Contract (without extensive screening): 1,792 (≈24.1% of screened).
- Structural allowance: The process includes automatic routing that can bypass intermediate steps to reach a signing decision (for example, directly from application to contract). This path is permitted but rarely observed in logs and represents a governance risk.

Phase 2 — Decision and contracting
9) Sign Contract → 10) Move In
- Sign Contract → Move In: 2,770 cases (≈27.7% of all applicants; ≈100% of those who signed).

Phase 3 — Post-leasing monthly rent cycle (repeats until termination)
11) Recurring month-to-month cycle (loop)
- From Move In (first cycle):
  - Move In → Pay Rent: 2,656 cases (≈95.9% of moved-in).
  - Move In → Miss Rent Payment: 114 cases (≈4.1% of moved-in).
- Regular recurring payments:
  - Pay Rent → Pay Rent (repeat month): 14,819 occurrences. Across all Pay Rent outcomes:
    - Continue paying next month: 14,819 of 18,269 outcomes (≈81.1%).
    - Tenant Cancels Apartment (terminal state): 2,680 of 18,269 (≈14.7%).
    - Miss Rent Payment next cycle: 770 of 18,269 (≈4.2%).
  - Note: After each paid cycle, the process branches to alternative paths—continue paying, cancel, or miss next payment—via automatic system routing.
- Delinquency handling (missed payments and rework loop):
  - Miss Rent Payment → Issue Warning: 945 (aggregated across cycles).
  - Issue Warning (decision point requiring a choice):
    - Accept Late Payment: 855 (≈90.5% of warnings), then:
      - Accept Late Payment → Pay Rent: 794 (≈92.9% of late acceptances).
      - Accept Late Payment → Miss Rent Payment: 61 (≈7.1%), creating a rework loop for repeat delinquency.
    - Evict Tenant (terminal state): 90 (≈9.5% of warnings).
  - Structural note: After Accept Late Payment, automatic routing allows either returning to regular payment or re-entering the missed-payment branch, enabling repeated late cycles.

Termination outcomes (end conditions)
- Reject Prospective Tenant (terminal): may occur at:
  - After Apply (≈8.5% of starts; 849 cases),
  - After View (≈17.1% of starts; 1,710 cases),
  - After Screening (≈28.6% of starts; 2,856 cases),
  - After Extensive Screening (≈18.15% of starts; 1,815 cases).
  - Total pre-contract rejections: ≈7,230 (≈72.3% of starts).
- Tenant Cancels Apartment (terminal; common post-move-in outcome): 2,680 cases. Among moved-in tenants, ≈96.7% end via cancellation (2,680 of 2,770).
- Evict Tenant (terminal; rare): 90 cases. Among moved-in tenants, ≈3.3% end via eviction (90 of 2,770), typically after an unresolved warning.

Business insights: bottlenecks, cost drivers, and impact
- High attrition pre-contract (≈72.3% of starts rejected): Significant capacity spend on viewings (100% of scheduled attend), paperwork checks (100%), and screening (100%), much of which does not convert. This is a major cost center and opportunity to improve early qualification.
- Screening is the primary filter:
  - Only ≈37.2% of screened applicants are accepted; extensive screening accepts ≈35.0% of those it touches but consumes added cycle time and resources.
- Monthly delinquency loop:
  - Warnings often result in late acceptance (≈90.5%), with ≈7.1% falling back into delinquency—creating recurring rework and revenue risk.
  - Eviction occurs in ≈9.5% of warnings (rare), suggesting a lenient posture that may encourage repeat lateness for some tenants.
- Cycle-time hotspots:
  - Appointment scheduling and viewing handoffs (≈82k) and contract-to-move-in (≈81k) are long waits and likely drivers of applicant drop-off and dissatisfaction.
  - Faster paperwork-to-screening (≈38k–44k) is a relative bright spot.
- Termination pattern:
  - Among moved-in tenants, ≈96.7% end via Tenant Cancels Apartment and ≈3.3% via eviction. Cancellations follow paid cycles; the process does not explicitly enforce a balance check at cancellation.

Risks and controls
- Governance risk: The process allows rare step-skipping (e.g., signing without documented viewing/paperwork/screening) via automatic routing. This could breach policy or regulatory expectations.
- Revenue leakage risk: Cancellations can occur after paid cycles without an explicit control to ensure full balance settlement. Repeat delinquency (late-acceptance loop) also indicates potential leakage or moral hazard.
- Compliance and auditability: High reliance on automatic routing and silent decisions reduces traceability unless controls and logs are enforced.

Strategic recommendations
- Tighten front-end qualification:
  - Introduce or strengthen pre-appointment eligibility checks and automated scoring to reduce low-probability viewings. Target reduction in post-viewing rejections (currently ≈18.7% of viewers).
- Rebalance screening strategy:
  - Calibrate criteria for Extensive Screening to focus on applicants with a meaningful conversion uplift. Monitor acceptance rate (35% post-extensive) vs. time/cost to set thresholds.
- Strengthen governance:
  - Enforce mandatory step completion for signing (no contract if viewing/paperwork/screening not complete). Implement system hard stops to remove the step-skip allowance.
  - Add a cancellation clearance control requiring balance verification before Tenant Cancels Apartment is processed.
- Optimize cycle time:
  - Commit SLA targets for Appointment → Viewing and Sign → Move In (the ≈80k segments). Use digital scheduling, reminders, and pre-move readiness checklists.
- Delinquency policy and collections:
  - Define a clear escalation matrix: number of late acceptances before escalation; incentives for on-time payment; late-fee and payment-plan policies. Aim to reduce the 7.1% relapse post-late acceptance and the 4.2% miss-after-pay rate.
- KPI governance:
  - Track conversion by stage, screening path yields, delinquency loop rate, eviction rate, and time-to-milestone. Use monthly dashboards to validate improvements and ensure compliance.
