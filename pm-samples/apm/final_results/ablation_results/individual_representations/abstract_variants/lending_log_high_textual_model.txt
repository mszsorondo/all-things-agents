AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: Origination of a loan from initial appointment request through credit application intake, verification, optional enrichment (co-signer and collateral assessment), underwriting, and final outcome (approval and signing, denial, or rejection).
- Scope: From the moment a prospective borrower requests an appointment until one of four end states: Appointment Denied, Application Rejected, Loan Denied, or Sign Loan Agreement.
- Note on activity names: The source log uses the labels “Hand In Credit Appliaction” (intended: Application) and “Make Visit to Assess Colatteral” (intended: Collateral). The model retains the source labels.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Roles are not fully specified in the source data.
- Underwriter is explicitly implied by “Submit File to Underwriter.”
- Other activities imply front-office loan processing and field assessment functions, but no explicit roles are named in the data; therefore, responsibilities are described at the activity level.

Detailed step-by-step process flow

Start conditions and triggers
- Trigger: Prospective borrower initiates the process by “Request Appointment.”

End conditions and outcomes (all observed)
- Appointment Denied (end)
- Application Rejected (end)
- Loan Denied (end)
- Sign Loan Agreement (end - successful completion)

Main sequence and decision points
1) Request Appointment (start)
2) Exclusive decision: Appointment outcome
   - If appointment cannot be granted: Appointment Denied (end). Observed as a direct path “Request Appointment -> Appointment Denied” (9% of cases; avg ~0.998 days).
   - If appointment granted: proceed to Set Appointment.
3) Set Appointment
4) Hand In Credit Appliaction (borrower submits application)
5) Verify Borrowers Information (check completeness and basic eligibility)
6) Exclusive decision after verification:
   - Application Rejected (end) when pre-underwriting rules cause a stop (16% of cases; avg ~2.74 days end-to-end).
   - Proceed to risk enrichment and/or underwriting preparation.

Conditional enrichment before underwriting (inclusive/iterative behavior)
7) Optional branches that may occur before underwriting, in various orders and possibly multiple times (inclusive-or behavior with rework):
   - Request Co-Signer On Loan
     - May be present for some cases (various low-frequency variants).
     - Appears in combination with collateral visits in multiple sequences.
   - Make Visit to Assess Colatteral
     - Often performed 0, 1, or many times (observed rework loops with 2–8+ repeated “Make Visit” events in rare cases).
     - Repetition indicates iterative attempts/returns (e.g., scheduling issues, incomplete access, additional documentation), although specific reasons are not captured in the log.
   - Both actions can co-occur in either order; the log shows no strict sequencing constraint between them.

8) Submit File to Underwriter (gate to formal credit decision)
   - Common path from verification/enrichment to formal decision.
   - Not present in the “skipped_examination” variant (see exception below).

9) Exclusive decision by underwriting:
   - Loan Denied (29% of cases for the straight path without enrichment; plus multiple lower-frequency enriched paths ending in denial). Typical durations for these denial paths range ~3.9–5.4 days depending on added steps and rework.
   - Sign Loan Agreement (16% for the straight path without enrichment; plus low-frequency enriched paths leading to signing). Typical durations range ~3.9–4.5 days.

Exception handling and error paths (as observed)

- Mixed enrichment order:
  - “Request Co-Signer On Loan” and “Make Visit to Assess Colatteral” appear in various orders and quantities, sometimes both present before underwriting, sometimes only one, sometimes none.

Rework loops and synchronization
- Rework loop: “Make Visit to Assess Colatteral” can repeat multiple times prior to “Submit File to Underwriter.”
- Inclusive behavior: If both “Request Co-Signer On Loan” and “Make Visit to Assess Colatteral” occur, they are executed in sequence (order varies) and implicitly merge before submitting to underwriting.

Process metrics and performance characteristics (from the provided variants)

Key challenges and pain points

- Lack of explicit synchronization rules:
  - When both co-signer and collateral are needed, their sequence is ad hoc, with no clear gating prior to underwriting beyond the eventual “Submit File to Underwriter.”

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: A governed, efficient loan origination flow that ensures underwriting precedes any signing, constrains rework, and standardizes optional enrichment steps to reduce cycle time and variability.
- Scope: Same start and end as AS-IS, but with tightened control logic and streamlined enrichment.

- Underwriter remains explicitly responsible for the credit decision prior to signing.

Detailed step-by-step process flow

Start conditions and triggers
- Trigger: Prospective borrower initiates the process by “Request Appointment.”

Core sequence and decision points (streamlined and controlled)

4) Hand In Credit Appliaction
5) Verify Borrowers Information

   - Activities (zero, one, or both may be required):

     - No indefinite loops. Any further need triggers a policy-based decision to reject or to proceed with disclosure of limitations per underwriting rules.

7) Submit File to Underwriter (mandatory gate)

- Incomplete application after verification:
  - If critical data cannot be obtained, route to Application Rejected with documented reason.

Rework loops and escalation procedures
- Collateral visits:

- Co-signer follow-up:
  - One formal request cycle. If unsuccessful, stop or proceed per policy (default to rejection if mandatory).

End conditions and outcomes
