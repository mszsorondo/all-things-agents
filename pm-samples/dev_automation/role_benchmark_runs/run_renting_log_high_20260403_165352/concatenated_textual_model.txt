This process model is organized by ROLE. Each section describes the process from the perspective of one participant. Together, all sections describe the complete end-to-end process.

============================================================
ROLE 1: Hotline
============================================================

AS-IS Process Model — Hotline

Section 1: Role Overview (purpose, responsibilities)
The Hotline role is an intake/contact-center role that records incoming applications and sets appointments. Hotline is the main front-line channel for receiving Apply for Viewing Appointment and creating Set Appointment events when tenants (or prospects) call or inquire. The hotline forwards work to Real Estate Agents and occasionally to other internal actors.

Section 2: Activities Performed (complete list with descriptions)
- Apply for Viewing Appointment: record the prospect’s application request into the system (intake).
- Set Appointment: schedule viewing appointments; creates the chain that leads to View The Property.

Section 3: Process Flow (when activated, what happens, decision points)
Activation triggers
- Incoming prospect contact: Hotline is activated when a potential tenant contacts the organization (phone/email), or when other roles route inbound inquiries to the hotline.

Typical sequences and decision points (all observed variants)
- Intake path: Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Set Appointment -> View The Property (forwarded to Real Estate Agent). This is the common observed sequence for Hotline-initiated cases.
- Direct forwarding vs reassign: After setting an appointment, the Hotline passes the case to a Real Estate Agent (often REA2 or REA1). It can also send to REA5 and other agents depending on availability.

Ordering and optional steps
- Apply for Viewing Appointment is typically followed by Set Appointment (chain constraint). There are traces where application is recorded and the case stops (if the applicant is rejected before appointment), but the primary behavior is to set an appointment and then hand over to an agent.

Decision criteria handled by Hotline
- Decide which agent to route the appointment to (based on availability, territory, or internal policies as reflected by handover frequencies), though the exact selection rules are not visible in the log—only the observed handover patterns.

Section 4: Interactions (who hands work to this role, who receives work from this role)
Who hands work TO Hotline
- Real Estate Agents occasionally hand cases back to the Hotline for scheduling or clarification (small but observed weights: REA1 -> Hotline, REA2 -> Hotline, REA5 -> Hotline).

Who Hotline hands work TO
- Real Estate Agents (primary receivers): Hotline -> REA2, -> REA1, -> REA5 (weights show REA2 and REA1 as the most common recipients).
- Hotline also has self-handovers (small) indicating repeated Hotline actions for the same case.

Section 5: Variants and Exceptions (all observed paths involving this role)
- Normal intake: Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Set Appointment -> View The Property (handed to Real Estate Agent).
- Rare exception traces: Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Reject Prospective Tenant (some applications are rejected early and do not proceed to appointment/viewing).
- Hotlines sometimes reassign appointment-setting tasks to different agents (handover network shows repeated small routing flows between Hotline and agents).

Section 6: Key Observations (pain points, inefficiencies observed in the data)
- Hotline is a consistent intake point but passes almost all substantive processing to agents; it rarely appears in post-contract lifecycle events.
- The routing behavior is dispersed across multiple agents; there is no single dominant recipient but REA1/REA2 appear most frequently—this may reflect load-balancing or geographic assignment.
- A small portion of applications are rejected immediately after application, meaning some screening is done at intake or leads are low quality.

Practical notes for someone assuming the Hotline role
- Your primary responsibilities are rapid intake and correct routing to the right agent.
- Because downstream processing (screening, paperwork) is handled by agents and lawyers, ensure that handover notes and appointment details are complete to reduce rework.

(End of Hotline role description)

============================================================
ROLE 2: Landlord
============================================================

AS-IS Process Model — Landlord

Section 1: Role Overview (purpose, responsibilities)
The Landlord role is responsible for the Move In activity recorded in the log. Landlords perform or accept the final operational step of moving a tenant into the property and occasionally interact with tenants after move-in (handover to tenant). Multiple landlord resources (Landlord 1..5) are grouped into this role.

Section 2: Activities Performed (complete list with descriptions)
- Move In: execute or coordinate the tenant move-in (handover of the property). This is the only explicit activity assigned to landlords in the resource–activity mapping.

Section 3: Process Flow (when activated, what happens, decision points)
Activation triggers
- Sign Contract: after agents sign contracts and coordinate the process, Move In is triggered (Sign Contract -> Move In is a high-frequency transition).

Typical sequences and decision points (all observed variants)
- Standard move-in: Sign Contract -> Move In -> Pay Rent (tenant begins rent payment cycle). Move In is followed by the tenant’s Pay Rent events.
- Move In may also be followed by occasional direct Miss Rent Payment events if tenants fail the initial payment.

Decision criteria handled by the role
- Landlord action is largely operational: enabling access and property turnover. The dataset does not show landlords making acceptance/rejection decisions — those are handled upstream by agents and screening.

Section 4: Interactions (who hands work to this role, who receives work from this role)
Who hands work TO Landlord
- Real Estate Agents: agents hand the case to landlords to coordinate the move-in (REA -> Landlord edges observed, e.g., REA1/REA2 -> Landlord 1/4/5 etc.).

Who Landlord hands work TO
- Tenant: Landlord -> Tenant (observed handover) to complete move-in and enable rent payments; landlords may notify tenants about handover details.

Section 5: Variants and Exceptions (all observed paths involving this role)
- Standard: Sign Contract -> Move In -> Pay Rent (tenant payment cycle begins).
- Rare exceptions: Move In -> Miss Rent Payment (immediate missed payment after move-in seen in a small number of traces). Move In can appear in any trace that reaches the sign-contract branch; traces that end in rejection never involve the landlord.

Section 6: Key Observations (pain points, inefficiencies observed in the data)
- Landlords appear only in the move-in stage; their activity is narrow and operational, so they are unlikely to be a major source of control-flow complexity.
- The bulk of delays and rework are upstream (screening, paperwork) and downstream (rent payment cycles). Landlords mainly receive completed cases from agents.

Practical notes for someone assuming this role
- Expect to receive handovers from agents after signature; your role is to operationalize the move-in and hand over the property to the tenant.
- Coordinate with agents to ensure the tenant is properly briefed to avoid immediate missed payments or confusion.

(End of Landlord role description)

ROLE 3: Lawyer
============================================================

AS-IS Process Model — Lawyer

Section 1: Role Overview (purpose, responsibilities)
The Lawyer role performs the Check Paperwork activity observed in the log. Lawyers review and validate the paperwork that applicants hand in and then hand the checked information back to the Real Estate Agent for screening and decision-making. There are multiple lawyer resources (Lawyer 1..3) that behave consistently and are grouped here.

Section 2: Activities Performed (complete list with descriptions)
- Check Paperwork: review and validate applicant documents, perform legal/contractual checks, and record the result (pass/fail). This is the primary visible activity for the Lawyer role in the dataset.

Section 3: Process Flow (when activated, what happens, decision points)
Activation triggers
- Hand In The Paperwork: Lawyers are activated after agents or applicants hand in paperwork. The observed direct succession Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork is frequent.

Typical sequences and decision points (all observed variants)
- Paperwork review path (high-frequency): Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> (then control returns to agent for Screen Prospective Tenant). Check Paperwork generally precedes screening.
- Optionality: Hand In The Paperwork can be skipped in traces where the prospect is rejected immediately after viewing (View The Property -> Reject Prospective Tenant) — in those cases lawyers are not involved.

Decision criteria handled by the role
- Determine whether submitted paperwork satisfies legal/contractual requirements. Although the log does not provide explicit pass/fail labels, the common observed successor to Check Paperwork is Screen Prospective Tenant — indicating that paperwork check completion enables screening. Negative findings would likely cause a stop or return to agent, sometimes leading to rejection.

Section 4: Interactions (who hands work to this role, who receives work from this role)
Who hands work TO Lawyer
- Real Estate Agents: agents hand paperwork to lawyers for checking (observed RA -> Lawyer handovers with weights ~0.01).
- Real Estate Agent 2 appears as a common sender to lawyers in the handover network.

Who Lawyer hands work TO
- Real Estate Agents: lawyers hand back the results of the paperwork check to the agent (Lawyer -> Real Estate Agent edges observed), enabling the agent to proceed with Screen Prospective Tenant or make a rejection decision.

Section 5: Variants and Exceptions (all observed paths involving this role)
- The primary and most frequent path: Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant. This sequence is observed in many traces and is part of the main documented variants.
- Exceptions: When a prospect is rejected at viewing (no paperwork handed in), lawyers are not invoked.

Section 6: Key Observations (pain points, inefficiencies observed in the data)
- Check Paperwork is a high-frequency precondition for screening (Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant is a strong edge). If lawyers are a bottleneck (turnaround time not provided here but likely reflected in case durations), they can delay the screening and decision process.
- Because lawyers are a specialized role, handovers to/from lawyers are required but limited in variety; ensuring timely processing by lawyers would speed the central path.

Practical notes for someone assuming this role
- Expect a steady inflow of paperwork handed in by agents after viewings; your timely checks directly enable the screening and acceptance decisions.
- Coordinate clearly with agents to avoid repeated handovers or unclear paperwork that could cause rework.

(End of Lawyer role description)

ROLE 4: Real Estate Agent
============================================================

AS-IS Process Model — Real Estate Agent

Section 1: Role Overview (purpose, responsibilities)
The Real Estate Agent role performs the bulk of tenant-facing and case-managing work in the renting process. Agents: receive applications (sometimes enter applications), set or accept viewing appointments, conduct/show viewings, collect paperwork, coordinate or perform screening (including initiating "Extensive Screening" when needed), decide on acceptance or rejection of prospective tenants, sign rental contracts, coordinate move‑in, manage rent-collection follow-up (issue warnings, accept late payments, and in some cases initiate eviction), and liaise with lawyers and landlords for document checks and legal/operational actions. Multiple individual resources named "Real Estate Agent 1..5" behave similarly and are grouped here.

Section 2: Activities Performed (complete list with descriptions)
- Apply for Viewing Appointment: (sometimes performed by agents) enter or register applications into the system on behalf of prospects or during intake.
- Set Appointment: schedule and confirm viewing appointments.
- View The Property: perform or host on-site/property viewings with prospects.
- Hand In The Paperwork: collect application documents from prospects; may be performed by the agent who hosted the viewing.
- Screen Prospective Tenant: perform the initial screening (credit, references). Based on screening the agent either rejects, accepts, or escalates to extensive screening.
- Extensive Screening: initiate or conduct extra checks (deeper background checks); invoked for borderline or high-risk prospects.
- Reject Prospective Tenant: decide and record rejection after viewing or screening.
- Sign Contract: complete contract signing with accepted tenants.
- Move In: coordinate (sometimes execute) move-in logistics in cooperation with landlord; agents are involved in handovers to the landlord for actual move-in in many cases.
- Issue Warning: send warnings to tenants after missed rent payments.
- Accept Late Payment: record and accept late rent payments after warnings.
- Evict Tenant: initiate/coordinate eviction proceedings in cases of unresolved non-payment (rare but observed).

Section 3: Process Flow (when activated, what happens, decision points)
Activation triggers
- Intake: Agent involvement starts when an application is received (either entered by the agent or forwarded from Hotline). The agent is also activated by direct handover from Tenant (rare), Hotline, or another agent.
- Viewing & documentation: Agents are triggered after an appointment is set or when paperwork is handed in.
- Post-contract lifecycle: After Sign Contract and Move In, agents are re-activated recurrently for rent-collection issues (Issue Warning, Accept Late Payment, Evict Tenant).

Typical sequences and decision points (all observed variants included)
- Application intake path (most common): Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Set Appointment -> View The Property. After View The Property the agent either:
  - Directly reject the prospect (View The Property -> Reject Prospective Tenant). This path is observed frequently (~17% variant).
  - Collect paperwork (View The Property -> Hand In The Paperwork) then hand paperwork for checking.
- Paperwork & screening path (observed main branch): Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork (performed by Lawyer or occasionally internal) -> Screen Prospective Tenant. After screening the agent decides:
  - Reject Prospective Tenant (frequent ~28% variant after full check & screen).
  - Initiate Extensive Screening (Screen Prospective Tenant -> Extensive Screening) then likely Reject or Sign Contract (observed medium frequency ~18% variant leads to rejection; others lead to contract signing).
  - Sign Contract (if screening passes): Sign Contract -> Move In -> Pay Rent loop begins.
- Rent management loop (post move-in): Move In -> Pay Rent repeated multiple times. Observed behaviors include:
  - Normal payment sequence: Pay Rent repeated (many cases show repeated Pay Rent events).
  - Missed payment handling: Pay Rent -> Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning -> Accept Late Payment -> Pay Rent (this loop repeats; Accept Late Payment sometimes leads back to Miss Rent Payment in later cycles).
  - Escalations: After repeated Miss Rent Payment and Issue Warning, the agent may handle Evict Tenant (rare: observed edges), or the tenant may cancel the apartment (Tenant Cancels Appartment).

Ordering and optionality rules observed (explicitly all valid orderings)
- Set Appointment always directly precedes View The Property in the majority of cases (chain constraint), but there are traces where Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Reject Prospective Tenant (application rejected before any appointment) (optional skip of Set/View).
- After View The Property, Hand In The Paperwork is optional; View can be followed by Reject (skip paperwork) or by Hand In The Paperwork.
- After Screen Prospective Tenant, either Reject, Extensive Screening, or Sign Contract can occur; Extensive Screening is optional.
- After Sign Contract, Move In always follows (chain), then the Pay Rent / Miss Rent Payment loops start; Pay Rent can repeat many times and interleave with Miss Rent Payment / Issue Warning / Accept Late Payment cycles in many combinations.

Decision criteria handled by the role
- Whether to reject a prospect immediately after viewing (no paperwork) vs collect paperwork and proceed to checks.
- Whether standard screening suffices or Extensive Screening is required.
- Whether to sign contract (accept applicant) or reject after screening results.
- How to treat missed rent: issue warning, accept late payment, or escalate to eviction depending on recurrence and response.

Section 4: Interactions (who hands work to this role, who receives work from this role)
Who hands work TO Real Estate Agent (top observed senders from handover network)
- Hotline -> Real Estate Agent (agents 1/2/5): Hotline forwards incoming applications and appointment requests (weights: Hotline → REA2 0.0147, Hotline → REA1 0.0144, Hotline → REA5 0.0076).
- Tenant -> Real Estate Agent (weight small but present): tenants initiate rent payments and cancellations that the agent must react to (Tenant → REA1/2/5 observed).
- Other Real Estate Agents -> Real Estate Agent: frequent internal handovers among agents (agents hand cases among themselves; RA2→RA1, RA1→RA2 etc; weights ~0.046–0.024), indicating reassignments or collaborations.
- Lawyers -> Real Estate Agent: lawyers return checked paperwork or legal advice to agents (Lawyer 1/2/3 -> REA edges ~0.01 weight).

Who Real Estate Agent hands work TO
- Lawyer(s): for Check Paperwork or legal review (REA -> Lawyer 1/2/3 weights ~0.010–0.011), especially after Hand In The Paperwork.
- Other Real Estate Agents: reassignments or collaboration (self and inter-agent handovers observed).
- Landlord(s): coordinate Move In (REA -> Landlord 1/4/5 etc. small weights) and pass operational tasks to landlords.
- Tenant: for communication about rent, warnings, accept late payments, or notifying about eviction/cancellation.
- Hotline: occasionally hand back to Hotline (small weights) for scheduling or call handling follow‑ups.

Section 5: Variants and Exceptions (all observed paths involving this role)
Observed high/medium frequency variants involving agents (percentages from variant output):
- Main happy-ish paths (high frequency): Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Set Appointment -> View The Property -> Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant -> Reject Prospective Tenant (28%).
- Alternative common path: Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Set Appointment -> View The Property -> Hand In The Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> Screen Prospective Tenant -> Extensive Screening -> Reject Prospective Tenant (18%).
- Short rejection before paperwork: Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Set Appointment -> View The Property -> Reject Prospective Tenant (17%).
- Immediate application rejection: Apply for Viewing Appointment -> Reject Prospective Tenant (8%).
- Contract and long post‑move rent loops (low frequency but important): Apply -> Set -> View -> Hand In Paperwork -> Check Paperwork -> Screen -> Sign Contract -> Move In -> Pay Rent repeated many times and intertwined Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning -> Accept Late Payment loops; outcomes include Tenant Cancels Appartment or Evict Tenant (variants with many repeated Pay Rent and Miss Rent Payment cycles; each such long-running variant individually low frequency (~1–2%) but collectively show the rent-recovery loop behavior).
- Rare exception: after repeated non-payment the agent initiates Evict Tenant (observed but rare) or tenant cancels the apartment mid-lifecycle.

Section 6: Key Observations (pain points, inefficiencies observed in the data)
- Significant fragmentation of handovers among multiple agents (1..5) produces frequent internal reassignments which can add delays.
- The screening branch produces many rejections even after full paperwork (28%), indicating potentially inefficient screening criteria or poor lead quality.
- The Check Paperwork step is often performed by lawyers (handover delays to an external role) which may be a bottleneck before screening; Check Paperwork -> Screen is a high-frequency edge suggesting a necessary handoff.
- The rent collection lifecycle shows heavy repetition and long durations (many repeated Pay Rent events and repeated Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning -> Accept Late Payment cycles). This creates very long-case durations for tenants who enter the pay-recovery loop.
- Rare but costly escalations (eviction proceedings) exist and correlate with long-running traces (very high performance/time values), indicating expensive exception handling.

Practical notes for someone assuming this role
- Expect to alternate between front-office intake (appointments & viewings) and long-running back-office follow-up (rent collection and legal coordination).
- You will frequently hand paperwork to lawyers and coordinate with landlords; expect reassignments to/from other agents.
- Be prepared to manage repeated payment cycles and to escalate when warnings are ignored.

(End of Real Estate Agent role description)

ROLE 5: Tenant
============================================================

AS-IS Process Model — Tenant

Section 1: Role Overview (purpose, responsibilities)
The Tenant role represents the person renting the property and performs the tenant-side actions recorded in the log: paying rent, occasionally missing payments, and sometimes cancelling the apartment. Tenants do not appear as the primary actors for application intake in the provided mapping (Hotline/Agents often enter applications), but they are the main actors in the post‑move lifecycle (payments and cancellations).

Section 2: Activities Performed (complete list with descriptions)
- Pay Rent: perform periodic rent payment events. Observed as the most frequent activity in the log and often repeated many times per case.
- Miss Rent Payment: an event recording a missed payment; occurs multiple times in some cases.
- Tenant Cancels Appartment: tenant-initiated cancellation/termination of the rental agreement.

Section 3: Process Flow (when activated, what happens, decision points)
Activation triggers
- Move In: tenant activity typically becomes frequent after Move In (handled by agents/landlords). The first Pay Rent events start after Move In.
- Notifications/Warnings: Miss Rent Payment events are recorded when a scheduled payment is not made; this triggers subsequent interactions handled by agents (Issue Warning, Accept Late Payment).
- Dissolution: Tenant Cancels Appartment can occur any time after Move In and ends the tenant’s active payment lifecycle.

Observed sequences and decision points (complete list of valid orderings)
- Standard payment path: Move In -> Pay Rent (this repeats many times). Pay Rent may occur multiple consecutive times without intervening events.
- Missed payment path: Pay Rent -> Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning (action by Agent) -> Accept Late Payment -> Pay Rent. After Accept Late Payment the tenant may resume paying rent, or later miss payment again (Accept Late Payment -> Miss Rent Payment observed in some traces).
- Repeat cycles: The Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning -> Accept Late Payment -> Pay Rent loop can repeat multiple times. In some traces Miss Rent Payment recurs several times before final resolution (Tenant Cancels Appartment or Evict Tenant occurs).
- Cancellation path: Tenant Cancels Appartment can happen after a sequence of Pay Rent events, or after repeated Miss Rent Payment / warnings; it ends the case.

Decision criteria the tenant controls
- Whether to pay rent on time (Pay Rent) or miss a payment (Miss Rent Payment) — the log records the result but does not show the underlying reason.
- Whether to accept late-payment arrangements (Accept Late Payment is recorded as a role by agents, but tenant behavior determines if and when payment follows).
- Whether to cancel the apartment (Tenant Cancels Appartment) which ends the tenant lifecycle.

Section 4: Interactions (who hands work to this role, who receives work from this role)
Who hands work TO Tenant
- Landlord(s) and Real Estate Agents: agents and landlords send move-in confirmations, warnings, and notices (handover edges from Landlord -> Tenant and Real Estate Agent -> Tenant observed).
- Occasionally landlords or agents trigger tenant actions (e.g., providing move‑in details that enable payments).

Who Tenant hands work TO
- Tenant -> Tenant (self): high self-handover frequency indicates repeated tenant actions over time (Pay Rent repeated sequences and recurring Miss Rent Payment events). This is represented as Tenant -> Tenant in the handover network.
- Tenant -> Real Estate Agent: tenants cause follow-up by agents (e.g., missed payments prompt Issue Warning), though the direct tenant->agent handover weights are smaller compared to self-handover.

Section 5: Variants and Exceptions (all observed paths involving this role)
- Straightforward paying tenant: Move In -> Pay Rent (repeated; case may end by Tenant Cancels Appartment at some point). This is the simplest observed variant.
- Payment trouble loops (observed across many low-frequency long-running variants): Move In -> Pay Rent (x N) -> Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning -> Accept Late Payment -> Pay Rent -> (possibly repeat Miss Rent Payment -> Issue Warning -> Accept Late Payment cycles) -> outcome may be continued payments, Tenant Cancels Appartment, or (rarely) Evict Tenant.
- Tenant Cancels Appartment: observed both as a relatively rare termination option after many payments or after conflicts.

Section 6: Key Observations (pain points, inefficiencies observed in the data)
- Payments dominate the event log and create long-running cases; repeated pay/miss cycles are a major driver of case duration and variance.
- Self-handover is high: tenants generate multiple consecutive events, which indicates that most of the lifecycle after move-in is tenant-driven (payments), but the follow-up (warnings, acceptance of late payments) requires agent involvement.
- Missed payments generate repeated cycles of manual intervention (Issue Warning, Accept Late Payment) — this creates operational load for agents and lengthens case lifetime.
- Eviction is rare but visible; when it occurs it represents a costly exception and appears after repeated missed payments and warnings.

Practical notes for someone assuming this role (tenant perspective)
- After moving in, expect frequent periodic actions (Pay Rent) and that failures to pay will trigger forced interactions with the agent (warnings) and may escalate.
- Tenant events are mostly recorded and reacted to by agents and landlords; tenants will observe delays and repeated cycles if payments are inconsistent.

(End of Tenant role description)
