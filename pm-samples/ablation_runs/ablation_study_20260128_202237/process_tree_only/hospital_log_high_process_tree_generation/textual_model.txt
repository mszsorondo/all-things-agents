"AS-IS Process Model

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: This model describes the discovered patient flow from registration through examination, diagnosis, treatment, and discharge. It was mined from event data and represented as a block-structured process tree. The model captures optional and repeatable behaviors observed in practice, including examination variability and iterative treatment cycles.
- Scope: From initial registration (at FD or ER) to discharge. Roles are not explicitly specified in the source; therefore, this description does not assign responsibilities to specific actors.

Key stakeholders and roles involved
- Roles and systems are not specified in the source representation. The description remains role-agnostic to avoid introducing unobserved actors.

Detailed step-by-step process flow
Start conditions and triggers
- The process instance (case) starts when one of the following mutually exclusive registrations is recorded:
  1) Register at FD
  2) Register at ER

Main flow (sequential backbone)
1) Registration (exclusive choice)
   - Decision: Choose exactly one:
     - Register at FD
     - Register at ER

2) Examination stage (exclusive choice including skip)
   - Decision: Exactly one of the following occurs:
     - Skip examination entirely (silent path)
     - Expert Examination
     - Examination
   - Note: Expert Examination and Examination are mutually exclusive in the discovered model. It is possible that neither is performed (skip).

3) Thorough Examination (optional)
   - Decision: Either:
     - Skip Thorough Examination, or
     - Perform Thorough Examination
   - This is independent of Step 2; Thorough Examination may occur even if the previous examination stage was skipped.

4) Diagnosis and Treatment loop (rework cycle, zero or more repetitions)
   - The model allows zero or more iterations of:
     a) Diagnosis
     b) Treatment
   - Between iterations, an event Treatment unsuccessful occurs to indicate that another iteration will follow.
   - Loop behavior:
     - If Treatment unsuccessful occurs after an iteration, the process returns to Diagnosis for another cycle.
     - If no Treatment unsuccessful event is taken, the loop exits.
     - Because the loop permits zero repetitions, it is possible (though undesirable) to proceed without any Diagnosis or Treatment.

5) Treatment success marker (optional)
   - Decision: Either:
     - Skip the explicit success marker, or
     - Record Treatment successful
   - This marker may be absent even when treatment has in fact succeeded (based on loop exit).

6) Discharge (always)
   - The process always ends with Discharge.

Decision points with criteria and outcomes
- Registration routing (exclusive): Register at FD vs Register at ER; criteria not specified in the source.
- Examination selection (exclusive with skip):
  - Skip entirely; or
  - Expert Examination; or
  - Examination.
- Thorough Examination (optional): may be executed or skipped regardless of prior examination.
- Treatment loop decision:
  - After each Diagnosis + Treatment iteration, either take Treatment unsuccessful (repeat) or do not take it (exit loop).
- Success marker (optional): Treatment successful may be recorded or omitted before Discharge.

Parallel activities and synchronization points
- None. The discovered model is purely sequential with exclusive decisions and a loop; no parallel branches were discovered.

Exception handling and error paths (as observed)
- Examination can be entirely skipped.
- Thorough Examination can occur even if prior examination was skipped, yielding inconsistent sequencing.
- The Diagnosis/Treatment loop permits zero iterations; therefore, discharge without any clinical work is possible.
- Success may not be explicitly recorded (Treatment successful is optional), creating ambiguity.
- Potentially many rework cycles can occur if Treatment unsuccessful repeats.

Rework loops and escalation procedures
- Rework loop: Each time Treatment unsuccessful occurs, the process returns to Diagnosis and then Treatment. There is no explicit cap on the number of cycles.
- No explicit escalation or consultation steps are modeled (not present in the source).

End conditions and outcomes
- The process ends with Discharge in all cases.
- Outcomes at discharge may be ambiguous:
  - Treatment successful may or may not be recorded.
  - It is theoretically possible to discharge without any Diagnosis/Treatment due to the zero-iteration loop.

Process metrics and performance characteristics
- Throughput time: Registration (FD/ER) to Discharge.
- Registration mix: Percentage of cases registering at FD vs ER.
- Examination coverage:
  - Rate of skipped examination.
  - Split between Expert Examination vs Examination.

  - Percentage of cases with at least one Diagnosis/Treatment iteration.

  - Distribution of iterations until loop exit.
- Outcome documentation:
  - Presence rate of Treatment successful.
  - Share of cases exiting loop without a success marker.
- Rework intensity:
  - Rate of Treatment unsuccessful occurrences per case.
- Data quality:
  - Frequency of paths where examination is skipped or success is not recorded.

Key challenges and pain points
- Clinical completeness risk:
  - The model permits skipping all examinations and even skipping all Diagnosis/Treatment iterations before Discharge.
- Documentation gaps:
  - Treatment successful is optional; the absence of this marker reduces outcome traceability.
- Inconsistent sequencing:
  - Thorough Examination can occur independently of prior examination, allowing thorough checks without any baseline exam.
- Mutual exclusivity constraint:
  - Expert Examination and Examination are mutually exclusive; cases that might benefit from both are not represented.
- Prolonged rework without guardrails:

- Governance and auditability:

Process overview and purpose
- Purpose: This improved model standardizes clinical assessment before treatment, enforces outcome-driven loop control, and clarifies exit criteria, while preserving the original activity set (no new activities introduced).

  - Register at FD
  - Register at ER

   - Decision: Register at FD or Register at ER (exactly one).

3) Conditional thorough assessment (optional, but sequenced)

   - Constraint: Thorough Examination can only occur after completion of the mandatory baseline examination.

4) First clinical cycle (mandatory)
   - Diagnosis
   - Treatment
   - Rationale: Eliminates the zero-iteration possibility; every case receives at least one Diagnosis + Treatment cycle.

5) Outcome evaluation and loop control (explicit and documented)
   - Decision:
     - If Treatment unsuccessful: record Treatment unsuccessful and return to Diagnosis (Step 4) for another cycle.
     - If treatment is effective: record Treatment successful and proceed.
   - Constraint:
     - Treatment successful is mandatory before exiting the loop to Discharge (no silent exit).
